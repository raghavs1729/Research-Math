Two elementary lemmas used repeatedly.

**Lemma 1.1.** A trigonometric polynomial `P = Σ_{g∈S} c_g e_g` on `A` (`S ⊂ G` finite, the
`c_g` not all zero) vanishes only on a `λ_A`-null set.

*Proof.* The subgroup `G_S ⊂ G` generated by `S` is finitely generated and torsion-free,
hence `≅ ℤ^n`; let `g_1,…,g_n` be a basis and `π = (e_{g_1},…,e_{g_n}) : A → 𝕋^n`. `π` is a
continuous homomorphism whose image is compact and connected, and no nontrivial character
of `𝕋^n` vanishes on it (the `e_{g_i}` are multiplicatively independent), so `π` is onto and
`π_*λ_A` is Haar measure. `P = Q∘π` with `Q` a nonzero trigonometric polynomial on `𝕋^n`; the
zero set of a nonzero real-analytic function on `𝕋^n` is Lebesgue-null (apply to `|Q|²`). ∎

**Lemma 1.2.** If `h` is real-analytic and non-constant on an open interval `J`, the image of
Lebesgue measure on `J` under `h` is absolutely continuous.

*Proof.* The zeros of `h'` are isolated; on each of the countably many open intervals between
them `h` is a diffeomorphism onto its image. ∎

## 2. Theorem A: the atom at `0` is the jump of the bias in the weight

**Standing hypotheses and definitions for §2.** `χ` is a real primitive character mod `q`;
GRH(`χ`): all nontrivial zeros of `L(s,χ)` have real part `1/2` (no hypothesis on any other
`L`-function, none on `ζ`); NCZ(`χ`): `L(1/2,χ) ≠ 0`. NCZ is a theorem for `q = 3, 4` (and for
all real primitive characters of conductor `≤ 10^9`, `GRH-ONLY.md` §0), so for `χ_{−4}` and
`χ_{−3}` the only hypothesis is GRH(`χ`). NCZ is used through (I1) (the constant term of
`F_M` is `1`) and through the form of `λ_χ`; with a central zero of order `n_0` the same proofs
go through with `1 + 2n_0` in place of `1` and the general `λ_χ` of `GRH-ONLY.md` Theorem 7.1
(not written out). For a measurable `B ⊂ [2,∞)`,

    \underline d(B) := liminf_{X→∞} (1/log X) ∫_{[2,X]∩B} dt/t,     \bar d(B) := limsup_{X→∞} (1/log X) ∫_{[2,X]∩B} dt/t

(lower and upper logarithmic densities); `B` *has a logarithmic density* if they agree. The
weights: `m > −1/2`, `M = m + 1/2`; the results of this section hold for `M` in an interval
`I` on which `λ_χ(M,M') > 0` for all `M < M'`, and `I = (0, M_σ(χ)]` is such an interval
(I4), i.e. `−1/2 < m ≤ M_σ(χ) − 1/2` (`≤ 1.3239` for `q = 4`, `≤ 1.6277` for `q = 3`); the
classical race `m = 0` is an interior point. Beyond `M_σ(χ)` the criterion `σ_Mσ_{M'} < 2` is
no longer available and nothing is claimed. The inclusion behind everything is the
lead-inheritance theorem of this repository (`monotone-coupling.tex`, Theorem A(ii);
`GRH-ONLY.md` Corollary 2.4 and Theorem 7.1(a)): for `m < m'` with `λ_χ(M,M') > 0`,
`D_{m'}(x) ≥ 0 ⇒ D_m(x) > 0` for `x ≥ x_0`. Below it is used in its hull form (2.2), which is
what gives statements about `ν_M` rather than about tie sets.

For `0 < M < M'` put `κ = M' − M` and

    G_{M,M'}(θ) := 1 + Re Σ_{γ∈Γ} m_γ · 4MM' θ_γ / ((M+iγ)(M'+iγ)),

a continuous function on `A` (the series converges absolutely and uniformly), with

    M M' λ_χ(M,M') ≤ G_{M,M'}(θ) ≤ 2 − M M' λ_χ(M,M')     for every θ ∈ A              (2.1)

(triangle inequality and (I4)). Comparing Fourier coefficients
(`4/(M+iγ) − 4/(M'+iγ) = 4κ/((M+iγ)(M'+iγ))`, constant terms `1/M − 1/M' = κ/(MM')`):

    F_M/M − F_{M'}/M' = (κ/(MM')) · G_{M,M'}     λ_A-a.e.                                (2.2)

(this is the hull form of `GRH-ONLY.md` Corollary 2.4). Let `I ⊂ (0,∞)` be an interval with
`λ_χ(M,M') > 0` for all `M < M'` in `I`; by (I4) one may take `I = (0, M_σ(χ)]`, which
contains `M = 1/2` in its interior. Define

    d(M) := ν_M((0,∞)) = λ_A(F_M > 0),      \bar d(M) := ν_M([0,∞)) = λ_A(F_M ≥ 0).

By (I1), (I3), for every `m > −1/2` (ties counted either way, `M = m + 1/2`):

    d(M) ≤ \underline d{D_m > 0} ≤ \bar d{D_m > 0} ≤ \bar d{D_m ≥ 0} ≤ \bar d(M),
    d(M) ≤ \underline d{D_m > 0} ≤ \underline d{D_m ≥ 0} ≤ \bar d{D_m ≥ 0} ≤ \bar d(M).       (2.3)

**Theorem 2.1 (GRH + NCZ, no LI).** Let `I` be as above.

(a) For `M < M'` in `I`: `\bar d(M') ≤ d(M)`. Hence `d` and `\bar d` are nonincreasing on `I`.

(b) For every interior point `M_0` of `I`:
`d(M_0) = d(M_0^+) = \bar d(M_0^+)`, `\bar d(M_0) = \bar d(M_0^-) = d(M_0^-)`, and

    ν_{M_0}({0}) = d(M_0^−) − d(M_0^+) = δ_*(m_0^−) − δ_*(m_0^+),

where `δ_*` is any of the four functions `m ↦ \underline d{D_m > 0}, \bar d{D_m > 0},
\underline d{D_m ≥ 0}, \bar d{D_m ≥ 0}` (all four are nonincreasing on `I − 1/2` and have the
same one-sided limits at every interior point).

(c) The set `𝔼 := {M ∈ I : ν_M({0}) > 0}` is countable and `Σ_{M∈𝔼} ν_M({0}) ≤ 1`; more
precisely `Σ_{M∈𝔼∩[M_1,M_2]} ν_M({0}) ≤ \bar d(M_1) − d(M_2)` for `M_1 < M_2` in `I`.

(d) For every `M = m + 1/2 ∈ I ∖ 𝔼`: the logarithmic density of `{x : D_m(x) > 0}` exists and
equals `d(M)`; the same for `{D_m ≥ 0}` and for the `θ`-form
`{Σ_{p≤x} χ*(p)p^m log p > 0}`; and for every function `ε(x) → 0` the set of near-ties
`{x : |D_m(x)| ≤ ε(x) x^M/log x}` has logarithmic density `0` (in particular
`{|D_m(x)| ≤ K}` for each fixed `K`).

*Proof.* (a) By (2.2) and (2.1), `F_M/M ≥ F_{M'}/M' + κ λ_χ(M,M')` a.e., so
`{F_{M'} ≥ 0} ⊂ {F_M > 0}` up to a null set. Monotonicity: `d(M') ≤ \bar d(M') ≤ d(M) ≤ \bar d(M)`.

(b) `M ↦ F_M ∈ L²(A)` is continuous (dominated convergence in the coefficients), so
`M ↦ ν_M` is weakly continuous, and by (I3) `d` is lower semicontinuous and `\bar d` upper
semicontinuous. For `M ↓ M_0`: `d(M) ≤ \bar d(M) ≤ d(M_0)` by (a), and
`liminf d(M) ≥ d(M_0)`; so `d(M_0^+) = \bar d(M_0^+) = d(M_0)`. For `M ↑ M_0`:
`\bar d(M) ≥ d(M) ≥ \bar d(M_0)` and `limsup \bar d(M) ≤ \bar d(M_0)`; so
`\bar d(M_0^−) = d(M_0^−) = \bar d(M_0)`. Subtract: `ν_{M_0}({0}) = \bar d(M_0) − d(M_0)`. By
(2.3) each `δ_*` lies between `d` and `\bar d` at every weight, hence has the same one-sided
limits; each `δ_*` is nonincreasing by (a) and (2.3) (`δ_*(m') ≤ \bar d(M') ≤ d(M) ≤ δ_*(m)`).

(c) By (a), for `M_1 ≤ M ≤ M_2` in `I` the sets `S_M := {F_M = 0}` are pairwise disjoint mod
null sets and each is contained, mod null sets, in `{F_{M_1} ≥ 0} ∖ {F_{M_2} > 0}`, a set of
measure `\bar d(M_1) − d(M_2)` (since `{F_{M_2} > 0} ⊂ {F_{M_1} ≥ 0}`). A family of pairwise
disjoint sets in a probability space has at most countably many members of positive
measure, and their measures sum to at most the measure of a set containing them.

(d) If `ν_M({0}) = 0` then `d(M) = \bar d(M)` and (2.3) gives the densities. The `θ`-form has
the same limiting distribution (I1). Near-ties: `|D_m(x)| ≤ ε(x)x^M/log x` iff
`|E_M(x)| ≤ 2Mε(x)`; for every fixed `ε_0 > 0` this set is eventually contained in
`{|E_M| ≤ ε_0}`, whose upper density is `≤ ν_M([−ε_0,ε_0])` by (1.1), and
`ν_M([−ε_0,ε_0]) → ν_M({0}) = 0`. ∎

**Corollary 2.2 (the classical race).** Assume GRH for `L(s,χ_{−4})`. Then
`ν_{1/2}({0}) = δ(0^−) − δ(0^+)`, where `δ(m)` is the lower (or upper) logarithmic density of
`{x : Σ_{p≤x} χ*(p)p^m > 0}`; in particular

    \bar d{π(x;4,3) > π(x;4,1)} − \underline d{π(x;4,3) > π(x;4,1)} ≤ δ(0^−) − δ(0^+),

and if `m ↦ δ(m)` is continuous at `m = 0` then the logarithmic density of
`{π(x;4,3) > π(x;4,1)}` exists and near-ties have density `0`. The same for `χ_{−3}`.
For all but countably many `m ∈ (−1/2, 1.3239]` (`χ_{−4}`), resp. `(−1/2, 1.6277]` (`χ_{−3}`),
the logarithmic density of `{x : Σ_{p≤x} χ*(p)p^m > 0}` exists under GRH alone.

(The converse "density exists ⇒ no atom" is not claimed: an atom at `0` could in principle be
carried by times at which `E` is small and positive. The total mass of the atoms over
`I = (0, M_σ]` is `< 1`: by Theorem 2.1(c) with `M_2 = M_σ` it is `≤ 1 − d(M_σ)`, and
`d(M) = ν_M((0,∞)) > 0` because `ν_M` has mean `1`.)

**Remark on prior art.** That a race against a *level* has a density for all but countably
many levels is classical (a probability measure has countably many atoms); Devin
(arXiv:1706.06394) attributes a statement of this type under RH only to Kaczorowski–Ramaré
(Acta Arith. 2003, Thm 3; not read by us). What is specific to Theorem 2.1 is that the
level is fixed (`0`, the actual race) and the parameter is the weight; this uses the
transversality (2.1)–(2.2) and extends `GRH-ONLY.md` Theorem 7.1.

**Remark 2.2a (what Theorem 2.1 does not give).** (i) No explicit weight: the proof shows
that the exceptional set `𝔼` is countable, not that any given `m` (in particular `m = 0`) is
outside it; its complement is co-countable (so of full measure and comeagre), but we cannot
name one of its elements. (ii) No structural reason for continuity of `δ` at a particular
weight was found: the family is real-analytic in `M` (`M ↦ F_M ∈ L²(A)` is), but analyticity of
the family does not transfer to the distribution function without information on the law.
(iii) The same countability holds along every one-parameter family `F + sH` with `H`
continuous and positive on `A` (e.g. levels: `H ≡ 1`; or `H = G_{M,M'}`, the Cesàro-type mean
of §2), and for the same reason it never decides a prescribed parameter. (iv) For smoothed
races the situation is: analytic smoothing — no exceptional parameter at all (Theorem 4.1);
finite-order smoothing — not decided; for the resolvent `K_κ` (and, by iteration, for
products of resolvents, e.g. Riesz-type means of integer order) Proposition 4.4 shows that
the smoothing neither creates atoms nor removes the difficulty.

**Theorem 2.3 (two-sided quantitative form; GRH + NCZ).** Let `M_1 < M_0 < M_2` in `I` and put

    a_1 = (M_0 − M_1) M_0 λ_χ(M_1,M_0),      b_1 = (M_0 − M_1)(2/M_1 − M_0 λ_χ(M_1,M_0)),
    a_2 = (M_2 − M_0) M_0 λ_χ(M_0,M_2),      b_2 = (M_2 − M_0)(2/M_2 − M_0 λ_χ(M_0,M_2)).

Then

    ν_{M_0}((−a_1, a_2)) ≤ d(M_1) − \bar d(M_2) ≤ \bar d(M_1) − d(M_2) ≤ ν_{M_0}([−b_1, b_2]).

So the concentration function of `ν_{M_0}` at `0` and the modulus of continuity of the bias
`M ↦ d(M)` at `M_0` control each other, with explicit constants: `ν_{M_0}((−t,t)) = O(t)`
as `t → 0` if and only if `d` is Lipschitz at `M_0`; `ν_{M_0}((−t,t)) → 0` iff `d` is
continuous at `M_0`. Since a monotone function is differentiable Lebesgue-a.e., for
Lebesgue-almost every weight `m ∈ I − 1/2` one has
`limsup_{t→0} ν_M((−t,t))/t < ∞` ("near-ties at scale `t x^M/log x` have density `O(t)`").

*Proof.* By (2.2), (2.1): `F_{M_1}/M_1 − F_{M_0}/M_0 ∈ [(M_0−M_1)λ_χ(M_1,M_0), (M_0−M_1)(2/(M_1M_0) − λ_χ(M_1,M_0))]`
a.e., and likewise for `(M_0,M_2)`. Hence, a.e.: if `F_{M_0} > −a_1` then `F_{M_1} > 0`; if
`F_{M_0} < a_2` then `F_{M_2} < 0`; so `{−a_1 < F_{M_0} < a_2} ⊂ {F_{M_1} > 0} ∖ {F_{M_2} ≥ 0}`,
and the latter has measure `d(M_1) − \bar d(M_2)` because `{F_{M_2} ≥ 0} ⊂ {F_{M_1} > 0}`.
Conversely if `F_{M_1} ≥ 0` then `F_{M_0} ≥ −b_1`, and if `F_{M_2} ≤ 0` then `F_{M_0} ≤ b_2`; so
`{F_{M_1} ≥ 0} ∖ {F_{M_2} > 0} ⊂ {−b_1 ≤ F_{M_0} ≤ b_2}`. The remaining statements follow by
taking `M_{1,2} = M_0 ∓ t` and the continuity of `λ_χ` (with `λ_χ(M_0,M_0) > 0`). ∎

**Proposition 2.4 (the critical weight).** There is a version of the family such that for
every `θ` outside a null set the map `M ↦ Φ_θ(M) := F_M(θ)/M` is continuous and strictly
decreasing on `I`, with
`(M'−M)λ_χ(M,M') ≤ Φ_θ(M) − Φ_θ(M') ≤ (M'−M)(2/(MM') − λ_χ(M,M'))`. Let `M*(θ) ∈ I` be its
zero when it has one in `I` (`M*(θ) := inf I` if `Φ_θ < 0` on `I`, `:= sup I` if `Φ_θ > 0` on
`I`). Then for interior `M`: `d(M) = λ_A(M* > M)`, `\bar d(M) = λ_A(M* ≥ M)`,
`ν_M({0}) = λ_A(M* = M)`: **the exceptional weights are exactly the atoms of the law of the
critical weight** `M*`.

*Proof.* Fix `M_1 ∈ I` and a Borel version of `F_{M_1}`; define
`Φ_θ(M) := F_{M_1}(θ)/M_1 + ((M_1−M)/(MM_1)) G_{M,M_1}(θ)` (with `G_{M,M'} := G_{M',M}` and
`G_{M,M} := 1 + Re Σ m_γ 4M²θ_γ/(M+iγ)²`). This is continuous in `M` for every `θ`, equals
`F_M/M` a.e. for each `M` by (2.2), and the cocycle identity
`(M_1−M)G_{M,M_1}/(MM_1) + (M_2−M_1)G_{M_1,M_2}/(M_1M_2) = (M_2−M)G_{M,M_2}/(MM_2)` (an identity
between absolutely convergent series, checked on coefficients) gives the two-sided bound
for every `θ` from (2.1). ∎

**Remark 2.5 (reading on the prime side; not used).** For finite `x`,
`E_M(x)/M ∝ x^{−M} P_x(m)` with `P_x(m) = Σ_{p≤x} χ*(p)p^m`, a Dirichlet polynomial in `−m`
supported on primes. The hull variable `M*` is the limit object of `m*(x) + 1/2`, `m*(x)` the
real zero of `P_x` in the range `I − 1/2`; `m*(x)` is a step function of `x` which moves by
`≍ x^{−1/2}` at each prime, up for `p ≡ 3`, down for `p ≡ 1 (mod 4)`. In these terms: a tie
is `m*(x) = 0`; `ν_{1/2}` has no atom at `0` (which implies that the density of Chebyshev's
bias exists) iff the limiting distribution of the critical exponent `m*(x)` has no atom at
`0`; and Theorem 2.1 says that
this distribution has at most countably many atoms. (That `m*(x)` has the law of `M* − 1/2`
as limiting distribution follows from Theorem 2.1 at continuity points; not written out.)

## 3. Theorem B: weak substitutes for LI

Notation: `Γ_T := Γ ∩ (0,T]`, `Γ_{>T} := Γ ∖ Γ_T`; `⟨X⟩_ℚ` the `ℚ`-span of `X ⊂ ℝ`; `G_T` the
subgroup of `ℝ` generated by `Γ_T`.

**Hypothesis TS ("the tail does not span").** There is `T > 0` with `⟨Γ_{>T}⟩_ℚ ≠ ⟨Γ⟩_ℚ`;
i.e. some ordinate is not a rational linear combination of ordinates exceeding `T`.

Equivalently: there is a `ℚ`-linear form `φ : V → ℚ`, `φ ≠ 0`, with `φ(γ) = 0` for all but
finitely many `γ ∈ Γ`. (If `φ` vanishes on `Γ_{>T}` it vanishes on its span; conversely a
proper subspace is contained in the kernel of a nonzero form.)

**Hypothesis FD ("finite-level directions").** There are `N_0 ≥ 1` and `ε > 0` such that for
arbitrarily large `T` there exist `Λ_T ⊂ Γ_T` with `#Λ_T ≤ N_0` and `γ_T ∈ Λ_T` with
`γ_T ≤ T^{1/2−ε}` and `γ_T ∉ ⟨Γ_T ∖ Λ_T⟩_ℚ`.

Equivalently: for arbitrarily large `T` there is a homomorphism `φ_T : G_T → ℤ` which is
nonzero on at most `N_0` elements of `Γ_T`, one of them `≤ T^{1/2−ε}`. (Given `γ_T, Λ_T`, take
a `ℚ`-linear form on `⟨Γ_T⟩_ℚ` vanishing on `⟨Γ_T∖Λ_T⟩_ℚ` and not at `γ_T`, and multiply by a
common denominator of its values on the finite set `Λ_T`; conversely put
`Λ_T := {γ ∈ Γ_T : φ_T(γ) ≠ 0}`.) TS implies FD (take the fixed finite set
`Λ = {γ : φ(γ) ≠ 0}`, `N_0 = #Λ`, any `ε < 1/2`, all large `T`).

### 3.1 TS implies absolute continuity

**Theorem 3.1 (GRH + NCZ + TS).** For every `M > 0` the measure `ν_M` is absolutely
continuous with respect to Lebesgue measure. Consequently, for every `m > −1/2` the
logarithmic densities of `{D_m > 0}`, `{D_m ≥ 0}` and of `{x : D_m(x) > c·x^M/log x}`
(every real `c`) exist, and near-ties (`|D_m(x)| ≤ ε(x)x^M/log x`, `ε(x) → 0`) have
logarithmic density `0`. In particular, for `χ_{−4}`: under GRH + TS the logarithmic density
of `{x : π(x;4,3) > π(x;4,1)}` exists.

*Proof.* Let `φ : V → ℚ` be as above, `Λ := {γ ∈ Γ : φ(γ) ≠ 0}` (finite, nonempty). After
multiplying `φ` by an integer, `φ(Λ) ⊂ ℤ`, hence `φ(G) ⊂ ℤ`. Define
`j(s) := (e^{iφ(γ)s})_{γ∈Γ} ∈ 𝕋^Γ`, `s ∈ ℝ`. If `Σ n_γ γ = 0` (finite integer relation) then
`Π e^{iφ(γ)s n_γ} = e^{isφ(Σ n_γ γ)} = 1`; so `j(s) ∈ A` by (I2), and `j : ℝ → A` is a
continuous homomorphism, `2π`-periodic, with `e_g(j(s)) = e^{iφ(g)s}` for `g ∈ G`.

Write `F_M = F' + P`, `P := Re Σ_{γ∈Λ} m_γ r_γ e_γ` (a trigonometric polynomial),
`F' := F_M − P ∈ L²(A)`. The Fourier coefficients of `F'` are supported on
`{0} ∪ {±γ : γ ∈ Γ∖Λ} ⊂ ker φ`, so for every `s`, `F'(θ j(s)) = F'(θ)` for a.e. `θ` (compare
coefficients). Fix Borel versions; by Fubini there is a null set `N_1 ⊂ A` such that for
`θ ∉ N_1`: `F'(θ j(s)) = F'(θ)` for a.e. `s ∈ [0,2π]`. For every `θ`,

    p_θ(s) := P(θ j(s)) = Σ_{k≠0} C_k(θ) e^{iks},
    C_k(θ) := ½ Σ_{γ∈Λ, φ(γ)=k} m_γ r_γ θ_γ + ½ Σ_{γ∈Λ, φ(γ)=−k} m_γ \bar r_γ \bar θ_γ .

Choose `k_0 ≠ 0` with `{γ ∈ Λ : φ(γ) = ±k_0} ≠ ∅`. `C_{k_0}` is a trigonometric polynomial on
`A` in the pairwise distinct characters `e_γ` (`γ ∈ Λ`, `φ(γ) = k_0`) and `e_{−γ}` (`γ ∈ Λ`,
`φ(γ) = −k_0`), with nonzero coefficients; by Lemma 1.1 its zero set `N_2` is null. For
`θ ∉ N_2`, `p_θ` is a non-constant real-analytic function, so by Lemma 1.2 the image of
Lebesgue measure on `[0,2π]` under `s ↦ F'(θ) + p_θ(s)` is absolutely continuous.

Let `B ⊂ ℝ` be a Lebesgue-null Borel set. By invariance of `λ_A` under translation by `j(s)`
and Fubini,

    ν_M(B) = (1/2π) ∫_0^{2π} λ_A{θ : F_M(θ j(s)) ∈ B} ds = ∫_A (1/2π) Leb{s ∈ [0,2π] : F_M(θ j(s)) ∈ B} dλ_A(θ),

and for `θ ∉ N_1 ∪ N_2` the inner set equals, up to a Lebesgue-null set,
`{s : F'(θ) + p_θ(s) ∈ B}`, which is null. So `ν_M(B) = 0`. The consequences follow from
(1.1) and (2.3) exactly as in Theorem 2.1(d) (levels `c` instead of `0`). ∎

**Corollary 3.2 (what an atom forces).** Assume GRH + NCZ. If for some `M > 0` the measure
`ν_M` is not absolutely continuous (in particular if it has an atom), then for every `T`
every ordinate of `L(s,χ)` is a rational linear combination of ordinates exceeding `T`.

**Example 3.3 (TS is strictly weaker than the direct-sum hypothesis).** Let
`b_0, b_1, b_2, … > 0` be `ℚ`-linearly independent reals with `b_i → ∞` and let
`X := {b_0, b_0+b_1} ∪ {b_i+b_j : 1 ≤ i < j}` (an abstract set of frequencies; no claim that
ordinates of an `L`-function look like this). `X` satisfies TS: the form `φ = b_0^*` (dual
to `b_0`) is nonzero exactly on `b_0, b_0+b_1`. But `X` has no finite nonempty subset `Λ'`
with `⟨Λ'⟩_ℚ ∩ ⟨X∖Λ'⟩_ℚ = {0}`: for a finite `Λ'`, every `b_i` (`i ≥ 1`) lies in `⟨X∖Λ'⟩_ℚ`
(`2b_i = (b_i+b_k)+(b_i+b_l)−(b_k+b_l)` for large `k < l`); so any `b_i+b_j ∈ Λ'` lies in the
intersection, and if `Λ' ⊂ {b_0, b_0+b_1}` then one of `b_0 = (b_0+b_1) − b_1`,
`b_0+b_1 = b_0 + b_1`, `b_1 = (b_0+b_1) − b_0` is a nonzero element of the intersection. In
particular `X` has no self-sufficient element, and for all large `T` no element of
`X ∩ (0,T^{1/2}]` is outside the span of the other elements of `X ∩ (0,T]`.

**Example 3.3a (finite-level self-sufficiency does not imply TS; supplied by the first
referee of this note).** Let `b_1, b_2, … > 0` be `ℚ`-independent with `b_n → ∞`, the index set
being split into two disjoint infinite sets of *primary* and *linking* indices, the primary
`b_p` placed so that every window `(log T, T^{1/2−ε}]`, `T ≥ T_0`, contains one (possible for
every fixed `0 < ε < 1/2`). For each `n` choose infinitely many linking indices
`N(n,1) < N(n,2) < …` with `b_{N(n,1)} ≥ e^{b_n}`, and let
`X := {b_n : n ≥ 1} ∪ {b_n + b_{N(n,j)} : n, j ≥ 1}`. TS fails:
`b_n = (b_n + b_{N(n,j)}) − b_{N(n,j)}` with both terms above any given height. But for a
primary `b_p ∈ (log T, T^{1/2−ε}]` the only elements of `X` involving `b_p` are `b_p` itself and
the sums `b_p + b_{N(p,j)} ≥ e^{b_p} > T` (a primary index is never a linking index), so by
`ℚ`-independence `b_p` is not in the span of the other elements of `X ∩ (0,T]`: `X` satisfies
the hypothesis of Devin's Theorem 5.4(1).

### 3.2 FD implies continuity

Quoted input:

- **(I6) Turán–Nazarov inequality** (F. L. Nazarov, *Local estimates for exponential
  polynomials and their applications to inequalities of the uncertainty principle type*,
  Algebra i Analiz 5 (1993), no. 4, 3–66; Engl. transl. St. Petersburg Math. J. 5 (1994),
  663–717; main theorem of §1). There is an absolute constant `A_N ≥ 1` such that for every
  exponential polynomial `p(t) = Σ_{k=1}^n c_k e^{iλ_k t}` (`c_k ∈ ℂ`, `λ_k ∈ ℝ`), every
  interval `I ⊂ ℝ` and every measurable `E ⊂ I` of positive measure,
  `sup_I |p| ≤ (A_N |I|/|E|)^{n−1} sup_E |p|`. (Statement recalled; the numbering in the
  source was not re-read.)

**Theorem 3.4 (GRH + NCZ + FD).** For every `M > 0` the measure `ν_M` is continuous (has no
atoms); hence the conclusions of Theorem 3.1 about densities and near-ties hold. If FD holds
for all `T ≥ T_0`, then moreover `sup_c ν_M([c−ℓ, c+ℓ]) ≪ ℓ^β` as `ℓ → 0`, with
`β = β(N_0, ε) > 0`.

*Proof.* Fix `M`, and `T` as in FD with `φ_T`, `Λ_T`, `γ_T`; write `φ = φ_T`, `Λ = Λ_T`,
`n := #Λ ≤ N_0`. Let `A_T` be the closure of `{(e^{iγy})_{γ∈Γ_T}}` in `𝕋^{Γ_T}`, `π_T : A → A_T`
the restriction (onto; it maps `λ_A` to `λ_{A_T}`), and
`F^{(T)} := 1 + Re Σ_{γ∈Γ_T} m_γ r_γ e_γ` on `A_T`. Then

    ‖F_M − F^{(T)}∘π_T‖_2² = V^{>T} := ½ Σ_{γ∈Γ_{>T}} m_γ² |r_γ|² ≤ C_1 M² log²(T+3)/T,                  (3.1)

with `C_1` depending on `χ` only: by (Q3) of `GRH-ONLY.md` §1.1 (`N(t+1)−N(t) ≤ A_0 log(t+3)`),
`m_γ ≤ A_0 log(γ+3)` and `Σ_{γ>T} γ^{−2} log(γ+3) ≪ log²(T+3)/T` (sum with multiplicity).

*Step 1 (a direction).* As in the proof of Theorem 3.1 (with `G_T`, `A_T` in place of
`G`, `A`), `j(s) := (e^{iφ(γ)s})_{γ∈Γ_T}` is a `2π`-periodic one-parameter subgroup of `A_T`,
and for all `θ ∈ A_T`, `s ∈ ℝ` (everything is a finite sum now):

    F^{(T)}(θ j(s)) = F'(θ) + Σ_{k∈K} C_k(θ) e^{iks},   F' := 1 + Re Σ_{γ∈Γ_T∖Λ} m_γ r_γ e_γ,

`K := {±φ(γ) : γ ∈ Λ}` (`#K ≤ 2n`, `0 ∉ K`), `C_k` as in the proof of Theorem 3.1. Put
`k_* := φ(γ_T)` and `a := ½ m_{γ_T} |r_{γ_T}| ≥ 2M/(M + T^{1/2−ε})`.

*Step 2 (sublevel sets along the direction).* Fix `c ∈ ℝ`, `ℓ > 0`, `θ ∈ A_T` and let
`q(s) := F'(θ) − c + Σ_{k∈K} C_k(θ)e^{iks}`, an exponential polynomial with at most `2n+1`
terms, and `E_θ := {s ∈ [0,2π] : |q(s)| ≤ ℓ}`. Since `C_{k_*}(θ) = (1/2π)∫_0^{2π} q(s)e^{−ik_*s}ds`,
`sup_{[0,2π]}|q| ≥ |C_{k_*}(θ)|`. By (I6) (if `|E_θ| > 0`):
`|C_{k_*}(θ)| ≤ (2πA_N/|E_θ|)^{2n} ℓ`, i.e.

    |E_θ|/2π ≤ A_N (ℓ/|C_{k_*}(θ)|)^{1/(2n)} ≤ A_N (ℓ/|C_{k_*}(θ)|)^{1/(2N_0)}   when ℓ ≤ |C_{k_*}(θ)|.     (3.2)

*Step 3 (small values of the coefficient).* `C_{k_*} = Σ_{g∈S} c_g e_g` with `S ⊂ ±Λ`,
`#S ≤ n`, pairwise distinct characters, and `|c_{γ_T}| = a`. Claim: for `0 < η < 1`,

    λ_{A_T}{θ : |C_{k_*}(θ)| ≤ η a} ≤ A_N η^{1/N_0}.                                           (3.3)

If `#S = 1` the set is empty. Otherwise `G_T` is free of finite rank and `S ⊂ G_T` is
finite, so there is a homomorphism `ψ : G_T → ℤ` injective on `S` (a generic integer linear
form in a basis). `u ↦ (e^{iψ(γ)u})_{γ∈Γ_T}` is again a one-parameter subgroup `j_ψ` of `A_T`,
and `u ↦ C_{k_*}(θ j_ψ(u)) = Σ_{g∈S} c_g e_g(θ) e^{iψ(g)u}` is a trigonometric polynomial with
`#S` distinct integer frequencies whose coefficient at `ψ(γ_T)` has modulus `a`; so its sup
over `[0,2π]` is `≥ a`, and (I6) gives
`Leb{u ∈ [0,2π] : |C_{k_*}(θ j_ψ(u))| ≤ ηa}/2π ≤ A_N η^{1/(#S−1)} ≤ A_N η^{1/N_0}`. Integrate
over `θ` (invariance of `λ_{A_T}` under `j_ψ(u)`, Fubini).

*Step 4.* By invariance under `j(s)` and Fubini, then (3.2) on `{|C_{k_*}| > ηa}` and (3.3):

    λ_{A_T}{|F^{(T)} − c| ≤ ℓ} = ∫_{A_T} |E_θ|/(2π) dλ_{A_T}(θ) ≤ A_N η^{1/N_0} + A_N (ℓ/(ηa))^{1/(2N_0)}     (ℓ ≤ ηa).

By Chebyshev's inequality and (3.1),

    ν_M([c−ℓ/2, c+ℓ/2]) ≤ λ_{A_T}{|F^{(T)} − c| ≤ ℓ} + 4V^{>T}/ℓ²
                        ≤ A_N η^{1/N_0} + A_N (ℓ/(ηa))^{1/(2N_0)} + 4C_1M² log²(T+3)/(Tℓ²).

Take `ℓ = T^{−1/2+ε/2}`, `η = T^{−ε/4}`: then `ℓ/(ηa) ≤ T^{−ε/4}(M + 1)/(2M)` for `T ≥ 1`, and the
right side is `≪_{M,χ,N_0} T^{−ε/(8N_0)} + T^{−ε} log²(T+3)`, uniformly in `c`. Letting `T → ∞`
along the sequence of FD gives `ν_M({c}) = 0` for every `c`. (Here `0 < ε < 1/2`, which
is forced by `γ_T ≤ T^{1/2−ε}`, was used in `ℓ/(ηa) ≤ T^{−ε/4}(M+1)/(2M)`; for large `T` this is
`≤ 1`, as required in (3.2).) If every `T ≥ T_0` is allowed: given a small `ℓ_1 > 0` take `T`
with `T^{−1/2+ε/2} = 2ℓ_1`; then `sup_c ν_M([c−ℓ_1, c+ℓ_1]) ≪ T^{−ε/(8N_0)} = (2ℓ_1)^β`,
`β = ε/(4N_0(1−ε))`. ∎

Hypotheses found in the literature under which the limiting distribution of a two-way race
is known to be continuous, or its density to exist (see `LITERATURE.md`; all quoted verbatim
there from the arXiv PDFs):

| Source | Hypothesis (besides GRH-type assumptions) | Relation |

| Devin, Thm 2.2; Bailleul 2007.05763, Thm 1.7; Devin 1909.03975, Cor. 2 | finite `Λ ≠ ∅` with `⟨Λ⟩_ℚ ∩ ⟨Γ∖Λ⟩_ℚ = {0}` | ⇒ TS (take `T ≥ max Λ`: `⟨Γ_{>T}⟩ ⊂ ⟨Γ∖Λ⟩ ∌ λ_1`); not conversely (Example 3.3) |

TS does not imply it (Example 3.3) and it does not imply TS (Example 3.3a) |
| Martin–Ng, Thm 1.5(a), 1.7(a) | three self-sufficient ordinates (⇒ weakly inclusive, i.e. the densities exist) | ⇒ one self-sufficient ordinate |

So: **FD is implied by each of the hypotheses in the table, and is strictly weaker than each
of them** as a condition on a set of frequencies (Example 3.3 satisfies TS, hence FD, and
none of the first three rows). Theorem 3.1 gives *absolute* continuity under TS (Devin's
Theorem 2.2 gives continuity under the direct-sum hypothesis, and an `L¹` density under
three self-sufficient ordinates). We did not find Corollary 3.2 or a criterion in terms of
the tail `Γ_{>T}` in the literature (search limited to the papers listed in `LITERATURE.md`).
The mechanism (move along a subgroup of the hull on which the function is a non-constant
trigonometric polynomial) is the same as Devin's; what is added is that the subgroup need
not be a direct factor carrying an *independent* summand: conditioning on the complementary
coordinates replaces independence. The gain is modest, and **none of these hypotheses is
known to hold**: not even the existence of two ordinates with irrational ratio is known
(Problem 1 of arXiv:2407.03530).

### 3.4 Tame directions and the structure of a hypothetical atom

A homomorphism `φ : G → ℝ` is called *tame* if `|φ(γ)| ≤ C_φ log(γ+3)` for all `γ ∈ Γ`. For
`v ∈ ℝ` let `C^φ_v := Σ_{g∈±Γ, φ(g)=v} m_g (2M/(M+ig)) e_g ∈ L²(A)` (the part of `F_M − 1` on
which the direction `φ` has frequency `v`).

**Theorem 3.5 (GRH + NCZ).** Let `φ` be tame, `j_φ(s) := (e^{iφ(γ)s})_{γ∈Γ}` (a one-parameter
subgroup of `A`, by (I2)).

(a) For `λ_A`-a.e. `θ` there is a real-analytic function `h_θ` on `ℝ` with
`F_M(θ j_φ(s)) = h_θ(s)` for Lebesgue-a.e. `s`.

(b) If the level set `S := {F_M = c}` has positive measure, then `S` is invariant under
`j_φ(ℝ)` up to null sets, `C^φ_v = 0` a.e. on `S` for every `v ≠ 0`, and
`1 + C^φ_0 = c` a.e. on `S`. (Invariance is a property of the full level set, not of its
subsets; the last two conclusions pass to subsets.)

(c) If for some `v ≠ 0` the set `{g ∈ ±Γ : φ(g) = v}` is finite and nonempty, then `ν_M` is
absolutely continuous.

*Proof.* (a) Take `c_0 := 1/(4C_φ)`. For `z` in the strip `Σ := {|Im z| < c_0}` and `N ≥ 1` let
`F^z_N(θ) := 1 + Σ_{g∈±Γ, |g|≤N} m_g (2M/(M+ig)) e^{iφ(g)z} θ_g`. For `|Im z| ≤ c_0`,
`|e^{iφ(g)z}| ≤ (|g|+3)^{1/4}`, so by orthonormality, for `N < N'`,

    sup_{z∈Σ} ‖F^z_{N'} − F^z_N‖_2² ≤ Σ_{γ∈Γ, γ>N} 2 m_γ² · 4M² (γ+3)^{1/2}/γ² =: ρ_N → 0

(convergent by (Q3) and `m_γ ≤ A_0 log(γ+3)`). Choose `N_1 < N_2 < …` with `Σ_i ρ_{N_i}^{1/2} < ∞`.
For fixed `θ`, `z ↦ F^z_N(θ)` is entire; for a holomorphic `u` on a neighbourhood of a
closed disc `\bar D(z_0,2r)`, `sup_{D(z_0,r)}|u|² ≤ (πr²)^{−1}∫_{D(z_0,2r)}|u|² dA` (sub-mean-value
property). Hence for discs with `\bar D(z_0,2r) ⊂ Σ`,

    ∫_A sup_{z∈D(z_0,r)} |F^z_{N_{i+1}}(θ) − F^z_{N_i}(θ)| dλ_A ≤ (πr²)^{−1/2} (∫_{D(z_0,2r)} ‖F^z_{N_{i+1}} − F^z_{N_i}‖_2² dA(z))^{1/2} ≤ 2ρ_{N_i}^{1/2},

which is summable in `i`. Covering `Σ` by countably many such discs: for a.e. `θ` the
sequence `z ↦ F^z_{N_i}(θ)` converges locally uniformly on `Σ` to a holomorphic `H_θ`. Also
`F^0_{N_i} → F_M` in `L²` with summable errors, hence a.e.; and
`F^s_N(θ) = F^0_N(θ j_φ(s))` for real `s`. So for a.e. `θ`: for a.e. `s`,
`F_M(θ j_φ(s)) = lim_i F^0_{N_i}(θ j_φ(s)) = H_θ(s)` (Fubini and invariance of `λ_A`: the set
of `(θ,s)` with `θ j_φ(s)` outside the a.e.-convergence set is null). `F_M` is real, so
`h_θ := H_θ|_ℝ` is real-analytic.

(b) Fix a Borel version of `F_M` and let `S = {F_M = c}`, `S_θ := {s : θ j_φ(s) ∈ S}`. The set
`N := {θ ∈ S : Leb(S_θ) = 0}` is null: if `θ j_φ(s_0) ∈ N` then `{s : θ j_φ(s) ∈ N} ⊂ S_θ` is a
translate of a null set, so every orbit meets `N` in a Lebesgue-null set of times, and
`λ_A(N) = ∫_A Leb{s ∈ [0,1] : θ j_φ(s) ∈ N} dλ_A = 0`. For a.e. `θ ∈ S` the function `h_θ` of (a)
therefore equals `c` on a set of positive measure, so `h_θ ≡ c`, i.e. `θ j_φ(s) ∈ S` for a.e.
`s`. Integrating, `λ_A(S ∖ S j_φ(−s)) = 0` for a.e. `s`; translations preserve `λ_A`, so
`λ_A(S △ S j_φ(s)) = 0` for a.e. `s`, hence for all `s` (continuity of translations in
`L¹(A)`). Let `U_s f := f∘(· j_φ(s))` on `L²(A)` and, for
`v ∈ ℝ`, `P_v f := L²\text{-}lim_{R→∞} (1/R)∫_0^R e^{−ivs}U_s f ds` (mean ergodic theorem; on
characters `P_v e_g = e_g` if `φ(g) = v` and `0` otherwise, so `P_v(F_M − 1) = C^φ_v` for
`v ≠ 0` and `P_0 F_M = 1 + C^φ_0`). Since `1_{S}` is `U`-invariant, `P_v(1_S f) = 1_S P_v f`.
Apply this to `1_S F_M = c 1_S`: `1_S C^φ_v = P_v(c1_S) = 0` for `v ≠ 0`, and
`1_S(1 + C^φ_0) = c 1_S`.

(c) If `ν_M(B) > 0` for a Lebesgue-null Borel `B`, then, as in the proof of Theorem 3.1,
there is a set of `θ` of positive measure with `Leb{s ∈ [0,1] : h_θ(s) ∈ B} > 0`; for such
`θ`, `h_θ` is constant by Lemma 1.2. So `S_1 := {θ : h_θ constant}` has positive measure; it
is `j_φ`-invariant, and `c(θ) := h_θ(0)` is a `j_φ`-invariant measurable function with
`F_M = c(θ)` a.e. on `S_1`. The argument of (b) (with `1_{S_1}F_M = c(·)1_{S_1}`, a
`U`-invariant function) gives `C^φ_v = 0` a.e. on `S_1` for all `v ≠ 0`. Under the hypothesis
of (c), `C^φ_v` is a nonzero trigonometric polynomial; contradiction with Lemma 1.1. ∎

**Corollary 3.6 (necessary conditions for an atom).** Assume GRH + NCZ and `ν_M({c}) > 0`,
`S = {F_M = c}`. Then for **every** tame homomorphism `φ : G → ℝ`: `1_S` is invariant under
`j_φ(ℝ)`; for every `v ≠ 0` the fibre `{g ∈ ±Γ : φ(g) = v}` is empty or infinite; and if
`c ≠ 1` the fibre `{γ ∈ Γ : φ(γ) = 0}` is infinite. (For finitely supported `φ` this gives
the atom case of Corollary 3.2; the general case of Corollary 3.2 is Theorem 3.1.)

*Proof.* Theorem 3.5(b), and Lemma 1.1: a nonzero trigonometric polynomial `C^φ_v` cannot
vanish on `S`; and if the fibre over `0` were finite, `1 + C^φ_0` would be a trigonometric
polynomial equal to `c` on a set of positive measure, hence identically `c`, which forces
`C^φ_0 = 0` and `c = 1`. ∎

## 4. Smoothed races, and level sets with interior

### 4.1 Analytic smoothing (Theorem C)

**Theorem 4.1 (GRH + NCZ, no LI).** Let `k : ℝ → ℝ` be measurable with
`∫(1+|u|)|k(u)|du < ∞`, `|\hat k(ξ)| ≤ C e^{−a|ξ|}` for some `a > 0`
(`\hat k(ξ) = ∫k(u)e^{−iξu}du`; e.g. a Gaussian, or `k(u) = (2a\cosh(πu/2a))^{−1}`,
`\hat k(ξ) = 1/\cosh(aξ)`), and `\hat k(γ_0) ≠ 0` for some `γ_0 ∈ Γ`. Let
`E^k_M(y) := ∫_ℝ E_M(e^{y−u}) k(u) du` (with `E_M(e^v) := 0` for `v < log 2`). Then `E^k_M` has
a limiting distribution `ν^k_M`, the law under `λ_A` of the continuous function
`F^k_M := \hat k(0) + Re Σ_{γ∈Γ} m_γ r_γ(M) \hat k(γ) e_γ`, and `ν^k_M` is absolutely continuous.
In particular `{y : E^k_M(y) > c}` has a density for every `c`.

*Proof.* *Limiting distribution.* Let `h(v) := E_M(e^v)`, `P_T(v) := F_{M,T}(ι(v))`
(`GRH-ONLY.md` §1.3), `r_T := h − P_T` on `ℝ` (so `r_T = −P_T` on `(−∞, log 2)`, bounded by
`B_T := sup|P_T|`). By `GRH-ONLY.md` Theorem 1.3(a),
`ρ_T := limsup_Y (1/Y)∫_0^Y |r_T|² → 0` as `T → ∞`, and `C_T := sup_{Y≥1}(1/Y)∫_0^Y|r_T|² < ∞`.
By Cauchy–Schwarz `|k*r_T(y)|² ≤ ‖k‖_1 ∫|k(u)||r_T(y−u)|²du`, hence

    (1/Y)∫_0^Y |k*r_T|² dy ≤ ‖k‖_1 ∫ |k(u)| [ (1/Y)∫_0^{Y+|u|} |r_T|² + B_T² min(|u|,Y)/Y ] du .

For `|u| ≤ Y` the bracket is `≤ 2 sup_{Y'≥Y}(1/Y')∫_0^{Y'}|r_T|² + B_T²|u|/Y`; for `|u| > Y` it is
`≤ 2C_T|u|/Y + B_T²`. Since `∫(1+|u|)|k| < ∞`, dominated convergence gives
`limsup_Y (1/Y)∫_0^Y|k*r_T|² ≤ 2‖k‖_1² ρ_T`. Now `k*P_T = F^k_{M,T}∘ι` with
`F^k_{M,T} := \hat k(0) + Re Σ_{γ≤T} m_γ r_γ \hat k(γ) e_γ`, and `F^k_{M,T} → F^k_M` uniformly on
`A` (absolute convergence). So `E^k_M − F^k_M∘ι → 0` in the `B²` seminorm, and by (I1) applied
to the continuous function `F^k_M` the limiting distribution of `E^k_M` is the law of `F^k_M`.

*Absolute continuity.* For every `θ ∈ A`,
`t ↦ F^k_M(τ_tθ) = \hat k(0) + Σ_{g∈±Γ} m_g (2M/(M+ig)) \hat k(g) θ_g e^{igt}` converges
absolutely and uniformly on `{|Im t| ≤ a'}` for `a' < a`; it is real-analytic on `ℝ`, and
not constant, since its Bohr coefficient at `γ_0`,
`lim (1/R)∫_0^R (·)e^{−iγ_0t}dt = m_{γ_0}(2M/(M+iγ_0))\hat k(γ_0)θ_{γ_0}`, is nonzero. For a
Lebesgue-null Borel `B`, by invariance of `λ_A` under `τ_t` and Fubini,
`ν^k_M(B) = ∫_A Leb{t ∈ [0,1] : F^k_M(τ_tθ) ∈ B} dλ_A(θ) = 0` (Lemma 1.2). ∎

**Corollary 4.2.** For `s > 0` let `k_s` be the centred Gaussian of variance `s²` and
`δ^{(s)}` the density of `{y : E^{k_s}_M(y) > 0}` (it exists by Theorem 4.1). Then
`ν_M((0,∞)) ≤ liminf_{s→0} δ^{(s)} ≤ limsup_{s→0} δ^{(s)} ≤ ν_M([0,∞))`.

*Proof.* `F^{k_s}_M → F_M` in `L²(A)` (dominated convergence on coefficients), so
`ν^{k_s}_M → ν_M` weakly; (I3). ∎

So the only obstacle to the existence of the density is the lack of smoothness of the race
in `log x` at scale `0`: every analytic mollification of it has a density under GRH alone.
(Chebyshev's 1853 sum `Σ χ(p)e^{−p/x}` is a mollification of the same kind in spirit — its
explicit formula has coefficients `Γ(ρ)`, exponentially small — though it is not literally
of the form `E^k_M`; there the race is never lost: `monotonicity/chebyshev1853`.)

### 4.2 No level set has interior (Proposition D)

**Proposition 4.3 (GRH + NCZ).** For every `M > 0`, `c ∈ ℝ` and every nonempty open `U ⊂ A`:
`λ_A(U ∖ {F_M = c}) > 0`.

*Proof.* Suppose `F_M = c` a.e. on `U`. Choose a nonempty open `U' ⊂ U` and `δ > 0` with
`τ_tU' ⊂ U` for `|t| < δ`, and then a nonempty open `U'' ⊂ U'` and `δ_0 > 0` with
`τ_tU'' ⊂ U'` for `|t| < δ_0`. For `η ∈ C_c^∞((−δ,δ))` put

    W_η(θ) := ½∫η + Σ_{g∈±Γ} m_g θ_g ∫ η(t)e^{igt}dt

(absolutely convergent since `∫η e^{igt}dt = O_N(|g|^{−N})`; continuous on `A`). Comparing
Fourier coefficients (`F_M` has coefficient `m_g·2M/(M+ig)` at `e_g`, and
`∫(−η'+Mη)e^{igt}dt = (M+ig)∫ηe^{igt}dt`):

    ∫ F_M(τ_tθ) (−η'(t) + Mη(t)) dt = 2M W_η(θ)     for a.e. θ.

For a.e. `θ ∈ U'` we have `F_M(τ_tθ) = c` for a.e. `|t| < δ` (Fubini), so the left side is
`cM∫η`. Hence `2W_η = c∫η` a.e. on `U'`, and everywhere on `U'` by continuity (nonempty open
sets have positive measure). By (I1) (time averages of a continuous bump supported in `U''`)
there are arbitrarily large `y` with `ι(y) ∈ U''`, hence `ι(t) ∈ U'` for `|t−y| < δ_0`; for `y`
large this interval contains `t_0 = log p` for a prime `p ∤ q`. Apply the above at
`θ = ι(t_0)` and use (I5) with the test function `η(· − t_0)`:

    (c/2)∫η = W_η(ι(t_0)) = ½∫η − Σ_n Λ(n)χ(n)n^{−1/2} η(log n − t_0) + ∫η(t−t_0)Ω(t)dt .

Take `η(t) = η_1(t/ϵ)` with `η_1 ≥ 0`, `η_1(0) = 1`, supported in `(−1,1)`, and let `ϵ → 0`:
all terms tend to `0` except the one with `n = p`, which equals `−χ(p)(log p)/√p ≠ 0`.
Contradiction. ∎

So a hypothetical atom is carried by a set which is "nowhere dense in measure". This is
where the explicit formula on the dense orbit enters; it says nothing about such sets.

### 4.3 Finite-order smoothing

**Proposition 4.4 (finite-order smoothing does not create atoms; GRH + NCZ).** Let `κ > 0`,
`M > 0`, and `G := K_κ F_M`, `K_κ f(θ) := ∫_0^∞ κe^{−κu} f(τ_{−u}θ) du` (so
`\hat G(g) = \hat F_M(g)·κ/(κ+ig)`; these coefficients are absolutely summable, and `G` denotes
the continuous version). Then for every `c`:
`{G = c} ⊂ {F_M = c}` up to a `λ_A`-null set. In particular the law of `G` has no atom at any
level at which `ν_M` has none, and for `G_{M,M'} = K_M F_{M'}` (§2):
`λ_A{G_{M,M'} = c} ≤ λ_A({F_M = c} ∩ {F_{M'} = c})`.

*Proof.* `∫_A∫_0^∞ κe^{−κu}|F_M(τ_{−u}θ)| du dλ_A = ‖F_M‖_1 < ∞`, so for a.e. `θ` the function
`f_θ(t) := F_M(τ_tθ)` satisfies `∫_{−∞}^t e^{κs}|f_θ(s)|ds < ∞` for all `t`, and
`g_θ(t) := G(τ_tθ) = ∫_{−∞}^t κe^{−κ(t−s)}f_θ(s)ds` is locally absolutely continuous with
`g_θ' = κ(f_θ − g_θ)` a.e. The derivative of an absolutely continuous function vanishes a.e.
on each of its level sets; so for a.e. `t` with `g_θ(t) = c` we get `f_θ(t) = g_θ(t) = c`.
Fubini (invariance of `λ_A` under `τ_t`) gives the inclusion. For `G_{M,M'}`: on `{G_{M,M'} = c}`
we get `F_{M'} = c`, and then `F_M = c` by (2.2). ∎

## 5. Time reversal: approximate symmetry without LI, and improved bounds

Write `θ_γ = e^{iφ_γ}` and

    w_γ := 8M²/(M²+γ²) = 2 Re r_γ(M),      s_γ := 4Mγ/(M²+γ²) = −Im r_γ(M),      (w_γ/2)² + s_γ² = |r_γ|²,

    R := Σ_{γ∈Γ} m_γ w_γ cos φ_γ      (absolutely convergent; continuous on A; |R| ≤ Σ m_γ w_γ = σ_M²),
    Y := Σ_{γ∈Γ} m_γ s_γ sin φ_γ ∈ L²(A).

`σ_M² = 4M(Λ'/Λ)(M+1/2)` is the closed form of `GRH-ONLY.md` (Q2) (zeros counted with
multiplicity to the first power). Let `inv(θ) := θ^{−1}`; it preserves `λ_A`.

**Theorem 5.1 (reflection; GRH + NCZ, no LI).** For every `M > 0`:

(a) `F_M = 1 + R/2 + Y` a.e., `R∘inv = R`, `Y∘inv = −Y`. Hence
`F_M(θ) + F_M(θ^{−1}) = 2 + R(θ) ∈ [2 − σ_M², 2 + σ_M²]` for a.e. `θ`, and the law of `Y` is
symmetric about `0`.

(b) For every real `t`: `ν_M((−∞,t]) ≤ ν_M([2 − σ_M² − t, ∞))` and
`ν_M([t,∞)) ≤ ν_M((−∞, 2 + σ_M² − t])`. Equivalently, there is a coupling `(X,X')` of `ν_M` with
itself such that `|X + X' − 2| ≤ σ_M²` almost surely: `ν_M` is symmetric about its mean `1` up
to `σ_M²` in the `L^∞`-Wasserstein distance.

(c) If `σ_M² < 2` (i.e. `M < M_σ(χ)`): the sets `{F_M ≤ 0}` and `inv{F_M ≤ 0}` are disjoint
mod null sets, `2ν_M((−∞,0]) = λ_A{|Y| ≥ 1 + R/2}`, and in particular
`ν_M((−∞,0]) ≤ 1/2`: the lower logarithmic density of `{D_m > 0}` is `≥ 1/2` for every
`−1/2 < m < M_σ(χ) − 1/2`. More precisely `ν_M((−∞,t]) ≤ 1/2` for `t < 1 − σ_M²/2` and
`ν_M([t,∞)) ≤ 1/2` for `t > 1 + σ_M²/2`.

*Proof.* (a) `Re(r_γθ_γ) = Re r_γ cos φ_γ − Im r_γ sin φ_γ`; under `inv`, `φ_γ ↦ −φ_γ`. The
identities hold in `L²(A)` (compare coefficients), hence a.e. (b) `X := F_M`,
`X' := F_M∘inv` have the same law, and `X' ≤ t` implies `X ≥ 2 − σ² − t`. (c)
`F_M ≤ 0 ⟺ Y ≤ −(1+R/2)`, and `F_M∘inv ≤ 0 ⟺ Y ≥ 1 + R/2`; `1 + R/2 ≥ 1 − σ²/2 > 0`. The
two sets have equal measure. Last statement: `ν_M((−∞,t]) ≤ ν_M([2−σ²−t,∞))` and the two
half-lines are disjoint when `t < 1 − σ²/2`, so `ν_M((−∞,t]) ≤ 1/2`; symmetrically above. ∎

(Under LI the law of `F_M` is exactly symmetric about `1`, by the independent rotations
`φ_γ ↦ φ_γ + π`; that symmetry is not available without LI. Devin (arXiv:1706.06394,
Theorem 2.3) obtains exact symmetry from the weaker hypothesis that every integer relation
`Σk_γγ = 0` has `Σk_γ` even, which puts the simultaneous half-turn `φ_γ ↦ φ_γ + π` in the
hull; this is the nearest prior result we found (`LITERATURE.md`, third check), and it is
also unproved. The involution used here, `y ↦ −y` on the orbit, is always available. On the prime side `F_M∘inv` is the limit
object of the "tail race" `−2Mx^{M}Σ_{p>x} χ*(p)p^{−M−1/2}log p`; not used.)

**Theorem 5.2 (GRH + NCZ; no LI, no non-resonance).** If `σ_M² < 2`, then for every
`0 < c < 2 − σ_M²`,

    ν_M((−∞,0]) ≤ (V_M + (1−c)²) / (c² + (2 − σ_M² − c)²);     in particular  ν_M((−∞,0]) ≤ V_M / (1 + (1 − σ_M²)²).

*Proof.* Let `N := {F_M ≤ 0}`, `N' := {F_M ≥ 2 − σ_M²}`. By Theorem 5.1(a), `inv N ⊂ N'` mod
null sets, so `λ_A(N') ≥ λ_A(N)`; `N ∩ N' = ∅`. `(F_M − c)² ≥ c²` on `N` and `≥ (2−σ²−c)²` on `N'`,
so `V_M + (1−c)² = ∫(F_M−c)² ≥ (c² + (2−σ²−c)²)λ_A(N)`. ∎

(The "in particular" form is the case `c = 1`, admissible when `σ_M² < 1`. For `1 ≤ σ_M² < 2` the
stated bound still holds: there `(1−σ_M²)² < 1 ≤ σ_M² ≤ V_M`, so `V_M/(1+(1−σ_M²)²) > V_M/(1+V_M)`,
which bounds `ν_M((−∞,0])` by Cantelli's inequality (mean `1`, variance `V_M`). Added after the
coordinating session's referee, `REFEREE-2-DENSITY.md`, finding 1.)

(For small `V_M` this is about half of Cantelli's `V_M/(1+V_M)`, as for a symmetric law.)

**Theorem 5.3 (GRH + NCZ; finite non-resonance).** In the setting of `GRH-ONLY.md`
Theorem 6.1 (head `γ_1 < … < γ_N`, complete list of simple zeros up to `T ≥ 3γ_N`,
`T' = T − 2γ_N`, `𝒦 = Γ ∩ (γ_N,T']`, conditions (NR1)–(NR3)), write `w_n, s_n` for

    2 ν_M((−∞,0]) ≤ J_R := ½a_0² Σs_n² + ¼(Σs_n²)(Σk_n²) − ⅛Σ s_n²k_n² + (a_0² + ½Σk_n²) V^o_𝒦 + (a_0 + Σk_n)² V^o_t .

*Proof.* `ρ = ½Σ_{γ∉head} m_γ w_γ` (closed form of `σ_M²`; head zeros simple), so with

`|x| ≤ x_max < 1`. Since `1/(1+x) = 1 − x + x²/(1+x) ≤ 1 − x + e`,

    κ := (1 + e − x)/(1−ρ) = a_0 − Σ_{n≤N} k_n cos φ_n  ≥ 1/((1−ρ)(1+x)) ≥ 1/(1 + R/2) > 0 .

In particular `1 + R/2 > 0` everywhere, which is all that the proof of Theorem 5.1(c) uses. So
`2ν_M((−∞,0]) = λ_A{|Y| ≥ 1+R/2} ≤ λ_A{κ|Y| ≥ 1} ≤ ∫_A κ²Y² dλ_A`. Split
`Y = Y_h + Y_𝒦 + Y_t` (head; `𝒦`; ordinates `> T'`, with multiplicities). `κ²` and `Y_h` are
trigonometric polynomials of degrees `2` and `1` in `e_{γ_1},…,e_{γ_N}`. Exactly as in items

1. `∫κ²Y_h²` has degree `4` in the head, so by (NR1) it equals its value for independent
   uniform phases: `E[Y_h²] = ½Σs_n²`; `E[Y_h² cos φ_l] = 0`;

   `E sin²cos² = ⅛`, `E sin cos = 0`). This gives the first three terms.

4. `∫κ²Y_𝒦Y_t = 0` by (NR3) and the buffer `T' = T − 2γ_N`.

   `∫Y_t² = ½Σ_{γ>T'} m_γ²s_γ² = V_M − V^e − ½Σ_{n≤N}s_n² − V^o_𝒦`. ∎

## 6. Equivalent forms and necessary conditions for an atom

**Proposition 6.1 (band densities; GRH + NCZ).** For every `M > 0` and `c ∈ ℝ`:

    ν_M({c}) = lim_{ε↓0} \underline d{x : |E_M(x) − c| < ε} = lim_{ε↓0} \bar d{x : |E_M(x) − c| ≤ ε}.

So `ν_{1/2}({0}) = 0` for `χ_{−4}` iff for every `η > 0` there is `ε > 0` such that
`{x : |π(x;4,3) − π(x;4,1)| ≤ ε√x/log x}` has upper logarithmic density `< η`.

*Proof.* (1.1), and `ν_M((c−ε,c+ε)) ↓ ν_M({c})`, `ν_M([c−ε,c+ε]) ↓ ν_M({c})`. ∎

**Proposition 6.2 (an atom forces simultaneous balance in multiplicative short intervals;
GRH + NCZ).** Suppose `ν_M({c}) = η > 0`, `S := {F_M = c}`, and put
`ω(h) := λ_A(S ∖ τ_{−h}S)` (so `ω(h) → 0` as `h → 0`, by continuity of translations in
`L¹(A)`). Write `Δ_m(x,h) := Σ_{x<p≤xe^h} χ*(p) p^m log p`. Then for all `h_1,…,h_k ≥ 0` and
`ε > 0` the set of `x` with

    |E^θ_M(x) − c| < ε   and   |Δ_m(x,h_i) − (c/2M)(e^{Mh_i} − 1)x^M| < (ε/M) e^{Mh_i} x^M   (i = 1,…,k)

has lower logarithmic density `≥ η − Σ_i ω(h_i)`. For the classical race (`c = 0`, `m = 0`,
`q = 4`): for every `ε > 0` and all small `h`, the inequality

    |θ(xe^h;4,3) − θ(x;4,3) − θ(xe^h;4,1) + θ(x;4,1)| < 2ε e^{h/2} √x

would hold on a set of `x` of lower logarithmic density `≥ η − ω(h)`.

*Proof.* With `g(y) := E^θ_M(e^y)`, the vector `(g(y), g(y+h_1),…,g(y+h_k))` has limiting
distribution the law of `(F_M, F_M∘τ_{h_1},…,F_M∘τ_{h_k})` under `λ_A` (same proof as
`GRH-ONLY.md` Theorem 1.3(b): `g(·+h_i) − P_{M,T}(·+h_i)` is small in the `B²` seminorm, and
`(P_{M,T}(y+h_i))_i` is a continuous function of `ι(y)`). The open set
`{|v_0−c| < ε, |v_i − c| < ε ∀i}` has mass `≥ λ_A(S ∩ ⋂_i τ_{−h_i}S) ≥ η − Σω(h_i)` under that
law; (I3). Finally `g(y+h) − e^{−Mh}g(y) = 2M(xe^h)^{−M}Δ_m(x,h)` (`x = e^y`), so
`|g(y)−c| < ε` and `|g(y+h)−c| < ε` imply
`|2M(xe^h)^{−M}Δ_m(x,h) − c(1−e^{−Mh})| < 2ε`. ∎

**Remark 6.3 (size of what is being excluded).** The mean square of
`g(y+h) − e^{−Mh}g(y)` is `(1−e^{−Mh})² + ½Σ_γ m_γ²|r_γ|²|e^{iγh} − e^{−Mh}|² ≥ e^{−Mh}Σ_{γ>0}|r_γ|²(1−cos γh) ≍ h log(1/h)`
as `h → 0` (zero counting). So the balance forced by an atom is at a scale `ε` arbitrarily
small compared with the root mean square `≍ (h log(1/h))^{1/2}` of the same quantity. This
is an anti-concentration statement for the limiting distribution of a short-interval sum,
i.e. again an "atom" question for another almost periodic function; we could not break the
circle. The converse of Proposition 6.2 is not claimed.

**Remark 6.4 (ties; Problem 13 of arXiv:2407.03530).** Exact ties `D_0(x) = 0` are contained
in every band, so their upper density is `≤ ν_{1/2}({0}) < 0.0891` (Theorem 5.4), and it is
`0` under FD. To do better than the band one must work at scales invisible to the hull. An
elementary computation shows what would be needed: if `Z ⊂ [X,2X]` is the tie set and
`|Z| ≥ ηX`, then for every `1 ≤ H ≤ ηX/2`, by Cauchy–Schwarz,
`∫_0^H |Z ∩ (Z−u)| du ≥ η²HX/8`, so for a set of `u ∈ [0,H]` of measure `≥ η²H/16` one has
`Σ_{t<p≤t+u} χ(p) = 0` for a set of `t ∈ [X,2X]` of measure `≥ η²X/16`. Excluding this for,
say, `H = (log X)^3` is a local limit statement for `Σ_{t<p≤t+u}χ(p)` (a sum of `≍ u/log X`
signs vanishing with probability `≫ 1`), which implies in particular a statement on the
parity of the number of primes in short intervals. We know of no technique for this, with
or without GRH. **Not proved: ties have density zero.**

## 7. Obstructions: what the available information cannot decide

Everything GRH gives about the hull function is its Fourier series: frequencies `±Γ`,
coefficients `m_γ·2M/(M ± iγ)`, i.e. `(D + M)F_M = 2M·w` with `w = ½ + Σ_{g∈±Γ} m_g e_g` a
positive-definite formal series with *positive integer* coefficients. The proofs of §2 and
of Theorems 5.1, 5.2 use only the positivity of the weights `m_γ` and the size of `σ_M²`
(Theorem 5.3 uses in addition the finite arithmetic conditions (NR1)–(NR3)); those of §3 use
hypothetical algebraic information on `Γ`; §4.2 uses the explicit formula on the dense orbit.

**Proposition 7.1 (a positive-weight model with an atom at 0).** Let `M > 0`, `0 < a < π`,
`a/π ∉ ℚ`, `κ² := 2π²/(M²a²)`. On `𝕋 = ℝ/2πℤ` with the flow `t ↦ t + s` put

    w_n := κ²(M²+n²) sin²(na/2)/(π²n²)  ∈ (0, κ²(M²+1)/π²]     (n ≥ 1),
    F(t) := 1 + Re Σ_{n≥1} w_n · (4M/(M+in)) e^{int}  ∈ L²(𝕋).

Then `F(t) = (Mκ²/π)·[M(a−|t|) + sgn t]·1_{|t|<a}` on `(−π,π]`; in particular `F = 0` on the
arc `a < |t| ≤ π`, so the law of `F` has an atom at `0` of mass `1 − a/π`. Its variance
parameter is `Σ_{n≥1} w_n·8M²/(M²+n²) = 4π/a − 2 > 2`.

*Proof.* Let `v := κ1_{[0,a]}`, `\hat v(n) = (1/2π)∫v e^{−int}dt`, so `\hat v(0) = κa/2π` and
`|\hat v(n)|² = κ² sin²(na/2)/(π²n²)`, and let `Φ(t) := (1/2π)∫v(t+s)v(s)ds = κ²(a−|t|)^+/(2π)`,
`\hat Φ(n) = |\hat v(n)|²`. The function `2M(MΦ − Φ')` equals the displayed closed form, and its
Fourier coefficients are `2M(M−in)|\hat v(n)|² = w_n·2M/(M+in)` for `n ≠ 0` (with `w_{−n} := w_n`)
and `2M²κ²a²/4π² = 1` for `n = 0`; this is the two-sided form of the series defining `F`.
Finally `Σ_{n≥1}w_n 8M²/(M²+n²) = 4M²Σ_{n≠0}|\hat v(n)|² = 4M²(κ²a/2π − κ²a²/4π²) = 4π/a − 2`. ∎

So the shape of the spectrum (coefficients `≍ 1/γ`, positive weights) does not exclude an
atom at a given weight; the proof must use at least one of: (i) `σ_M² < 2`; (ii) integrality
of the weights; (iii) the growth `N(T) ~ (T/2π) log T`; (iv) arithmetic information on `Γ`.

**Proposition 7.2 (positive weights `≥ 1` and arbitrarily small `σ²` do not exclude an atom
at 0).** Let `0 < r < α' < β' < π`, let `J ⊂ (α',β')` be a closed arc and `χ ∈ C_c^∞((α',β'))`,
`0 ≤ χ ≤ 1`, `χ = 1` on `J`. Put `B_χ := e^π(π + 2‖χ'‖_{L¹} + π‖χ''‖_{L¹})`,
`e_r := inf_{x≥r}(1 − sin x/x) > 0`, `C_* := B_χ r/(2e_r)`,
`M_1 := min(1, (πe^π + 2C_*r/(3π))^{−1})`. Then for every `0 < M ≤ M_1` there are `c_n > 0`
(`n ≥ 1`) with `Σc_n ≤ 2C_*M` such that

    Ψ(t) := 1 + Σ_{n≥1} c_n cos nt   satisfies   Ψ' = MΨ on J,
    F(t) := Ψ − Ψ'/M = 1 + Σ_{n≥1} c_n (cos nt + (n/M) sin nt) ∈ L^∞(𝕋)   vanishes on J.

In the notation of Proposition 7.1, `F = 1 + Re Σ w_n (4M/(M+in))e^{int}` with weights
`w_n = c_n(M²+n²)/(4M²)` satisfying `1 ≤ B_χ/(4πM) ≤ w_n ≤ 12C_*/(πrM)` for **every** `n ≥ 1`,
variance parameter `σ² = Σ w_n 8M²/(M²+n²) = 2Σc_n ≤ 4C_*M`, and the law of `F` has an atom at
`0` of mass `≥ |J|/2π`. (Rescaling `t ↦ t/Ω`, `Ω := 1/(2M)`: the same weights on the
frequencies `Ω, 2Ω, 3Ω, …` give a model at the classical weight `1/2`.)

*Proof.* Fourier coefficients on `𝕋` are `\hat f(n) = (1/2π)∫_{−π}^{π} f(t)e^{−int}dt`. Let
`u(t) := (e^{M(t−α')} − 1)χ(t) ≥ 0` on `(0,π)`, `G(t) := u(|t|)` (`|t| ≤ π`),
`P(t) := ((1−|t|/r)^+)²`. For `n ≥ 1`, two integrations by parts give
`\hat G(n) = −(πn²)^{−1}∫_0^π u''(t)cos(nt)dt`, and
`u'' = M²e^{M(t−α')}χ + 2Me^{M(t−α')}χ' + (e^{M(t−α')}−1)χ''` with `e^{M(t−α')} ≤ e^π`,
`e^{M(t−α')} − 1 ≤ Mπe^π` (`M ≤ 1`); so `|\hat G(n)| ≤ MB_χ/(πn²)`, and `0 ≤ \hat G(0) ≤ Mπe^π`.
A direct computation gives `\hat P(0) = r/(3π)` and, for `n ≥ 1`,
`\hat P(n) = (2/(πrn²))(1 − sin(nr)/(nr))`, so `2e_r/(πrn²) ≤ \hat P(n) ≤ 4/(πrn²)`. With
`A := 2C_*M` we get `A\hat P(n) ≥ 2|\hat G(n)|` for all `n ≥ 1`. Let `N := 1 + G + AP` and
`Z := \hat N(0) = 1 + \hat G(0) + A\hat P(0)`, so `1 ≤ Z ≤ 2` (by the choice of `M_1`). Then
`A\hat P(n)/2 ≤ \hat N(n) ≤ 3A\hat P(n)/2` for `n ≥ 1`, `Σ\hat N(n) < ∞`, and
`Ψ := N/Z = 1 + Σ c_n cos nt` with `c_n := 2\hat N(n)/Z > 0`;
`Σc_n = Ψ(0) − 1 = (1+A)/Z − 1 ≤ A` (as `G(0) = 0`, `P(0) = 1`, `Z ≥ 1`). On `J`: `χ = 1` and
`P = 0` (`r < α'`), so `Ψ = e^{M(t−α')}/Z` and `Ψ' = MΨ`. `Ψ` is Lipschitz, so `Ψ' ∈ L^∞` has the
Fourier series `−Σ c_n n sin nt`, which gives the series for `F`; `F = 0` on `J`. Weights:
`Re(w(4M/(M+in))e^{int}) = w(4M²/(M²+n²))(cos nt + (n/M) sin nt)`, so `w_n = c_n(M²+n²)/(4M²)`;
from `c_n ≥ A\hat P(n)/Z ≥ Ae_r/(πrn²)` we get `w_n ≥ c_n n²/(4M²) ≥ Ae_r/(4πrM²) = B_χ/(4πM)`,
which is `≥ 1` because `B_χ ≥ πe^π` and `M ≤ 1`; from `c_n ≤ 3A\hat P(n) ≤ 12A/(πrn²)` and
`M² + n² ≤ 2n²` we get `w_n ≤ 6A/(πrM²) = 12C_*/(πrM)`. ∎

**Remark 7.4 (the model test).** Fix the weights `w_n` of Proposition 7.3 and define the
whole family `F^{mod}_M := 1 + Re Σ w_n (4M/(M+in))e^{int}`, `M > 0`, on `𝕋`. This family has:
(a) a compact connected hull with a dense one-parameter flow; (b) positive bounded weights;
(c) the resolvent/Abel identities between weights, hence convex order, and — since
`σ² < 2` at `M = 1/2`, so that `λ > 0` near `1/2` — the lead-inheritance inclusion, the
transversality (2.1)–(2.2), real-analyticity in `M`, and a critical weight `M*` exactly as in
§2; (d) the time-reversal identity and everything in Theorems 5.1–5.2; and its law at
`M = 1/2` has an atom at `0`. Therefore **no argument that uses only (a)–(d) can prove
`ν_{1/2}({0}) = 0`**; Theorem 5.2 (an atom is `≤ V/(1+(1−σ²)²)`) and Theorem 2.1 (countably
many exceptional weights) are the kind of statement such arguments give. In particular the
following natural strategies fail, each because the model satisfies all its premises:
(1) *quasi-invariance of the law of the critical weight* under some transformation built
from the resolvent or from Abel summation between weights (in the model the law of `M*` has
an atom at `1/2`; and for `M' ≠ 1/2` one has, on `J`, `F^{mod}_{M'} = ±(|M'−½|/½)·G` with
`G = K_{M'}F^{mod}_{1/2} > 0` and `(D+M')G = 0` there, i.e. a non-vanishing multiple of
`e^{−M't}`; the same holds a.e. on the whole tie set `{F^{mod}_{1/2} = 0}`, where
`(D+M')G = 0` a.e. and hence `DG ≠ 0` a.e., so by the level-set argument of Proposition 4.4
the tie set at weight `1/2` produces no atom at any level at nearby weights: there is
nothing to transfer);
(2) *forcing too much mass* from the atom, its mirror image under time reversal near level
`2`, and lead inheritance (in the model all the resulting inequalities hold);
(3) *iterating the resolvent on the tie set* to get an ODE to all orders at density points
and concluding by unique continuation (in the model the ODE `(D+M)^k K_M^k F = 0` holds on
the open set `J` for every `k`, and nothing follows, because the `k`-fold smoothings are
`C^{k}`-like, not quasi-analytic: their coefficients decay polynomially).
What the true spectrum has and this model lacks: (α) **integer** weights; (β) the explicit
formula on a dense orbit, which excludes level sets with interior (Proposition 4.3; in the
model the level set is open); (γ) weights `≥ 1` on every frequency (in the model of
Proposition 7.3 many weights are `≈ 10^{−10}`; but the models of Proposition 7.2 have all
weights `≥ 1`, at the price of a sparse spectrum `Ωℕ`); (δ) a non-cyclic `G` (Remark 7.6);
(ε) the growth `N(T) ~ (T/2π)log T`, hence an unbounded `F_M` (`GRH-ONLY.md` Proposition 3.2;
the model functions are bounded). We expect that (δ) and (ε) are not essential — the
construction of Proposition 7.2 should adapt to higher-dimensional tori and to a dominating
function with coefficients `≍ n^{−2}log n` — but this was **not carried out**. For a
positive-weight model on `𝕋` with `σ² < 2`, an arc `J` on which `F = 0` has length
`≤ M^{−1} log((2+σ²)/(2−σ²))`: `Ψ = 1 + R/2` takes values in `[1−σ²/2, 1+σ²/2]` and is
proportional to `e^{Mt}` on `J`.
So a proof of `ν_{1/2}({0}) = 0` must use at least one of (α)–(ε); the soft structure
(a)–(d) is not enough. Our guess is that it has to be (α) or (β), used in a way that reaches
sets of positive measure with empty interior. We have no idea how to do either.

**Remark 7.5 (what (ii) gives in the cyclic model; not used).** If all `w_n` are integers,
`w_n = O(n^k)`, and `F ≡ c` on an arc, then `Σ_{n≥1} w_n z^n` continues analytically across an
arc of the unit circle, hence is a rational function by the Pólya–Carlson theorem (a power
series with integer coefficients and radius of convergence `1` is rational or has the unit
circle as natural boundary), so `(w_n)` is eventually a quasi-polynomial; comparing constant
terms one finds `(1−c)/2 ∈ ℤ`, i.e. `c` is an odd integer: with integer weights a flat arc
at level `0` is impossible in the cyclic model, "because of the half-integer `½`" which
is Chebyshev's bias term. We did not find an analogue for sets of positive measure or for
non-cyclic `G`, where the coefficients `m_γθ_γ` on a general orbit are no longer integers.
**Sketch only.** (Now proved as Theorem 9.16. The only missing step was "`F ≡ c` on an arc ⇒
`g` continues across the arc", supplied there by a Herglotz transform. Given that step,
Pólya–Carlson gives rationality: continuation across one arc excludes a natural boundary.
Corollary 9.16′ covers rank-one `G`.)

**Remark 7.6 (`G` is not cyclic).** The ordinates of `L(s,χ)` are not all integer multiples
of one real number `2π/P`: otherwise the left side of (I5), as a distribution in the
translation parameter, would be `P`-periodic, so the atom `−χ(p)(log p)/√p` of the right
side at `log p` would recur at `log p + kP` for all `k ≥ 1`, whereas the atoms of the right
side at `log n` have size `Λ(n)/√n → 0`. (This is far from Problem 1 of arXiv:2407.03530:
it does not exclude that all ordinates are rational multiples of one number with
unbounded denominators, in which case `A` is a one-dimensional solenoid, every homomorphism
`G → ℝ` is a multiple of the inclusion, so there is no nonzero tame direction, and §3 is
empty.)

**Remark 7.7 (scales).** The hull retains the behaviour of `E(e^y)` at shifts `h` fixed as
`y → ∞`. Integrality of `π(x;4,3) − π(x;4,1)`, the size `y e^{−y/2}` of the jumps, and
sieve or large-sieve information on intervals `[x, x + x^{1−α}]` (`0 < α ≤ 1`) all live at
shifts `h = h(y) → 0` and leave no trace on `(A, F_M)`: an atom of mass `η` means that for
each fixed `ε` the race stays in a band of width `ε` on a set of density `≥ η`, and the
relevant shifts are `h ≍ ε²/log(1/ε)`, fixed. This is why ideas (b) and (f) of the brief
do not close the argument (see `PROGRESS.md`).

3.5(a),(b), Propositions 2.4, 4.3, 4.4, 6.1, 6.2; under additional algebraic hypotheses on the
ordinates: Theorems 3.1 (TS), 3.4 (FD), 3.5(c) (a tame direction with a finite fibre);

conditional reduction: Corollary 9.4.

**Not proved:** `ν_{1/2}({0}) = 0` under GRH alone (which would imply the existence of the
logarithmic density of `{x : π(x;4,3) > π(x;4,1)}`; the converse implication is not
claimed). The state of the question after this note:

1. `ν_{1/2}({0}) = 0` is equivalent to the continuity at `m = 0` of the monotone function
   `m ↦ \underline d{x : Σ_{p≤x} χ*(p)p^m > 0}` (Theorem 2.1), and it holds for all but
   countably many weights near `m = 0`;
2. it holds under FD, a hypothesis weaker than those in the literature (§3.3), which however
   cannot be verified by any finite computation and is not known to hold;
3. the defect is at most `0.0891` (`q = 4`), `0.0653` (`q = 3`) (Theorem 5.4);
4. an atom would have to live on a set with empty interior (Proposition 4.3), invariant under
   every tame direction (Corollary 3.6), and would force the balance of Proposition 6.2;
6. integrality of multiplicities is not enough either: Theorem 9.6 gives positive integer
   multiplicities (`≪ (log n)^{5/2}`; `σ² < 0.5` at `M = ½` after rescaling) with an atom at
   `0`. Only the refined Question P″ (the local counting law `N(T+1) − N(T) ≪ log T` together
   with `σ² < 2`) is left in this direction (Remarks 9.8, 9.9).
5. no argument that uses only a compact connected hull with a dense flow, the positivity of
   the weights, the identities between weights (resolvent, lead inheritance,
   transversality, analyticity in `M`), time reversal, and the size of `σ_M²` can work:
   Proposition 7.3 gives a model with all these features and an atom at `0` at the classical
   weight `M = 1/2` (Remark 7.4). A proof must use something the model lacks: integrality of
   the multiplicities, arithmetic information (on the ordinates, or on the dense orbit
   beyond Proposition 4.3), or possibly the non-cyclic hull or the growth of `N(T)`
   (the last two are expected, but not shown, to be inessential).

Also not proved: that ties, or near-ties `|π(x;4,3) − π(x;4,1)| ≤ K`, have logarithmic
density zero under GRH alone (Remark 6.4); the converse "density exists ⇒ no atom".

## 9. Integrality and parity: the lever the obstruction models lack

The models of §7 have real weights. The true multiplicities `m_γ` are positive **integers**,
while the constant term of the driving distribution `w = (D+M)F_M/(2M) = ½ + Σ_{g∈±Γ} m_g e_g`
is `½` — the half-integer that is Chebyshev's bias (it comes from the squares of primes). This
section shows, in a model class, that this mismatch alone rules out atoms at `0`, whereas real
weights in the same class allow them. Nothing here uses number theory.

**Setting (nested comb classes).** Let `1 = N_0 | N_1 | N_2 | …` be integers with
`N_{k+1} ≥ 2N_k`, `h_k := 2π/N_k`, and `b_0, b_1, … ∈ ℝ` with `Σ_k |b_k|/N_k < ∞`. Put
`m_n := Σ_{k : N_k | n} b_k` (`n ≥ 1`), `B(J) := Σ_{k≤J} b_k` (so `m_n = B(J)` when `N_J | n`,
`N_{J+1} ∤ n`), and for `M > 0`

    F(t) := 1 + Re Σ_{n≥1} m_n (4M/(M+in)) e^{int}    on 𝕋 = ℝ/2πℤ   (an L² sum).

This is the cyclic model of §7 with weights constant on the "shells" `{n : N_J ∥ n}`. Let
`ρ_k(t) := h_k e^{−M s}/(1 − e^{−Mh_k}) − 1/M`, `s := t mod h_k ∈ [0,h_k)` (an exponential
sawtooth of period `h_k`, mean `0`, decreasing on each period, `|ρ_k| ≤ h_k`).

**Lemma 9.0.** `F = 1 + 2M Σ_k b_k ρ_k`, the series converging uniformly on `𝕋`; in particular
`F` is a bounded Borel function defined everywhere.

*Proof.* Let `C_k := Σ_{n∈N_kℤ} e^{int} = h_k Σ_{j=0}^{N_k−1} δ_{jh_k}` (Poisson summation, with
`\hat f(n) = (1/2π)∫f e^{−int}`). On `(0,h_k)`, `(D+M)(h_k e^{−Ms}/(1−e^{−Mh_k})) = 0`, the jump at
`s = 0` is `h_k`, and the mean of `ρ_k` is `0`; hence `(D+M)ρ_k = C_k − 1`, i.e.
`\hat ρ_k(n) = 1_{N_k|n}/(M+in)` for `n ≠ 0` and `\hat ρ_k(0) = 0`. Since
`0 < h/(1−e^{−x}) − h/x < h` and `−h/2 ≤ h/(e^x−1) − h/x < 0` for `x = Mh > 0`, `|ρ_k| ≤ h_k`.
So `Σ |b_k|·sup|ρ_k| ≤ 2πΣ|b_k|/N_k < ∞`, and the Fourier coefficients of the uniform limit are
`2MΣ_k b_k 1_{N_k||n|}/(M+in) = 2M m_{|n|}/(M+in)` (`n ≠ 0`) and `1` (`n = 0`). ∎

(Checked numerically against the Fourier series: `parity_nested_check.py`, part (1).)

**Theorem 9.1 (parity rigidity; no hypothesis).** In this setting assume `b_k ∈ ℤ` for all
`k`. Then `λ{t : F(t) = c} = 0` for every real `c ∉ 1 + 2ℤ`. In particular the law of `F` has
no atom at `0`.

*Proof.* Suppose `S := {F = c}` has positive measure. The level-`J` cells
`I = [ah_J, (a+1)h_J)` form nested partitions generating the Borel σ-algebra, so by the
martingale convergence theorem (Lévy's 0–1 law) for a.e. `t_* ∈ S` the level-`J` cell
`I_J(t_*)` satisfies `|S ∩ I_J(t_*)| ≥ (1−δ)|I_J(t_*)|` for all large `J`; fix `δ = 1/8` and such
`t_*`, and let `J` be large and `I := I_J(t_*)`.

*Coarse and fine parts.* For `k ≤ J` the atoms of `C_k` are at multiples of `h_k`, which are
multiples of `h_J`, so none lies in the interior of `I`; there `ρ_k(t) = A_k e^{−Mt} − 1/M` with
`A_k := h_k e^{Mx_k}/(1−e^{−Mh_k})`, `x_k` the left end of the level-`k` cell containing `I`. Hence,
on `int(I)`,

    F(t) = 1 − 2B(J) + 2Mα e^{−Mt} + 2MΦ(t),     α := Σ_{k≤J} b_k A_k,     Φ := Σ_{k>J} b_k ρ_k .

Each `ρ_k` with `k > J` has period `h_k`, which divides `h' := h_{J+1}`, so `Φ(t + h') = Φ(t)`; and
`|Φ| ≤ ε_J := Σ_{k>J} |b_k| h_k → 0` as `J → ∞`.

*Killing the exponential.* `I` is the union of `r := N_{J+1}/N_J ≥ 2` consecutive sub-cells of
length `h'`. The set `E := {t ∈ I : t ∈ S, t + h' ∈ S ∩ I}` has measure
`≥ (r−1)h' − 2δrh' > 0` (as `δ = 1/8` and `r ≥ 2`). For `t ∈ E` (both points interior to `I`
after discarding a null set), `F(t) = F(t+h') = c` and `Φ(t) = Φ(t+h')` give
`2Mα(e^{−Mt} − e^{−M(t+h')}) = 0`, hence `α = 0`.

*Parity.* So `F = 1 − 2B(J) + 2MΦ` on `int(I)`, and at any `t ∈ S ∩ int(I)`:
`|c − 1 + 2B(J)| ≤ 2Mε_J`. Here `B(J) ∈ ℤ`, so `dist(c − 1, 2ℤ) ≤ 2Mε_J`. Letting `J → ∞`:
`c − 1 ∈ 2ℤ`. ∎

Remarks. (i) No sign condition on the `b_k` or the `m_n` is needed, and multiplicities may grow
(e.g. `m_n ≪ n^{1−ε}` along the chain is allowed by `Σ|b_k|/N_k < ∞`). (ii) The proof gives more
(observed by the referee of this section): `|c − 1 + 2B(J)| ≤ 2Mε_J` for **all** `J ≥ J_0(t_*)`,
so `B(J) → (1−c)/2`. Hence, for real `b_k`, at most one level `c = 1 − 2Σb_k` can carry positive
measure; for integer `b_k`, a level set of positive measure forces `b_k = 0` for `k > J_0`, so
**if infinitely many `b_k ≠ 0` the law of `F` has no atoms at all**. Atoms at odd integer levels
occur in finite combs for special `M` (e.g. `N = (1,4)`, `b = (1,−1)`, `M ≈ 0.5610998523`:
`F ≡ 1` on `[π/2, π)`, checked to `8·10^{−11}` by the referee), and trivially for `F ≡ 1`.
(iii) The proof
uses exactly three things: an exact local form "odd integer + `αe^{−Mt}`" of the coarse part,
exact periodicity of the fine part at the next scale, and integrality of the partial sums
`B(J)` against the constant `½` of `w`. (iv) *Relation to Helson's theorem.* If `w` is a finite
measure (equivalently `F ∈ BV`), Helson's theorem on measures with integer Fourier–Stieltjes
coefficients (H. Helson, Proc. Amer. Math. Soc. 4 (1953); W. Rudin, *Fourier analysis on
groups*, ch. 3) makes the coefficients `m_n` eventually periodic plus a finite part. Then `F` is
real-analytic off finitely many points, and a level set of positive measure forces `c ∈ 1+2ℤ`.
So the cyclic case of Question P holds for `F ∈ BV`. For general `G`, Cohen's idempotent theorem
should give the measure case similarly; not written out. In the nested class, integer `b_k`
with `Σ|b_k| < ∞` are finitely many, so the real content of Theorem 9.1 is the regime
`Σ|b_k| = ∞`, where `w` is not a measure. The true `w` is not a measure either.

**Example 9.2 (real weights: integrality cannot be dropped).** Take `N = (1, 3)`, any `M > 0`,

    A_0 := 2π/(1 − e^{−2πM}),   A_1 := (2π/3) e^{4πM/3}/(1 − e^{−2πM/3}),   b_0 := A_1/(2(A_1 − A_0)),   b_1 := ½ − b_0 .

Then `F = 0` on the whole arc `(4π/3, 2π)`, so the law of `F` has an atom at `0` of mass
`≥ 1/3`; the weights `m_n = b_0` (`3 ∤ n`), `m_n = ½` (`3 | n`) are positive (`A_1 > A_0`). At the
classical weight `M = ½`: `m_n = 0.66722…` or `½`, and `σ² = Σ m_n 8M²/(M²+n²) ≈ 1.842 < 2`.

*Proof.* On the level-1 cell `I = [4π/3, 2π)`: `x_0 = 0`, `x_1 = 4π/3`, so by the proof of
Theorem 9.1 `F = 1 − 2(b_0+b_1) + 2M(b_0A_0 + b_1A_1)e^{−Mt}` on `int(I)`, and both brackets
vanish by the choice of `b_0, b_1`. `A_1 > A_0`: with `u := 2πM/3`,
`A_1/A_0 = (1/3)e^{2u}(1 − e^{−3u})/(1 − e^{−u}) = (1 + e^u + e^{2u})/3 > 1` for `M > 0`.
(`parity_nested_check.py`, part (2), confirms `|F| ≤ 2·10^{−15}` on the arc; `σ² = 1.842045085…`
at `M = ½` in closed form, via `coth`.) ∎

So within one and the same class: **real nonnegative weights → an atom at `0` (even with
`σ² < 2` at `M = ½`); integer weights → no atom at `0`, at any `M`.** In the language of
Remark 7.4, this is feature (α) at work. A numerical integer example (`N_k = 3^k`,
`b_k ∈ {−1,0,1}`, part (3) of the script) shows `λ{|F| < ε}` decreasing linearly in `ε`.

**Remark 9.3 (finite combs in higher dimension; argument sketched, not a theorem).** On `𝕋^d`
with a flow of irrational direction `v`, the analogue of `C_k` is the Haar measure `μ_K` of the
closed codimension-one subgroup `K := ker e_{g_K}` (`g_K ∈ ℤ^d`); its Fourier transform is the
indicator of the cyclic subgroup `ℤg_K` of the dual, and `γ_K := g_K·v ≠ 0`.
If `w = ½ + Σ_{K∈𝒦} n_K(μ_K − 1)` with `𝒦` finite and `n_K ∈ ℤ`, the resolvent along the flow is
`F = 1 − 2Σn_K + Σ_K c_K(θ) e^{−Mψ_K(θ)/γ_K}`, with `ψ_K` the linear coordinate transverse to `K`,
`γ_K` its speed, and `c_K` locally constant off finitely many hypersurfaces. On a small ball
this is a finite exponential sum in `θ`, real-analytic; vanishing on a subset of positive
measure forces it to vanish identically, and the odd constant `1 − 2Σn_K` is linearly
independent of the nonconstant exponentials (terms with parallel `g_K` have the same linear
form and are grouped; only the constant term matters). So again no atom at `0`; here
`ψ_K = g_K·θ`. (Infinite families with
non-nested directions are **not** covered: the fine parts then have no common period.)

**Question P (integrality rigidity; open).** Let `G` be a countable subgroup of `ℝ`,
`Γ ⊂ G ∩ (0,∞)`, `m_γ ∈ {1, 2, 3, …}` with `Σ m_γ²/γ² < ∞` (one may add `m_γ ≪ log(γ+2)` and
`#Γ ∩ [T, T+1] ≪ log(T+2)`), and `M > 0`. Is it true that

    F = 1 + Re Σ_{γ∈Γ} m_γ (4M/(M+iγ)) e_γ   satisfies   λ_{Ĝ}{F = c} = 0   for every c ∉ 1 + 2ℤ ?

**Corollary 9.4 (the reduction).** Assume GRH(`χ`) and NCZ(`χ`) (NCZ is a theorem for
`q = 3, 4`). If Question P has a positive answer **for the group `G` generated by the ordinates
of `L(s,χ)`**, then for every weight `m > −1/2` the limiting distribution `ν_M` has no atom at
`0`; hence the logarithmic densities of `{x : D_m(x) > 0}` exist, in particular that of
`{x : π(x;4,3) > π(x;4,1)}` under GRH(`χ_{−4}`) alone. (If `L(1/2,χ) = 0` with order `n_0`, the
constant term of `F_M` becomes `1 + 2n_0` and the constant of `w` becomes `n_0 + ½`, still not an
integer; this case is conditional on the extension of (Q1) sketched in `GRH-ONLY.md` Remark 1.5,
which is not written out.)

*Proof.* Under GRH + NCZ, `ν_M` is the law of `F_M`, which is of the form in Question P with
`G` the group generated by the ordinates, `Γ` the distinct positive ordinates and `m_γ` their
multiplicities (`GRH-ONLY.md` Theorem 1.3; (Q3) for the side conditions); `0 ∉ 1 + 2ℤ`. Then
(1.1) and (2.3), valid for every `M > 0`. ∎

**What this does and does not achieve** (written before Theorem 9.6; see Remark 9.8, which
shows that Question P is **false** as stated). Corollary 9.4 gives a *sufficient* condition for
the existence of the density under GRH alone with no number theory in it, about
integer-weighted almost periodic functions on duals of countable subgroups of `ℝ` (with the
flow). Theorem 9.1 proves it in a model class, Remark 9.3 sketches another. Example 9.2 and Propositions 7.2–7.3 show that it fails without
integrality. **It is not proved here, and we have no evidence for it beyond these classes.**
The true group `G` is unknown: it could be far from nested or finite-comb structure (for
instance a rank-one solenoid if all ordinates are commensurable), and Question P could fail
for some exotic `G` while still holding for the true one. The parity viewpoint also suggests that for an *unbiased* race (mean `0`, e.g. two
quadratic non-residues modulo `8`) the constant of `w` is an integer and parity gives no
protection at level `0`. **Heuristic, not a theorem.**

**Remark 9.5 (why nestedness is used, and what happens in general).** For arbitrary integer
`m_n`, Möbius inversion `b_q := Σ_{d|q} μ(q/d) m_d ∈ ℤ` gives `m_n = Σ_{q|n} b_q` and, formally,
`F = 1 + 2M Σ_q b_q ρ_q` (now only conditionally convergent: `Σ|b_q|/q` may diverge). On a
Farey cell `I` of order `Q` the same computation gives

    F = (1 − 2B(Q)) + 2Mα_I e^{−Mt} + (fine part),     B(Q) = Σ_{q≤Q} b_q = Σ_{d≤Q} m_d·𝑀(⌊Q/d⌋) ∈ ℤ

(`𝑀` the Mertens function), with the same odd constant. But the fine part
`2MΣ_{q>Q} b_q ρ_q` now has no period shorter than `|I|`: the periods `2π/q` of the medium
combs are incommensurable at that scale. So `α_I` cannot be eliminated. The exponential
`(1−2B) + 2Mα_I e^{−Mt}` then vanishes at a point of `I`, is small on the tiny cell, and
**absorbs the parity defect**. To first order in `M/q`,
`F(t) ≈ 1 − 4πM Σ_q (b_q/q)((qt/2π))`, `((x)) = {x} − ½`: a Davenport series with integer
coefficients. Its values at irrational `t` are not constrained by integrality, since the
sawtooth values `((qt/2π))` are irrational. Whether such a series can vanish on a set of
positive measure is the first-order version of the cyclic case of Question P (without the sign
condition `m ≥ 1`); it was not settled at that point. Theorem 9.6 below settles the cyclic case
of Question P negatively. Davenport series: H. Davenport, Quart. J. Math. 8 (1937);
S. Jaffard, Ann. Inst. Fourier 54 (2004).
The absorption mechanism shows that the method of Theorem 9.1 stops at nested classes, and
it leaves open the possibility that Question P fails already on the circle.

**Theorem 9.6 (integrality does not exclude atoms; no hypothesis).** For every `M > 0` there
are integers `m_n ≥ 1` (`n ≥ 1`) with `m_n ≪_M (log(n+2))^{5/2}` such that

    F = 1 + Re Σ_{n≥1} m_n (4M/(M+in)) e^{int}

vanishes a.e. on a set of measure `> 0.1·2π`. So the law of `F` has an atom at `0`, and
**Question P has a negative answer for `G = ℤ`** in the generality stated: positive integer
multiplicities with `Σ m_n²/n² < ∞`, one frequency per unit interval.

*Addendum (σ² and the classical weight; observed by the referee of this theorem).* The
construction controls `σ_M² = Σ m_n 8M²/(M²+n²) = 2(Ψ(0) − 1)` exactly: each step adds
`2φ_{e,j}(0) = 2e^Ψ_j(0) b(0)/(ρ_j m_0(b))`. For small `M` (e.g. `M = 0.02`, with `c(x) = e^{−1/(1−4x²)}`)
the total stays `< 0.5`. The referee bounds it by `0.43` and finds `0.364` after three steps by
direct summation. Rescaling `t ↦ t/Ω`, `Ω = 1/(2M)`, turns the model at weight `M` on the
frequencies `n` into the model at the **classical weight `½`** on the frequencies `Ωn`, with
the same multiplicities, `σ²` and atom. So there is an integer-multiplicity counterexample at
`M = ½` with spectrum `25ℕ` and `σ² < 0.5`. (On the full spectrum `ℕ` at `M = ½`, any
`m_n ≥ 1` gives `σ² ≥ 2π coth(π/2) − 4 ≈ 2.85`.)

*Construction.* All functions below are real and of the form `(1 − D/M)Ψ`, `Ψ` even, which is
exactly the model class: for `F = (1 − D/M)Ψ`, `F̂(n) = (1 − in/M)Ψ̂(n)`, so
`m_n := F̂(n)(M+in)/(2M) = (M²+n²)Ψ̂(n)/(2M²)` is **real**. Conversely every real sequence `m`
with `Σm_n²/n² < ∞` arises (`Ψ̂(±n) = 2M²m_n/(M²+n²)`).

Parameters: `K = 4`, `N_j = K^j` (`j ≥ 0`), bands `B_j = [N_j, N_{j+1}) ∩ ℕ`, `N'_j := 3N_{j+1}`,
`ρ_j := ρ_0 (j+1)^{−5/4}` with `ρ_0 := 0.05`. Fix `c ∈ C_c^∞((−½,½))`, `c ≥ 0`, `c ≢ 0`, put
`b := c * c̃` (`c̃(x) = c(−x)`). Then `b ∈ C_c^∞((−1,1))`, `b ≥ 0`, `b` is even, and its Fourier
transform `\hat b = |\hat c|² ≥ 0` is a Schwartz function. Let `w_j := 2πρ_j/N'_j`,

    β_j(t) := Σ_{ℓ∈ℤ} b(2(t − 2πℓ/N'_j)/w_j),

which is even, smooth, `≥ 0`, `2π/N'_j`-periodic, and supported on a set `E_j` of measure
`N'_j w_j = 2πρ_j`. Its Fourier coefficients vanish off `N'_jℤ` and
`\hat β_j(kN'_j)/\hat β_j(0) = \hat b(πkρ_j)/\hat b(0) ∈ [0,1]`.

*Base.* `F_0` is Example 9.2: real weights `m^{(base)}_n ∈ {b_0, ½}`, `b_0 > 0`, and `F_0 = 0` on
`J := (4π/3, 2π)`.

*Step `j` (for `j = 0, 1, 2, …`).* Let `F_j` be given, in the model class, with integer
multiplicities on `[1, N_j)`, and `F_j = 0` a.e. on `S_j := J ∖ ∪_{i<j} E_i`. For `n ∈ B_j` let
`m^{cur}_n` be its (real) multiplicity, `δm_n := ⌈m^{cur}_n⌉ − m^{cur}_n ∈ [0,1)` (**rounding up**),
and let `e^Ψ_j := Σ_{n∈B_j} (2M²δm_n/(M²+n²))·2cos(nt)`, a trigonometric polynomial with
nonnegative coefficients and spectrum in `±B_j`. Put

    φ_{e,j} := e^Ψ_j · β_j / \hat β_j(0),      φ_j := (1 − D/M) φ_{e,j},      F_{j+1} := F_j + φ_j .

*Lemma 9.7 (one step).* (i) `φ_j` is supported in `E_j`. Hence `F_{j+1} = F_j` off `E_j`, and
`F_{j+1} = 0` a.e. on `S_{j+1}`. (ii) `\hat φ_j(n) = (1 − in/M)\hat e^Ψ_j(n)` for `|n| < N_{j+1}`. That is, the multiplicities of
`F_{j+1}` below `N_{j+1}` are those of `F_j` plus `δm`: integers on `[1, N_{j+1})`, unchanged on
`[1, N_j)`. (iii) For `|n| ≥ N_{j+1}` the
multiplicity change `m^{(j)}_n := (M²+n²)\hat φ_{e,j}(n)/(2M²)` satisfies
`0 ≤ m^{(j)}_n ≤ 4(1 + 9K²(k+1)²)·\hat b(πkρ_j)/\hat b(0)`, where `k ≥ 1` is the integer with
`|n − kN'_j| < N_{j+1}` (and `m^{(j)}_n = 0` if there is none). (iv)
`‖φ_j‖_2 ≤ C_c M(M+1) K N_j^{−1/2} ρ_j^{−3/2}` with `C_c` depending on `c` only
(`‖f‖_2² := (1/2π)∫|f|²`).

*Proof.* (i) `β_j` vanishes off `E_j`, and `D` does not enlarge supports. (ii) `\hat φ_{e,j}(n) =
Σ_k \hat e^Ψ_j(n − kN'_j)\hat β_j(kN'_j)/\hat β_j(0)`. For `|n| < N_{j+1}` and `k ≠ 0`,
`|n − kN'_j| > 2N_{j+1}`, outside the spectrum of `e^Ψ_j`; so `\hat φ_{e,j}(n) = \hat e^Ψ_j(n)`,
which is `0` for `|n| < N_j`. Multiply by `(1 − in/M)` and use the formula for `m_n`.
(iii) For `|n| ≥ N_{j+1}` at most one `k ≥ 1` (and its negative) contributes, with
`0 ≤ \hat e^Ψ_j(·) ≤ 2M²/(M²+N_j²)` and `0 ≤ \hat β_j(kN')/\hat β_j(0) = \hat b(πkρ_j)/\hat b(0)`. Also
`M² + n² ≤ M² + (k+1)²N'^2_j ≤ (1 + 9K²(k+1)²)(M² + N_j²)`. (iv) The spectra of `|e^Ψ_j|²`,
`|De^Ψ_j|²` lie in `(−2N_{j+1}, 2N_{j+1})`, and those of `β_j²`, `(Dβ_j)²` lie in `N'_jℤ` with
`N'_j > 2N_{j+1}`. So for these products the mean of the product is the product of the means,
and

    ‖φ_{e,j}‖ = ‖e^Ψ_j‖ ‖β_j‖/\hat β_j(0),   ‖Dφ_{e,j}‖ ≤ (‖De^Ψ_j‖ ‖β_j‖ + ‖e^Ψ_j‖ ‖Dβ_j‖)/\hat β_j(0).

Here `\hat β_j(0) = ρ_j·m_0(b)`, `‖β_j‖ = (ρ_j m_0(b²))^{1/2}`, `‖Dβ_j‖ = (ρ_j m_0(b'²))^{1/2}·2/w_j`,
with `m_0(f) := ½∫_{−1}^1 f`. Moreover
`‖e^Ψ_j‖² ≤ 2Σ_{n≥N_j}4M⁴/(M²+n²)² ≤ 11M⁴N_j^{−3}` and `‖De^Ψ_j‖² ≤ 2Σ_{n≥N_j}4M⁴n²/(M²+n²)² ≤ 16M⁴N_j^{−1}`.
Combining, with `2/w_j = N'_j/(πρ_j) = 3KN_j/(πρ_j)`:
`‖φ_j‖ ≤ ‖φ_{e,j}‖ + ‖Dφ_{e,j}‖/M ≤ C_c M²(N_j^{−3/2}ρ_j^{−1/2} + M^{−1}N_j^{−1/2}ρ_j^{−1/2} + M^{−1}KN_j^{−1/2}ρ_j^{−3/2})
≤ C_c M(M+1) K N_j^{−1/2}ρ_j^{−3/2}`. (Numerically, at `M = ½`, the ratio of `‖φ_j‖` to
`KN_j^{−1/2}ρ_j^{−3/2}` stays between `1.3` and `1.45` for `j ≤ 6`, according to the referee.) ∎

*Proof of Theorem 9.6.* By Lemma 9.7(iv), `Σ_j ‖φ_j‖ ≤ C Σ_j K^{1−j/2}(j+1)^{15/8} < ∞`, so
`F_∞ := F_0 + Σ_j φ_j` converges in `L²`.

(a) *Vanishing.* Each partial sum vanishes a.e. on `S_∞ := J ∖ ∪_j E_j`, so `F_∞` does too.
And `|S_∞| ≥ |J| − Σ_j 2πρ_j ≥ 2π(1/3 − 0.05·ζ(5/4)) > 2π·0.1`, since `ζ(5/4) < 4.6`.

(b) *Integer multiplicities.* For `n ∈ B_j`, `\hat φ_i(n) = 0` for all `i > j`: their low
coefficients are those of `e^Ψ_i`, which live in `B_i`. So the multiplicity of `F_∞` at `n` is
that of `F_{j+1}`, i.e. `⌈m^{cur}_n⌉ ∈ ℤ`. It is real by the model-class remark, and the
constant term is `1`, since `\hat φ_j(0) = 0`.

(c) *Positivity and growth.* `m^{cur}_n = m^{(base)}_n + Σ_{i<j} m^{(i)}_n ≥ m^{(base)}_n > 0` by
Lemma 9.7(iii), so `m_n = ⌈m^{cur}_n⌉ ≥ 1`. For the upper bound, for each `i` with
`N_{i+1} ≤ n` let `k_i ≈ n/N'_i`. Since `\hat b` decays faster than any power,
`(1 + 9K²(k+1)²)\hat b(πkρ)/\hat b(0) ≤ C_c K² ρ^{−2}·min(1, (kρ)^{−3})`. The `k_i` grow
geometrically as `i` decreases, so the sum over `i` is dominated by the `O(1 + log(1/ρ_i))`
indices with `k_iρ_i ≤ 1`. Writing `(k+1)² ≤ 4(kρ_i)²ρ_i^{−2}`, the contributions of these indices
are `≤ C_cK²ρ_i^{−2}(k_iρ_i)²`. Since `k_i` grows geometrically as `i` decreases, they sum
geometrically to `≤ C_cK²ρ_{i_0}^{−2}`, where `i_0 ≤ log_4 n` is the largest index involved.
Hence `m_n ≤ 1 + max m^{(base)} + C_cK²ρ_0^{−2}(log_4 n + 1)^{5/2} ≪ (log(n+2))^{5/2}`. This
rate is attained by the construction, with a huge implied constant: multiplicities reach
`46` at `n = 13` and `≈ 3·10^5` below `n = 4^7` (referee's computation). ∎

**Remark 9.6a (prior art for the technique).** Multiplying a low-frequency correction by a
fine periodic comb of bumps, which fixes the low Fourier coefficients exactly while living
on a set of small measure, is the classical Menshov-type correction. See Olevskii's survey
of modifications of functions and Fourier series, and the representation and correction
theorems of Kozma–Olevskii; titles were not re-checked. We know of no published statement
of Theorem 9.6 itself. For integer coefficients and plain `L²` series the analogous question
is trivial: Parseval forces a trigonometric polynomial. The rigidity theorems for integer
coefficients (Fatou, Szegő, Pólya–Carlson, Duffin–Schaeffer, Helson) do not apply, because
here the integers are weighted by `4M/(M+in)` and are unbounded.

**Remark 9.8 (what Theorem 9.6 kills, and what is left).** Theorem 9.6 with Example 9.2,
Proposition 7.3 and Theorem 9.1 gives a clean picture of the model world.

- Integer multiplicities exclude atoms at `0` when the multiplicities are constant on the
  shells of one divisor chain (Theorem 9.1).
- They do **not** exclude them in general (Theorem 9.6). The mechanism is the one of
  Remark 9.5: corrections at many incommensurable scales, here at the periods
  `2π/N'_j`, absorb the parity defect.

So the integrality and parity lever of this section **cannot by itself** prove that the
density exists. Question P is false as stated. The counterexample meets two of the conditions satisfied by the true zeros:
`#Γ ∩ [T,T+1] ≤ 1`, and `σ² < 2` at the classical weight after rescaling (Addendum). It
violates the local counting law `Σ_{γ∈[T,T+1]} m_γ = N(T+1) − N(T) ≪ log T`: its local mass
is `≍ (log T)^{5/2}`, and the construction needs `≫ (log T)^2` (Remark 9.9). It also has a
cyclic hull.

The refined question is **Question P″** (Remark 9.9). It adds the local counting law and
`σ² < 2`, and it is open. Spreading a large multiplicity over a cluster of nearby frequencies
does **not** obviously help: the obstruction of Remark 9.9 is on mass per unit frequency. A
negative answer to P″ on one group would not settle the true problem. Only a counterexample
on a hull that the available information about the true ordinates cannot exclude would show
that no argument from spectral data can work.

**Remark 9.9 (the counting law is what Theorem 9.6 cannot respect).** The true multiplicities
satisfy the *local* bound `Σ_{γ∈[T,T+1]} m_γ = N(T+1) − N(T) ≤ A_0 log(T+3)` ((Q3) of
`GRH-ONLY.md`), not just `m_γ ≪ log γ`. In the construction of Theorem 9.6 the local mass near
the frequency `n` is `m_n ≪ (log n)^{5/2}`. Within constructions of that type, gap corrections
at each scale, the following **sketch** indicates why growth `≫ (log n)^2` is needed:

- the rounding error at a frequency `n ∈ B_j` is `δm ≍ 1`, i.e. `≍ n^{−2}` in `Ψ`;
- it must be cancelled by a function supported on intervals of total length `2πρ_j` (summable
  in `j`, else the zero set disappears) with no Fourier coefficients below `N_{j+1}`;
- by Bernstein's inequality, applied to `ξ²\hat f(ξ)`, an entire function of exponential type
  `ℓ/2` with second derivative `2\hat f(0)` at `0`, a function supported in an interval of length
  `ℓ` and centred has `sup_ξ ξ²|\hat f(ξ)| ≥ 8|\hat f(0)|/ℓ²`. (Gaps in using this: the correction
  lives on many intervals; multiplicities are samples at integers, not a sup over `ℝ`; and the
  location of the sup is not controlled.) So such a function puts `Ψ`-mass `≍ n^{−2}` out
  to frequencies `≍ N_{j+1}/ρ_j`;
- since multiplicities are `(M² + ξ²)/(2M²)` times `Ψ`-mass, this gives multiplicities
  `≍ (N_{j+1}/(ρ_j n))² ≳ ρ_j^{−2}`.

Over `≍ log n` scales with `Σρ_j < ∞`, this forces `m_n ≳ (log n)^2` somewhere near every
height. Subdividing the spectrum more finely does not help, because the bound is on mass per
unit frequency. This is a sketch about one class of constructions, **not** a lower bound for
all integer models.

**Question P″ (spectral form with a free counting constant; answered negatively by Corollary 9.23).** Let `G ⊂ ℝ` be a countable subgroup,
`Γ ⊂ G ∩ (0,∞)`, `m_γ ∈ {1,2,…}` with `Σ_{γ∈[T,T+1]} m_γ ≤ A log(T+3)` for all `T ≥ 0`, and
`M > 0` with `σ_M² := Σ_γ m_γ·8M²/(M²+γ²) < 2`. Must `F = 1 + Re Σ m_γ (4M/(M+iγ)) e_γ` satisfy
`λ_{Ĝ}{F = 0} = 0`?

A positive answer, even just for the group generated by the ordinates of `L(s,χ_{−4})` at
`M = ½` (where `σ²_{1/2}(χ_{−4}) = 0.1556 < 2`; here `σ²` is linear in the `m_γ`), gives, by the
argument of Corollary 9.4 at `M = ½`, the density of `{π(x;4,3) > π(x;4,1)}` under GRH alone. A
negative answer, with some `Γ` obeying the same counting law as the true zeros, would show
that **no** argument from spectral data can work. Here spectral data means the hull,
positivity and integrality of multiplicities, the counting law, `σ² < 2`, the identities
between weights, and time reversal. Any proof would then have to use the explicit formula
beyond Proposition 4.3. Real weights satisfy the counting law and do have atoms
(Proposition 7.3), so in Question P″ integrality and the counting law must work together.
**Status:** Theorem 9.22 and Corollary 9.23 give a negative answer (`G = ℤ`, and `G = Ωℤ` at
`M = ½`), with `m_n ≪ (log n)^θ` but with an astronomically large constant `A ≈ 10^{14}`. What
survives is the form with the fixed constant `A_0` of the true zeros (Conjecture U), and the
bounded-multiplicity case.

**Proposition 9.10 (cyclic case, eventually periodic multiplicities; elementary).** Let `m_n ∈ ℤ`
(`n ≥ 1`) with `Σ m_n²/n² < ∞`, `M > 0`, and let `F` be the cyclic model
`1 + Re Σ m_n (4M/(M+in)) e^{int}`. If `(m_n)` is eventually periodic, then `λ{F = c} = 0` for every
`c ∉ 1 + 2ℤ`. Equivalently (see the proof), this holds whenever `g(z) := Σ_{n≥1} m_n zⁿ` is
rational. Hence, by the Pólya–Carlson theorem (a power series with integer coefficients and
radius of convergence `1` is rational or has the unit circle as natural boundary), an atom at a
level `c ∉ 1+2ℤ` forces `(m_n)` not to be eventually periodic, i.e. forces `g` to have the circle
as natural boundary. This is the case for the counterexample of Theorem 9.6, whose
multiplicities are unbounded.

*Proof.* `|m_n| ≤ Cn`, so `g` has radius of convergence `≥ 1`. If `g` is rational, Fatou's lemma
writes it in lowest terms as `R/Q` with `R, Q ∈ ℤ[z]` and `Q(0) = 1`. The reciprocal roots `α` of
`Q` are nonzero algebraic integers, closed under conjugation, with `|α| ≤ 1` because the radius
is `≥ 1` and the fraction is reduced. By Kronecker's theorem they are roots of unity, so `Q` is
`±` a product of cyclotomic polynomials. A pole of order `≥ 2` would force `|m_n| ≫ n` on a residue
class of positive density, contradicting `Σ m_n²/n² < ∞`. So the poles are simple and `(m_n)` is
eventually periodic; the converse is clear. Write `g = E + g_0` with `E ∈ ℤ[z]`, `E(0) = 0`, and
`g_0 = R_0/Q`, `deg R_0 ≤ deg Q`, `g_0(0) = 0`. (Divide `R` by `Q` with quotient `A`; take `E = A − A(0)`
and `R_0 = R_1 + A(0)Q`, where `R_1` is the remainder.) Then `g_0(∞)` is the coefficient of
`z^{deg Q}` in `R_0` divided by `lead Q = ±1`, an integer.

Let `Z` be the finite set of poles of `g`, a set of roots of unity closed under conjugation. On
an open arc `I ⊂ 𝕋 ∖ Z`, the distribution `Σ_{n≥1} m_n e^{±int}` equals `g(e^{±it})`: its Abel means
converge in `𝒟′` for polynomially bounded coefficients, and locally uniformly off the poles. So
`w = (D+M)F/(2M) = ½ + h(e^{it})` on `I`, with `h(z) := g(z) + g(1/z)` rational. Then `F` is
real-analytic on `I`: an analytic particular solution of `(D+M)F = 2Mw` plus `Ce^{−Mt}`.

If `λ{F = c} > 0`, some arc `I` of `𝕋 ∖ Z` meets `{F = c}` in positive measure, so `F ≡ c` there.
Then `w = c/2` on `I`, so `h ≡ κ := (c−1)/2` as rational functions. Near `z = 0`, `g(z) → 0` and
`g_0(1/z) → g_0(∞)`, while `E(1/z)` has a pole unless `E = 0`. So `κ = g_0(∞) ∈ ℤ`, i.e.
`c ∈ 1 + 2ℤ`. ∎

(Status, from the referee of this proposition: this is an elementary, folklore-level extension
of Remark (iv) after Theorem 9.1 beyond the measure case. For instance `m_n = 1_{n≡1 (3)}` gives a
`w` that is not a measure, `w = ½ + sin(t/2)/sin(3t/2)` off the cube roots of unity. The same
proof works for polynomially bounded `m_n`. It does **not** by itself make Remark 7.5 rigorous:
that remark's missing step is from "`F ≡ c` on an arc" to "`g` continues across the arc". That
step is supplied in Theorem 9.16. The referee's exhaustive search over 915
eventually periodic sequences with constant `h` found only integer `κ`.)

**Remark 9.11 (Question P″ in the cyclic case; what is and is not known).** In the cyclic case
Question P″ asks whether integers `0 ≤ m_n ≤ A log(n+2)` can make the model vanish on a set of
positive measure, at a weight with `σ² < 2`. (They can: Theorem 9.22 and Corollary 9.23, with
an astronomical `A`. The text below predates that.) By Proposition 9.10 such a sequence cannot be
eventually periodic. The single-scale view does not decide the question. On `S = {F = 0}` the
truncation `F_N` must equal `−(tail)`, of `L²` norm `≲ log N/√N`, so `F_N` must be that close to
`0` in `L²(S)` while ranging over a lattice-like set. For an arc `S`, the referee's computations
show that this lattice is badly conditioned and far from generic: in `L²(0,π)` one basis vector
stays at distance `≈ 0.83` from the span of the others for all `N ≤ 200`, and LLL/Babai distances
stay `≈ 0.3` for `N ≤ 24`. This is consistent with the rigidity on arcs. (An earlier
"Gaussian-heuristic threshold" claim in this remark was wrong and has been withdrawn.) For fat
sets with empty interior, where Theorem 9.6 lives once the box constraint is dropped, nothing
is known. The box constraint is therefore the whole question.

**Proposition 9.12 (lacunary comb support; no hypothesis).** Let `b_q ∈ ℤ` (`q ≥ 1`) with
`Σ_q |b_q|/q < ∞`, put `m_n := Σ_{q|n} b_q`, `M > 0`, and let `F` be the cyclic model. Write
`P(Q) := Σ_{q≤Q, b_q≠0} q` and `ε(Q) := Σ_{q>Q} |b_q|/q`. If `liminf_{Q→∞} P(Q)·ε(Q) = 0`, then
`λ{F = c} = 0` for every `c ∉ 1 + 2ℤ`.

*Proof.* As in Lemma 9.0, `F = 1 + 2MΣ_q b_qρ_q`, the series converging uniformly because
`|ρ_q| ≤ 2π/q`. Fix `Q` and let `𝒢_Q := ∪_{q≤Q, b_q≠0} (2π/q)ℤ ⊂ 𝕋`, a set of at most `P(Q)`
points. On each component `I` of `𝕋 ∖ 𝒢_Q`,

    F(t) = β_Q + 2Mα_I e^{−Mt} + r_Q(t),    β_Q := 1 − 2Σ_{q≤Q} b_q ∈ 1 + 2ℤ,    |r_Q| ≤ 4πM ε(Q),

exactly as in the proof of Theorem 9.1 (the combs with `q ≤ Q` have no atom inside `I`).

Suppose `S := {F = c}` has positive measure, and put `d := dist(c − 1, 2ℤ) > 0`. Choose
`Q_1 < Q_2 < …` with `P(Q_k)ε(Q_k) → 0`, and fix `λ ∈ (0,1)` with `2πλ < λ(S)`. The components of
`𝕋 ∖ 𝒢_{Q_k}` of length `< 2πλ/P(Q_k)` number at most `P(Q_k)`, so their union has measure
`≤ 2πλ`. Hence the set of `t` lying in such a short component for all but finitely many `k`
has measure `≤ 2πλ < λ(S)`. This is Fatou's lemma for sets: `λ(liminf_k A_k) ≤ liminf_k λ(A_k)`,
where `A_k` is the union of the short components. Also discard the countable union of the grids.
So there is a Lebesgue density point `t_*` of `S` that, for
infinitely many `k`, lies in a component `I_k` with `|I_k| ≥ 2πλ/P(Q_k)`. Also `|I_k| ≤ 2π/q_k^*`,
where `q_k^*` is the largest `q ≤ Q_k` with `b_q ≠ 0`, and `q_k^* → ∞` unless only finitely many
`b_q ≠ 0`. (In that case `F` is a finite comb, analytic off finitely many points, and the claim
follows from Theorem 9.1's parity step, or directly.) So `|I_k| → 0`, and `S` has density
`→ 1` in `I_k`.

Take `s_1 < s_2` in `S ∩ I_k` with `s_2 − s_1 ≥ |I_k|/2`, which is possible once the density
exceeds `3/4`; for `k` large, `M(s_2 − s_1) ≤ 1`, so `1 − e^{−M(s_2−s_1)} ≥ M(s_2−s_1)/2`. Subtracting the two equations `c = β + 2Mαe^{−Ms_i} + r(s_i)` gives
`2M|α|e^{−Ms_1}(1 − e^{−M(s_2−s_1)}) ≤ 8πMε(Q_k)`. Since `t ∈ [0,2π)` and `M(s_2 − s_1)` is
small, this yields `2M|α| ≤ C_M ε(Q_k)/|I_k|` with `C_M = 32πe^{2πM}`. Then
`d ≤ |β_{Q_k} − c| ≤ 2M|α| + 4πMε(Q_k) ≤ ε(Q_k)(C_M P(Q_k)/(2πλ) + 4πM) → 0`, a
contradiction. ∎

*Comments.* (i) The proof uses the uniform smallness of the tail relative to the density of
the coarse grid, not periodicity. So it covers comb supports that are not divisor chains,
e.g. any `b_q ∈ {0, ±1}` supported on `q_1 < q_2 < …` with `q_{k+1}/q_k → ∞`. (`P(Q)` may be
replaced by the number of distinct grid points; `Σ m_n²/n² < ∞` is automatic because `F` is
bounded. These comments are from the referee of this proposition, who found no error.) Theorem 9.1 covers
chains with bounded ratio, where `P ε ≍ 1`. (ii) It does not reach bounded or log-bounded
multiplicities in general. For typical bounded `m` the Möbius coefficients `b = μ * m` are
supported on almost all `q` and `Σ|b_q|/q = ∞`. Question P″ is untouched in that generic case.
(iii) Together with Theorem 9.6 this supports the following picture, a **heuristic**: atoms
need an integer cascade whose corrections are large compared with the coarse grid at
infinitely many scales; lacunary combs fail this; Theorem 9.6 achieves it at the price of
multiplicities `≫ (log n)^2`.

**Lemma 9.13 (reduction to finite models; GRH + NCZ).** Let `F_{M,T} := 1 + Re Σ_{γ∈Γ, γ≤T} m_γ r_γ(M) e_γ`
be the truncation of the hull function. It is a trigonometric polynomial on the
finite-dimensional torus `A_T := \overline{\{(e^{iγy})_{γ≤T} : y ∈ ℝ\}}`, and its law under `λ_{A_T}`
is the law of `F_{M,T}∘π_T` under `λ_A`. For all `ε > 0` and `T`,

    ν_M((−ε, ε)) ≤ λ_{A_T}{|F_{M,T}| < 2ε} + V_M^{>T}/ε²,     V_M^{>T} := ½Σ_{γ>T} m_γ²|r_γ(M)|² → 0.

*Proof.* `{|F_M| < ε} ⊂ {|F_{M,T}∘π_T| < 2ε} ∪ {|F_M − F_{M,T}∘π_T| ≥ ε}`; Chebyshev and (1.2) bound
the second set. ∎

So the existence of the density follows from **uniform anti-concentration of finite models**.
Let `𝒞(M, A_0, s)` be the class of all finite "integer models": a finite set `Γ' ⊂ (0,∞)`,
integers `m_γ ≥ 1` with `Σ_{γ∈Γ'∩(t,t+1]} m_γ ≤ A_0 log(t+3)` for all `t ≥ 0`, and
`Σ m_γ 8M²/(M²+γ²) ≤ s`, with the function `F_{Γ'} := 1 + Re Σ_{γ∈Γ'} m_γ r_γ(M) e_γ` on the torus
`A_{Γ'}`. No condition is imposed on the relations among the `γ`. Put

    ω(ε) := sup_{𝒞(M,A_0,s)} λ_{A_{Γ'}}{|F_{Γ'}| < ε}.

**Conjecture U (uniform small-ball bound; open).** For `M = ½`, some admissible constant `A_0` in
(Q3) for `χ_{−4}` (U becomes stronger as `A_0` grows), and `s = 0.1556 ≥ σ²_{1/2}(χ_{−4}) = 0.155568…`,
one has `ω(ε) → 0` as `ε → 0`.

By Lemma 9.13, Conjecture U implies that, under GRH(`χ_{−4}`) alone, `ν_{1/2}({0}) = 0` and the
logarithmic density of `{x : π(x;4,3) > π(x;4,1)}` exists. All truncations `F_{1/2,T}` belong
to the class, whatever the relations among the true ordinates are. In this form the unknown
group generated by the zeros disappears from the problem, and a finitary statement about
trigonometric polynomials on tori remains. It is a Littlewood–Offord-type anti-concentration
statement with *dependent* phases: Haar measure on an arbitrary subtorus. U is stronger than
Question P″ for the true hull. Two corrections from the referee of this lemma bear on its
sharpness.
- **Without integrality it fails** if `A_0 ≳ 2`. Take Example 9.2 at `M = 0.05`, rescaled to the
  classical weight on the spectrum `10ℕ`. It has `σ² = 0.1439 < 0.1556`, positive real weights
  `{4.868, ½}`, and an atom of mass `1/3`. It obeys the counting law with constant about
  `4.868/log 12 ≈ 1.96`. (Proposition 7.3 has `σ² ≈ 0.54` and does not fit the class.)
- **With multiplicities growing like `(log γ)^{5/2}` it fails**, provided `A_0 log` is replaced by
  `A(log)^{5/2}` with a large `A`. Run Theorem 9.6 at `M = 0.01` and rescale to `M = ½` on `50ℕ`.
  The Addendum's increment formula gives `σ² ≲ 0.125` (the referee's estimate, not separately
  certified), and the truncations have `λ{|F_N| < ε_N} ≥ 0.1` with `ε_N → 0`.

**Remark 9.14 (the counting law is one logarithm below the cascade threshold; heuristic).**
In the cascade of Theorem 9.6, write `ρ_j` for the gap fraction at scale `j` (band ratio `K`).
The multiplicity injected at scale `j` is `≍ K²/ρ_j²` (Remark 9.9), and the counting law allows
`≍ A_0 log n ≍ A_0 j log K`. Respecting it forces `ρ_j ≳ K/(A_0 j log K)^{1/2}`. The total measure
removed after `J` scales is then `≳ K√J/(A_0 log K)^{1/2}`, which diverges like
`(log N)^{1/2}` at truncation level `N = K^J`. Three consequences:
- **finite** near-atoms of fixed measure `η` and precision `ε ≍ N^{−1/2}` survive the counting
  law only up to `N ≈ exp(cA_0(2π/3 − η)²)`, that is, down to precision
  `ε ≈ exp(−c′A_0(2π/3 − η)²)`;
- the counting law is exactly one logarithm short of what this mechanism needs: allowing
  multiplicities up to `(log n)^{5/2}` gives atoms (Theorem 9.6; exponents `2+δ` would need the
  variant `ρ_j ∝ j^{−1−δ/2}`, not written out), and `≪ log n` makes the measure budget diverge.
  This ignores that multiplicities accumulate over scales and that the allowance at the
  injection heights is `A_0(j log K + log(1/ρ_j))`; neither changes the conclusion;
- this **suggested** (wrongly, for P″: see Theorem 9.22) that Question P″ and Conjecture U are true, with `ω(ε)` decaying very
  slowly.

(**But see Remark 9.21:** this count assumes plain rounding, and noise-shaped rounding
appears to change the conclusion for signed multiplicities.) A proof would need a
**multi-scale uncertainty principle** for integer models. Every way of
vanishing to precision `ε` on a set of measure `η` would have to cost, at each scale `j`, a
removed measure `ρ_j` with `Σ_{j≤J} ρ_j ≳ √J`. Within this heuristic, that is the precise
missing statement. **Heuristic only**: it bounds one family of constructions, and nothing
here excludes a cleverer mechanism.

**Theorem 9.15 (Toeplitz-symmetric bounded multiplicities; no hypothesis).** Found by a
subagent attack in this session; proof re-derived. Let `m_n ∈ ℤ` with `|m_n| ≤ B`, extended by
`m_{−n} := m_n`, `m_0 := 0`, and let `𝒩` be the set of `N ≥ 1` such that `n ↦ m_n·1[N ∤ n]` is
`N`-periodic on `ℤ`. If `𝒩` is infinite, then for every `M > 0` the cyclic model `F` satisfies
`λ{F = c} = 0` for every `c ∉ 1 + 2ℤ`.

Examples: the nested class of Theorem 9.1 with bounded multiplicities; "Toeplitz/digit"
sequences such as `m_n = 1` if the last nonzero base-5 digit of `n` is `1` or `4`, and `0`
otherwise. The latter is not constant on shells, is not eventually periodic (so it has natural
boundary), and has `Σ_{q≤X}|b_q|/q ≍ log X` for its Möbius coefficients, so Theorem 9.1 and
Propositions 9.10 and 9.12 do not apply.

*Proof.* (1) *Exact cell identity.* Fix `N ∈ 𝒩` and write `w = w_c + w_f`, where `w_f` collects
the frequencies in `Nℤ ∖ {0}`. The function `a(n) := m_n 1[N∤n]` is `N`-periodic, so
`Σ_n a(n)e^{int} = Σ_{r mod N} a(r) e^{irt} C_N(t)`, `C_N = (2π/N)Σ_j δ_{2πj/N}`. This is a measure on the
grid `(2π/N)ℤ` with real masses (by symmetry). Hence `w_c = ½ + (grid measure)` and, on each open
cell `I = (x_a, x_a + 2π/N)`, `w_c = ½`, so the resolvent of `w_c` is `1 + α_I e^{−Mt}` on `I`. The fine part
`2M(D+M)^{−1}w_f = 2MΣ_{d≠0} m_{|dN|} e^{idNt}/(M + idN)` is `2π/N`-periodic. With `t = x_a + s/N`,
`s ∈ (0,2π)`, and since `e^{idN x_a} = 1`,

    F(x_a + s/N) = 1 + α′ e^{−Ms/N} + (2M/N) H_N(s),     H_N(s) := Σ_{d≠0} m_{|dN|} e^{ids}/(μ + id),  μ := M/N,

with `α′ = α_I e^{−Mx_a}`, and `‖H_N‖_2² ≤ Σ_{d≠0} B²/d² =: C_0²`.

(2) *Blow-up.* Suppose `S := {F = c}` has positive measure. Let `t_*` be a Lebesgue density point
of `S` lying on none of the countably many grids. For `N ∈ 𝒩` let `I_N ∋ t_*` be its cell and
`E_N := {s ∈ (0,2π) : x_a + s/N ∈ S}`. Since `|I_N| = 2π/N → 0`, `|(0,2π) ∖ E_N| =: 2πδ_N → 0`.

(3) *Bounding `α′`.* By Chebyshev, for `N` large there are `s_1 ∈ (0, π/2)` and `s_2 ∈ (3π/2, 2π)` in
`E_N` with `|H_N(s_i)| ≤ 10C_0`. Subtracting the two equations `c = 1 + α′e^{−Ms_i/N} + (2M/N)H_N(s_i)`
gives `|α′| ≤ 40C_0/π + o(1)`. Hence `κ_N := (N/M)(c − 1 − α′)` is bounded.

(4) *Reduced equation.* Expanding `e^{−Ms/N} = 1 − Ms/N + O(N^{−2})` uniformly for `s ∈ (0,2π)`, and
using `α′ = (c−1) + O(1/N)`, the equation on `E_N` becomes, with `λ := (1−c)/2`,

    D_N(s) := λ s + H_N(s) − κ_N/2 = O(1/N)    uniformly on E_N.

(5) *Fourier coefficients of `D_N`.* On `(0, 2π)`, `s = π − Σ_{d≠0} e^{ids}/(id)`. Hence
`\hat D_N(d) = (m_{|dN|} − λ)/(id) + O(B μ/d²)` for `d ≠ 0`. Put `θ := dist(λ, ℤ) > 0` (since `c ∉ 1+2ℤ`).
Then for `N` large:
- `|\hat D_N(d)| ≥ θ/(2|d|)`, so `‖D_N‖_2² ≥ (θ²/4)Σ_{d≠0} d^{−2} = θ²π²/12`;
- `|\hat D_N(d)| ≤ (B + |λ| + 1)/|d|` and `\hat D_N(0)` is bounded, so by Hausdorff–Young
  `‖D_N‖_4 ≤ C_3` uniformly in `N`.

(6) *Contradiction.* With normalised norms,
`‖D_N‖_2² ≤ ess sup_{E_N}|D_N|² + δ_N^{1/2}‖D_N‖_4² ≤ C/N² + δ_N^{1/2}C_3² → 0`,
which contradicts the lower bound in (5). ∎

*Remarks.* (i) The proof also shows: if `λ{F = c} > 0` with `c ∈ 1 + 2ℤ`, then `m_{dN} → (1−c)/2`

**Theorem 9.16 (integrality excludes flat arcs at every growth; cyclic model; no hypothesis).**
Let `m_n ∈ ℤ` (`n ≥ 1`) with `|m_n| ≤ C(1+n)^A`, `M > 0`, and let `F` be the cyclic model. That is,
`F` is the distribution on `𝕋` with `\hat F(0) = 1` and `\hat F(±n) = 2M m_n/(M ± in)`; it is a
function in `L²` when `Σ m_n²/n² < ∞`. Suppose `F = c` on a nonempty open arc `I`, as distributions
(for `F ∈ L²`: a.e. on `I`). Then:
- `g(z) := Σ_{n≥1} m_n zⁿ` is a rational function whose poles are roots of unity in `𝕋 ∖ I`;
- `c ∈ 1 + 2ℤ`.

Hence for every integer sequence of polynomial growth and every `c ∉ 1 + 2ℤ`, the level set
`{F = c}` contains no arc up to a null set. The proof uses only that `w` is constant on `I`, so
the hypothesis may be weakened to `F ∈ c + ℂe^{−Mt}` on `I`. **Status:** a short consequence of
the Pólya–Carlson theorem once step (2) is in place; folklore-level. The distributional form and
the parity conclusion were not found stated (`LITERATURE.md`, fourth check).

*Proof.* Put `κ := (c−1)/2`. If `I = 𝕋`, all coefficients of `F` but the constant vanish, so
`m = 0` and `c = 1`. Otherwise `K := 𝕋 ∖ I` is a closed arc of length `ℓ < 2π`.

(1) *The driving distribution is constant on `I`.* `w := (D+M)F/(2M)` has `\hat w(0) = ½` and
`\hat w(±n) = m_n`. On `I`, `F = c`, so `DF = 0` and `w = c/2` there. Hence `T := w − c/2` is
supported in `K`, with `\hat T(0) = −κ` and `\hat T(±n) = m_n`.

(2) *Herglotz transform.* For `z ∉ K` let `k_z(θ) := (e^{iθ}+z)/(e^{iθ}−z)`. Choose a closed arc
`K′` with `K ⊂ int K′` and `z ∉ K′`, and `χ ∈ C^∞(𝕋)` equal to `1` near `K` and supported in `K′`.
Put `H(z) := ⟨T, χk_z⟩`, normalised so that `⟨T, e^{−inθ}⟩ = \hat T(n)`.
- `H(z)` does not depend on these choices: `T` vanishes near the support of the difference of two
  choices.
- `H` is analytic on `Ĉ ∖ K`, including `∞`. This follows by differentiating under the pairing,
  since `T` has finite order; also `k_z → −1` in `C^∞(K′)` as `z → ∞`.
- For `|z| < 1`, `k_z = 1 + 2Σ_{n≥1} zⁿe^{−inθ}` in `C^∞`, and `⟨T, (1−χ)k_z⟩ = 0`. So
  `H(z) = \hat T(0) + 2Σ_{n≥1}\hat T(n)zⁿ = 2g(z) − κ`.
- For `|z| > 1`, `k_z = −1 − 2Σ_{n≥1} z^{−n}e^{inθ}`. So `H(z) = κ − 2g(1/z)`, and `H(∞) = κ`.

Hence `g = (H + κ)/2` continues analytically from `𝔻` to `Ĉ ∖ K`, with `g(∞) = κ`.

(3) *Rationality.* By (2), `g` continues across `I`, so the unit circle is not its natural
boundary, and Pólya–Carlson gives rationality. Equivalently, via Pólya's capacity theorem:
`G(ζ) := g(1/ζ) = Σ_{n≥1} m_n ζ^{−n}` has integer coefficients. It is
analytic (single-valued) in the complement of `K^{−1} := {1/z : z ∈ K}`, which is a closed arc of
the unit circle of length `ℓ`. That arc has transfinite diameter `sin(ℓ/4) < 1`. Pólya's
theorem states: a series `Σ_{n≥0} a_n ζ^{−n}` with `a_n ∈ ℤ` that is analytic in the complement
of a compact set of transfinite diameter `< 1` is rational (G. Pólya, Math. Ann. 99 (1928);
**MEMORY**, see `LITERATURE.md`). So `G`, hence `g`, is rational.

(4) *Parity.* By Fatou's lemma, `g = R/Q` with `R, Q ∈ ℤ[z]` coprime and `Q(0) = 1`; write
`Q(z) = Π_j (1 − α_j z)`. The poles `1/α_j` of `g` lie in `K ⊂ 𝕋`, so `|α_j| = 1`. Hence the
leading coefficient `Π_j(−α_j)` of `Q` is an integer of modulus `1`, i.e. `±1`. Since `g` is
analytic at `∞`, `deg R ≤ deg Q`, and `g(∞)` equals `±` the coefficient of `z^{deg Q}` in `R`,
an integer. So `κ ∈ ℤ` and `c = 1 + 2κ ∈ 1 + 2ℤ`. Finally, the `α_j` are algebraic integers
(roots of the monic integer polynomial `z^{deg Q}Q(1/z)`), closed under conjugation and of
modulus `1`, so by Kronecker's theorem they are roots of unity. ∎

**Proposition 9.17 (on fat sets the right-limit mechanism fails without integrality; no
hypothesis).** Let `M > 0` and `J := (4π/3, 2π)`. Then there exist:
- real weights `m_n ∈ [¼, C]`;
- a set `S ⊂ J` with `λ(S) ≥ ¾|J|` and empty interior;
- integers `N_k → ∞`;

such that the cyclic model satisfies `F = 0` on `S` and the limits `b_r := lim_k m_{N_k+r}` exist
for every `r ∈ ℤ`, while the distribution `β := Σ_{r∈ℤ} b_r e^{irθ}` coincides on `J` with
`ϑ·1_S` for a constant `ϑ ≠ 0`. So the right limit does not vanish on the atom; it is carried by
it.

*Proof.* (a) *Bumps with zero exponential moments.* Fix an even `θ_0 ∈ C_c^∞(−½,½)`, `θ_0 ≥ 0`,
`θ_0 ≠ 0`. For `0 < ℓ ≤ 1` put `ψ_ℓ := (d/du)(d²/du² − M²ℓ²)θ_0`. Then
`∫ψ_ℓ(u)e^{su}du = −s(s² − M²ℓ²)∫θ_0(u)e^{su}du`, which vanishes for `s ∈ {0, ±Mℓ}`. Its
transform is `\hat ψ_ℓ(ξ) := (1/2π)∫ψ_ℓ(u)e^{−iξu}du = −iξ(ξ² + M²ℓ²)\hat θ_0(ξ)`. So:
- `|\hat ψ_ℓ(ξ)| ≤ C_0 min(|ξ|, |ξ|^{−2})` uniformly in `ℓ ≤ 1`;
- `\hat ψ_ℓ(1) → −i\hat θ_0(1) ≠ 0` as `ℓ → 0`, because `\hat θ_0(1) = (1/2π)∫θ_0 cos > 0`.

For `x ∈ J` with `x + ℓ[−½,½] ⊂ J`, let `v_{x,ℓ}(t) := ψ_ℓ((t−x)/ℓ) + ψ_ℓ((−t−x)/ℓ)`, periodised.
- `v_{x,ℓ}` is even, real and of mean `0`, with `\hat v_{x,ℓ}(n) = 2ℓ Re(e^{−inx}\hat ψ_ℓ(nℓ))`.
- Both bumps have vanishing exponential moments `∫v e^{Mt}`. So the periodic solution of
  `(D+M)φ = 2Mv_{x,ℓ}` is supported in `±(x + ℓ[−½,½])`.
- That solution satisfies `|φ| ≤ 2Mℓ‖ψ_ℓ‖_{L¹}`.

(b) *Levels.* Choose `ℓ_k ↓ 0` with `ℓ_1 ≤ |J|/8`, `ℓ_{k+1} ≤ ℓ_k/2` and `ℓ_{k+1}/ℓ_k → 0`. Put
`n_k := ⌈1/ℓ_k⌉` and `P_k := ⌊|J|2^{−k−2}/ℓ_k⌋`, so that `Σ_k P_kℓ_k ≤ |J|/4` and `Σ_k 1/P_k < ∞`.
The level-`k` candidate centres are the points of `(2π/n_k)ℤ` nearest to
`4π/3 + (i−½)|J|/P_k`, `1 ≤ i ≤ P_k`. A candidate `x` is kept if
`x + ℓ_k[−3/2, 3/2] ⊂ J` misses all gaps of earlier levels; its gap is then `x + ℓ_k[−½,½]`. Let
`𝒳_k` be the set of kept centres and `P′_k := #𝒳_k`. Put

    δm_n := ε Σ_k (1/(P_kℓ_k)) Σ_{x∈𝒳_k} \hat v_{x,ℓ_k}(n) = ε Σ_k (2/P_k) Re Σ_{x∈𝒳_k} e^{−inx}\hat ψ_{ℓ_k}(nℓ_k).

Then `|δm_n| ≤ 2εC_0 Σ_k min(nℓ_k, (nℓ_k)^{−2}) ≤ 8εC_0`, since `ℓ_k` decreases at least
geometrically. Choose `ε := 1/(32C_0)`. Let `m⁰_n` be the weights of Example 9.2 (`m⁰_n ∈ {b_0, ½}`,
`b_0 > ½`, `F_0 = 0` on `J`) and `m_n := m⁰_n + δm_n ∈ [¼, b_0 + ¼]`.

At level `k` the bump solutions have disjoint supports and total sup at most `C/P_k`. So
`F = F_0 + εΣ_k (1/(P_kℓ_k)) Σ_{x∈𝒳_k} φ_{x,ℓ_k}` converges uniformly, and its Fourier
coefficients are those of the cyclic model with weights `m_n`.

(c) *The atom.* Let `S := J ∖ ∪_{k, x∈𝒳_k}(x + ℓ_k[−½,½])`. `F_0` and every `φ` vanish on `S`, so
`F = 0` on `S`, and `λ(J ∖ S) ≤ |J|/4`.

`S` has empty interior. If an interval `I′ ⊂ S`, then all gaps lie outside `I′`. For large `k`,
the middle third of `I′` contains a level-`k` candidate, which is at distance `≥ |I′|/3 > 2ℓ_k`
from every earlier gap. It is therefore kept, and its gap lies in `I′`: a contradiction.

(d) *The right limit.* Take `N_k := n_k` along a subsequence on which:
- `n_k mod 3` is constant;
- `P′_k/P_k → q`;
- the probability measures `ν_k := P′_k^{−1}Σ_{x∈𝒳_k}δ_x` converge weakly to some `ν`.

Fix `r`. Then:
- `m⁰_{n_k+r} =: b⁰_r` is a fixed 3-periodic two-sided sequence along the subsequence.
- In the level-`k` term, `e^{−in_kx} = 1` on the grid. So that term equals
  `2ε(P′_k/P_k)Re[\hat ψ_{ℓ_k}(n_kℓ_k + rℓ_k)∫e^{−irx}dν_k]`, which tends to
  `2εq Re[\hat ψ_0(1)\hat ν(r)]`, where `\hat ν(r) := ∫e^{−irx}dν`.
- The other levels contribute at most `2εC_0Σ_{k′≠k} min((n_k+|r|)ℓ_{k′}, ((n_k−|r|)ℓ_{k′})^{−2})`.
  This tends to `0` because `ℓ_{k+1}/ℓ_k → 0`.

So `b_r = b⁰_r + 2εq Re[\hat ψ_0(1)\hat ν(r)]`, and

    β = β⁰ + 2πεq(\hat ψ_0(1) ν + \overline{\hat ψ_0(1)} ν^−),     ν^−(E) := ν(−E).

Here `β⁰` is a combination of Dirac masses at `0, 2π/3, 4π/3`, and `ν^−` lives on `−J̄`; both
vanish on `J`.

The candidates are equally spaced, at spacing `|J|/P_k ≈ 2^{k+2}ℓ_k`. Each earlier gap, of
length `ℓ_{k′}`, removes `(ℓ_{k′} + 3ℓ_k)P_k/|J| + O(1)` candidates. Moreover
`Σ_{k′<k}P_{k′} ≤ (4 + o(1))(ℓ_k/ℓ_{k−1})P_k = o(P_k)`, so the `O(1)` terms are negligible. Hence
the kept candidates are equidistributed with respect to Lebesgue measure on
`J ∖ (earlier gaps)`, up to `o(1)`. Letting `k → ∞` gives `ν = 1_S dθ/λ(S)` and `q = λ(S)/|J|`. On `J`, therefore,
`β = (2πε\hat ψ_0(1)/|J|)·1_S`, with `ϑ = −2πiε\hat θ_0(1)/|J| ≠ 0`. ∎

*Meaning.* On arcs, reflectionlessness of right limits holds for all bounded complex weights
(Breuer–Simon; Remark (ii) after Theorem 9.16), and integrality is needed only for rationality.
On fat sets even the first step fails for real weights. So any proof of the cyclic
bounded-multiplicity case of Question P″ by the route

    atom ⇒ right limits vanish on S ⇒ past determines future ⇒ eventual periodicity ⇒ Proposition 9.10

must use integrality already in its first arrow. (The later arrows are only sketched: by
compactness they are routine for finitely valued sequences *once* "β vanishes on `S`" has a
definition giving uniqueness. For nowhere dense `S` no such definition is available, and with
bounded coefficients `f_+` need not have nontangential limits.) Numerical check
(`right_limit_real_weights_check.py`, by the referee, at `M = ½`): on `S`, `|F_0 + φ| ≤ 1.5·10^{−15}`;
`(m − m⁰)_{n_k+r}/ε` matches `2q_k Re[\hat ψ_0(1)\hat ν_k(r)] = 2q_k\hat θ_0(1) Im \hat ν_k(r)` to
`≈ 2·10^{−5}` relative at `k = 3`. The effect is tiny in absolute terms, since `C_0 ≈ 10^5` forces
`ε ≈ 3·10^{−7}`.

**Lemma 9.18 (compactness: no atoms is the same as a uniform small-ball bound; no
hypothesis).** Fix `M > 0` and a compact set `𝒦` of real sequences (product topology) with:
- `sup_{m∈𝒦} Σ_{n>N} m_n²/n² → 0` as `N → ∞`;
- `𝒦` closed under truncation (`m ↦ m·1_{[1,N]}`).

Examples are all integer sequences with `|m_n| ≤ B`, or with `0 ≤ m_n ≤ A log(n+2)`. Let
`C ⊂ ℝ ∖ (1+2ℤ)` be compact. (Odd levels must be excluded: `m = 0` gives `F ≡ 1`, and
`m_n = 1 − 1_{4|n}` at `M = (2/π)log(1+√2)` lies in the bounded class.) Then the following are equivalent:
- (a) `λ{F_m = c} = 0` for all `m ∈ 𝒦` and `c ∈ C`;
- (b) `ω(ε) := sup_{m∈𝒦, c∈C} λ{|F_m − c| < ε} → 0` as `ε → 0`.

The supremum in (b) includes all truncations, i.e. all finite models of every degree.

*Proof.* (b) ⇒ (a) is clear. Suppose (b) fails: there are `η > 0`, `m^j ∈ 𝒦`, `c_j ∈ C` and
`ε_j → 0` such that `S_j := {|F_{m^j} − c_j| < ε_j}` has `λ(S_j) ≥ η`. Pass to a subsequence with
`m^j → m`, `c_j → c` and `1_{S_j} → ρ` weak* in `L^∞`, so `0 ≤ ρ ≤ 1` and `∫ρ ≥ η`.

Each coefficient of `F_m` depends continuously on `m`, and the tails are uniformly small. So
`F_{m^j} − c_j → F_m − c` in `L²`, and `∫|F_m − c|²ρ = lim_j ∫|F_{m^j} − c_j|²1_{S_j} ≤ lim 2πε_j² = 0`.
Hence `F_m = c` on `{ρ > 0}`, a set of measure `≥ η`, and (a) fails. ∎

In particular, within the cyclic class with bounded, or log-bounded, integer multiplicities,
Question P″ (at levels in a compact `C ⊂ ℝ∖(1+2ℤ)`, with the closed constraint `σ² ≤ 2 − δ`) and
the corresponding form of Conjecture U are one statement. For log-bounded
multiplicities this class contains the shape of the true counting law but not its exact
constant. The class of Conjecture U itself ranges over arbitrary finite spectra and subtori,
which is not a compact family, so Lemma 9.18 does not apply there.

**Remark 9.19 (the fat-set Pólya–Szegő problem; status).**
- *Restatement.* Put `Φ(z) := Σ_{n≥1}(2M m_n/(M+in))zⁿ ∈ H²` and `κ := (c−1)/2`. Then
  `F = 1 + Φ* + \overline{Φ*}` on `𝕋`, where `Φ = 2M(M + iz d/dz)^{−1}g` (`M + iz d/dz = M + D` on `𝕋`), and `F = c` on `S` says that `Φ` (from `𝔻`) and
  `2κ − \overline{Φ(1/\bar z)}` (from outside) have the same nontangential boundary values a.e. on
  `S`. In other words, an atom in the cyclic model is a partial pseudocontinuation of `Φ` across
  `S`, and `Φ` is an integer power series `g` integrated once against `(M + izd/dz)^{−1}`.
- *Arcs: solved.* If `S` contains an arc, Theorem 9.16 gives rationality and parity at any
  growth.
- *Fat sets, growth `(log n)^{5/2}`: false.* Theorem 9.6 is a non-rational integer series (it has
  natural boundary, by Pólya–Carlson; cf. Proposition 9.10) with such a partial
  pseudocontinuation across a set of positive measure. So the fat-set analogue of Pólya's
  theorem fails.
- *Fat sets, finitely many values: open.* This is the fat-set analogue of the Szegő and
  Duffin–Schaeffer theorems. By Lemma 9.18 it is equivalent to a uniform small-ball bound.
  Proposition 9.17 shows that the Breuer–Simon/Remling route needs integrality from its first
  step.
- *Obstacle met in a tail argument (heuristic).* The two-constants theorem does hold for a
  boundary set of positive measure. But the frequency-shifted data `N e^{−iNθ}(F − c)` contain
  the low-frequency part of `F` with a factor `N`. Suppressing it by a test function supported
  in `S` requires the test function to lie in `H^{1/2+}`. Functions supported on a nowhere dense
  set are not in `H^s` for `s > ½`, by continuity; the case `s = ½` is not settled. This is the same critical exponent as in
  Remark 9.9 and the logarithmic threshold of Remark 9.14.
- *Literature.* No fat-set version of Pólya, Szegő, Duffin–Schaeffer or Breuer–Simon was found
  (`LITERATURE.md`, fourth check).

**Remark 9.20 (integer certificates; standard duality; refereed).** In the setting of Question P,
let `F = c` a.e. on `S ⊂ A`, `κ := (c−1)/2`. Take any trigonometric polynomial `φ = Σ_g φ_g e_g`
whose coefficients satisfy `φ_0 ∈ ℤ` and `φ_γ + φ_{−γ} ∈ ℤ` for `γ ∈ Γ`. The other coefficients
are free, because `w` is inversion-symmetric. Then

    dist(κφ_0, ℤ) ≤ (2M)^{−1} ‖F − c‖_{L²(A)} ‖(M − D)φ‖_{L²(A∖S)} .

For `c = 0` and `φ_0` odd this reads `‖(M−D)φ‖_{L²(A∖S)} ≥ M/‖F‖_{L²(A)}`, where
`‖F‖² = 1 + 8M²Σ_γ m_γ²/(M²+γ²)`. If all zeros of `L(s,χ_{−4})` are simple, the bound at `M = ½` is
`≈ 0.465`; under GRH alone the multiplicities, hence `‖F‖`, are unknown.

*Proof.* Pair `T = w − c/2 = (D+M)(F−c)/(2M)` with `φ`. On one hand
`B(T, φ) := Σ_g \hat T(g)φ_{−g} = −κφ_0 + Σ_γ m_γ(φ_γ + φ_{−γ})`. On the other,
`B(T, φ) = (2M)^{−1}∫_{A∖S}(F − c)(M − D)φ dλ`. Cauchy–Schwarz. ∎

*Status* (fifth/sixth referee reads). This is the standard duality between integer
coefficients and Fekete polynomials, not new. With `φ = 1` it is the trivial bound
`λ(S) ≤ V/(1+V)`, `V := ‖F − 1‖²`. Further points:
- **Arcs.** In the cyclic case, if `S` contains an arc `I`, shrink `I` so that either `±1 ∈ I` or
  `Ī ∩ −Ī = ∅`. In the second case apply Fekete's theorem (1923; capacity `< 1` gives an integer
  polynomial `P` with `max|P| < 1`) to the symmetric set `K ∩ −K`, `K = 𝕋 ∖ (I ∪ −I)`. Then
  symmetrise, and cut off near `−I`, using the free odd part. This gives certificates with
  `‖(M−D)φ_n‖_{L²(𝕋∖S)} → 0` and recovers the level-`0` case of Theorem 9.16 for `F ∈ L²`.
- **The widening is necessary.** Within the narrow class (all `φ_g ∈ ℤ`) no certificate exists
  for an arc in a half-circle. A reflection/monodromy argument gives
  `‖(M−D)φ‖_{L²(𝕋∖I)} ≥ c_0 > 0` for every real `φ` with `φ_0 = 1`. So in the narrow class the
  duality question has a negative answer. For the widened class it is open.
- **An example on a nowhere dense set.** Take `U_j`, `j ≥ 1`, with
  `U_j = {dist(θ, (2π/2^j)ℤ) < e^{−8^j}2^{−j}}` and `S := 𝕋 ∖ ∪_{j≥1}U_j`. Then
  `φ_J := Π_{j≤J}(1 − z^{2^j})^{2^{J−j+1}+1}` are certificates. On `U_j` with `j > J` one uses
  `sup|(M−D)φ_J| ≤ e^{O(2^J)}` and `λ(∪_{j>J}U_j)^{1/2} ≤ e^{−8^{J+1}/2}`. This `S` has measure
  `> 1 − 2·10^{−4}`, so it is already excluded by `λ(S) ≤ V/(1+V)` unless `V ≥ 5000`.
- **Expected use for the true race: low.** A certificate would have to beat the trivial bound on
  the complement of an unknown nowhere-dense set.

**Remark 9.21 (noise-shaped rounding; the heuristic behind Theorem 9.22).**

*Why Theorem 9.6 cannot use clever rounding.* The correction of Theorem 9.6 is
`(1−D/M)(e^Ψ_j β_j/\hat β_j(0))`. Its high copies have multiplicities proportional to each
individual rounding error `δm_n`, so no rounding strategy lowers its cost.

*A modified correction.*
- Choose `u ∈ C_c^∞` with `u ≡ 1` on a neighbourhood `T` of the target set, `u = 0` near `θ = 0`,
  and `u` even.
- Add `(1−D/M)[e^Ψ_j − e^Ψ_j u (1 − g_j)]`, where `g_j := β_j/\hat β_j(0)`.
- This vanishes on `S_{j+1}`, and its low-frequency part is the rounding `e^Ψ_j` plus a leak.
  The leak is the Schwartz tail of `\hat u` seen through the copies of `g_j`. It can be removed
  exactly by applying the comb mechanism of Theorem 9.6 to its band-limited low part, at
  negligible cost.
- The copies now cost `≍ (K/ρ_j)²·sup_n |(e^Ψ_j u)^(n)|·n²`. This is the size of the rounding
  error **on `T`**, not of the individual errors.

*Noise shaping.*
- Round each band by a `k`-th order sigma-delta scheme: noise transfer function
  `H(z) = (1−z)^k` (or a Chebyshev polynomial of the arc), greedy integer quantiser, an offset
  `B/2` with a slowly varying profile, and window `[0, B]` with `B ≥ 2^k + 2`.
- Then `δm = (offset) + h * e` with `|e| ≤ ½`, and `Σ (h*e)_n e^{inθ} = H(θ)·Σ e_n e^{inθ}`.
- For the comb `(1, q)` of Example 9.2 type (base weights `≈ ½`, `F_0 = 0` on the last cell `J`),
  the set `T ⊃ J ∪ −J` lies in the arc `|θ| ≤ 2π/q`. There `|H| ≤ τ := (2 sin(π/q))^k`.
- Pointwise, `(e^Ψ u)^` involves the coefficients of `Hu`, whose `ℓ¹` norm is `≲ τ·poly(k)`.

`noise_shaping_check.py` (`q = 20`, `M = ½`, one band, `N = 4096`) confirms this. From `k = 0` to
`k = 8`, both the error on `T` and `max|(e^Ψu)^|` in multiplicity units fall by about
`5·10⁴`, close to `sup_T|H| ≈ 10^{−4}`, with multiplicities `≤ 258`.

*Budget.* With `B_j ≍ τ_j^{−1/β}`, `β := log_2(1/(2 sin(π/q)))` (`β ≈ 1.68` for `q = 20`):

    m_n ≲ 1 + τ_j poly(k_j)(K/ρ_j)² + B_j,   optimised:   m_n ≍ (K/ρ_j)^{2/(1+β)}·(log j)^{O(1)} .

With summable `ρ_j ≍ j^{−1−δ}` this is `≪ (log n)^{2(1+δ)/(1+β)+o(1)}`, an exponent `< 1` once
`β > 1 + 2δ` (`q ≥ 7`). Optimal filters (outer functions; Jensen) give `β` up to `(q−2)/2`.

*Positivity.* Greedy shaping needs room `‖h‖_1/2` below the target as well as above.
- The room comes from an offset `c(n) ≈ B_j/2` added to all multiplicities from some height
  `N_{j_0}` on. Its profile is smooth and slowly varying (smooth in `log n`).
- This applies also to the deepest comb class, whose real weight is pinned at the bias `½` by
  the constant condition `1 − 2B(J) = 0` of Theorem 9.1.
- Such an offset is a real perturbation of the base. Its contribution on `T` and its products
  with `u` are tiny, because `u` vanishes near `θ = 0`, so all moments `Σ_l l^r \hat u(l) = 0`.
  Its size is `≍ B_j/(N_jσ)` in multiplicity units; `noise_shaping_check.py` gives `2.4·10^{−4}` per
  unit offset at `N = 4096`.
- It can therefore be cancelled on `S` by the same modified correction, at negligible cost. A
  first version of this remark claimed that the `½`-class obstructs positive multiplicities;
  that was wrong.
- Fixed-alphabet schemes (Daubechies–DeVore; Güntürk) would keep multiplicities bounded. But on
  an arc of fixed width they reduce the error only by a bounded factor `≈ e^{−cq}`, in line with
  the Borwein–Erdélyi–Littmann theorem that finite-alphabet polynomials cannot be uniformly
  small on an arc. A bounded factor does not suffice, since `ρ_j → 0`.

*What this suggests.*
- **(a)** For every `M > 0` and `θ > 0` there are integers `m_n ≥ 0` with `m_n ≪ (log n)^θ` (taking `q`
  large) whose cyclic model vanishes on a set of positive measure.
  - With `M` small, `σ² < 2`.
  - Since `(log n)^θ ≤ A log(n+3)`, Question P″ would then have a **negative answer** for `G = ℤ`.
    Integrality, positivity, the counting law and `σ² < 2` would not exclude an atom, and
    Remark 9.14's conclusion would be reversed.
- **(b)** The bounded case is untouched, since the heuristic needs `B_j → ∞`. By Lemma 9.18 it
  remains equivalent to a uniform small-ball bound, and it is now the sharpest open spectral
  question. It concerns multiplicities bounded by a constant, which GRH does not provide.

Making (a) rigorous needs:
- the bookkeeping of the copies, which are now signed;
- the offset profile, introduced band by band through a smooth partition in frequency and
  corrected before the bands it touches are rounded;
- the filter assigned by the band of origin of each error, so that `δm − c = Σ_j h^{(j)} * (e 1_{B_j})`
  holds exactly. Cutting `h*e` at band edges would leave unshaped terms of size `2^k`;
- the leak correction, and summability of `‖φ_j‖`.
All of this is done in Theorem 9.22, which proves (a); (b) stands.

#### 9.22.1 Conventions and standing facts

**1.1 Fourier analysis.** `\hat f(n) := (1/2π)∫_𝕋 f e^{−inθ}dθ`, `‖f‖_2² := (1/2π)∫_𝕋|f|²`,
`‖f‖_A := Σ_n |\hat f(n)|` (Wiener algebra), `D := d/dθ`, `‖f‖_{H¹} := ‖f‖_2 + ‖Df‖_2`. For
`f ∈ H¹`, by Cauchy–Schwarz and `Σ_{n≠0} n^{−2} = π²/3`,

    ‖f‖_A ≤ |\hat f(0)| + (π/√3)‖Df‖_2 ≤ 2‖f‖_{H¹}.                                   (1.1)

If `f ∈ L²` and `g ∈ A` then `\widehat{fg}(n) = Σ_k \hat f(n−k)\hat g(k)`, absolutely convergent.
`|θ|` denotes the distance from `θ` to `0` in `𝕋`.

**1.2 The weight.** `a(x) := 2M²/(M²+x²)`. Since `a(x) = 2M·Im (x − iM)^{−1}`,
`a^{(r)}(x) = 2M(−1)^r r!·Im (x−iM)^{−r−1}`. Writing `x + iM = ϱe^{iφ}` (`ϱ = (x²+M²)^{1/2}`,
`sin φ = M/ϱ`) and using `|sin((r+1)φ)| ≤ (r+1)|sin φ|`,

    |a^{(r)}(x)| ≤ (r+1)!·a(x)·(x²+M²)^{−r/2} ≤ (r+1)!·a(x)·|x|^{−r}     (x ≠ 0, r ≥ 0).   (1.2)

Also `Σ_{n≥N} a(n) ≤ 2M²/(N−1) ≤ 4M²/N` for `N ≥ 2`, and `Σ_{n≥1} a(n) ≤ π²M²/3`.

**1.3 The model class** (from the construction of Theorem 9.6). Let `Ψ ∈ H¹(𝕋)` be real and even
with `\hat Ψ(0) = 1`, and put `F := (1 − D/M)Ψ ∈ L²` and `m_n := \hat Ψ(n)/a(n)` (`n ≥ 1`). Then
`m_n` is real, `\hat F(0) = 1`, `\hat F(n) = (1 − in/M)a(n)m_n = 2Mm_n/(M+in)` and
`\hat F(−n) = \overline{\hat F(n)}` for `n ≥ 1`; i.e. `F` is the cyclic model with
multiplicities `m_n`, and `Σ m_n²/n² < ∞`. If `Ψ ∈ A` (e.g. `Ψ ∈ H¹`), then

    σ_M² = Σ_{n≥1} m_n 8M²/(M²+n²) = 4Σ_{n≥1} a(n)m_n = 2(Ψ(0) − 1).                    (1.3)

We call `m_n` the *multiplicity* of `Ψ` at `n`, and `\hat ψ(n)/a(n)` the *multiplicity change*
caused by adding an even real `ψ` to `Ψ` ("multiplicity units"). Since `D` is local, if `ψ ∈ H¹`
vanishes on an open set `U` then `(1 − D/M)ψ = 0` a.e. on `U`.

**1.4 Rounding and differences.** `⟨x⟩ := ⌊x + ½⌋ ∈ ℤ`, so `x − ½ < ⟨x⟩ ≤ x + ½`; if `x ≥ ½` then
`⟨x⟩ ≥ 1`. For `k ≥ 0` put `h^{(k)}_i := (−1)^i C(k,i)` (`0 ≤ i ≤ k`), so
`Σ_i h^{(k)}_i z^i = (1 − z)^k` and `Σ_i |h^{(k)}_i| = 2^k`. For a sequence `x` on `ℤ`,
`(h*x)_n := Σ_{i=0}^k h_i x_{n−i}`. `Δf(i) := f(i+1) − f(i)`. Then

    Σ_{i=0}^k h^{(k)}_i f(i) = (−1)^k (Δ^k f)(0),                                         (1.4)
    Δ^k(FG)(0) = Σ_{r=0}^k C(k,r) (Δ^r F)(0)·(Δ^{k−r}G)(r),                               (1.5)

(1.5) because, with `E_F, E_G` the shifts acting on one factor only, `Δ = Δ_F E_G + Δ_G` with
commuting terms. For real `C^r` functions, `Δ^r F(x) = F^{(r)}(ξ)` for some `ξ ∈ [x, x+r]`
(B-spline representation), so `|Δ^r F(x)| ≤ sup_{[x,x+r]} |F^{(r)}|`.

**1.5 Auxiliary functions** (fixed once and for all; nothing depends on `M`, `θ`).
- `s ∈ C^∞(ℝ)`, `s(x) := e^{−1/x}/(e^{−1/x} + e^{−1/(1−x)})` on `(0,1)`, `s = 0` on `(−∞,0]`,
  `s = 1` on `[1,∞)`; `0 ≤ s ≤ 1`, `‖s'‖_∞ = 2` (numerically; only finiteness is used, together
  with the value in the explicit constants). `χ(x) := s(2x − 1)` (`0` on `(−∞,½]`, `1` on
  `[1,∞)`); `X_χ := 1 + ‖χ'‖_∞ + ‖χ''‖_∞`.
- `c_b(x) := e^{−1/(1−4x²)}` on `(−½,½)`, `0` elsewhere; `b := c_b * \tilde c_b`
  (`\tilde c_b(x) = c_b(−x)`). Then `b ∈ C_c^∞((−1,1))`, `b ≥ 0`, even,
  `b(0) = ∫c_b² = ‖b‖_∞`, and `\hat b(ξ) := (1/2π)∫b(x)e^{−iξx}dx = (1/2π)|∫c_b e^{−iξx}|² ≥ 0`,
  so `0 ≤ \hat b(ξ) ≤ \hat b(0)`; `\hat b` is a Schwartz function. Put `m_0(b) := ½∫b`,
  `κ_b := b(0)/m_0(b)`, `κ'_b := ‖b'‖_∞/m_0(b)`,

      Ξ(x) := x² \hat b(πx)/\hat b(0) (x ≥ 0),      C_Ξ := sup_{x>0} Ξ(x)/min(x², x^{−1}) = sup_{x>0} max(1, x³)\hat b(πx)/\hat b(0) < ∞.

  Numerically `C_Ξ ≈ 1.4256`, `κ_b ≈ 2.7005`.

**1.6 Combs** (as in Theorem 9.6). For an integer `N' ≥ 1` and `0 < ρ < 1` let `w := 2πρ/N'`,
`β(θ) := Σ_{ℓ∈ℤ} b(2(θ − 2πℓ/N')/w)`, `g := β/\hat β(0)`. Then `g` is `C^∞`, even, real, `≥ 0`,
`2π/N'`-periodic, supported in a closed set `E` (a union of `N'` closed arcs of length `w`) with
`λ(E) = 2πρ`, and:

    \hat g(n) = 0 for n ∉ N'ℤ,  \hat g(kN') = \hat b(πkρ)/\hat b(0) ∈ [0,1],  \hat g(0) = 1,      (1.6)
    g(0) = ‖g‖_∞ = κ_b/ρ,   ‖Dg‖_∞ = κ'_b N'/(πρ²),   ‖g‖_A = Σ_k \hat b(πkρ)/\hat b(0) < ∞,   (1.7)
    k²\hat g(kN') = ρ^{−2}Ξ(|k|ρ) ≤ C_Ξ ρ^{−2} min((kρ)², (|k|ρ)^{−1})   (k ≠ 0).             (1.8)

(`\hat β(0) = πρ\hat b(0) = ρ m_0(b)`, and `\hat β(kN') = πρ\hat b(πkρ)`, as in Theorem 9.6.)

    θ' := 4/(1+β),   0 < θ' ≤ θ,   θ' < 1.

(For `q = 60`: `τ = 0.098143…`, `β = 3.3491…`, `θ' = 0.9197…`.) Sets:

    J := (2π − 2π/q, 2π)   (the last level-1 cell of the comb (1, q)),
    T := {θ : η < |θ| < 2π/q − η},    J' := J ∩ T = (2π − 2π/q + η, 2π − η),   λ(J') = 3π/(2q).

**(P2) The cutoff `u`.** `u(θ) := s((|θ| − η + σ)/σ)·s((θ_1 − |θ|)/σ)` for `|θ| ≤ π`. Then
`u ∈ C^∞(𝕋)`, `u` is real and even, `0 ≤ u ≤ 1`, `u = 1` on `{η ≤ |θ| ≤ 2π/q − η} ⊃ T`, and
`u = 0` on `{|θ| ≤ σ}` and on `{|θ| ≥ θ_1}`. The two factors make their transitions on the
disjoint sets `{σ ≤ |θ| ≤ 2σ}` and `{2π/q − η ≤ |θ| ≤ θ_1}`, so `‖u'‖_∞ ≤ ‖s'‖_∞/σ = 16q/π`.
Since `u ≡ 0` near `0`, every derivative of `u` vanishes at `0`, hence

    Σ_p p^r \hat u(p) = (−i)^r u^{(r)}(0) = 0     for every r ≥ 0                         (2.1)

(absolutely convergent, `\hat u` being Schwartz). On `supp u ⊂ {|θ| ≤ θ_1}`,
`|e^{iθ} − 1| = 2|sin(θ/2)| ≤ τ`. Constants (finite since `u ∈ C^∞`):

    λ(s) := 1 + (π/√3)(s/τ + ‖u'‖_∞),    U_4 := (1/2π)∫|u^{(4)}|  (so |\hat u(p)| ≤ U_4 p^{−4}, p ≠ 0),
    U^♯ := Σ_p (1+p²)(2+|p|)|\hat u(p)|,    U^O := 2^{7/2}U^♯.

The mean-fixing bump: `w_0(θ) := (8π/(η∫b))·b(4θ/η)` (`|θ| ≤ π`): real, even, `C^∞`, supported in
`{|θ| < η/4} ⊂ {u = 0}` (so `w_0 = 0` on `T`), `\hat w_0(0) = 1`; `W_0 := sup_n (M²+n²)|\hat w_0(n)| < ∞`.

**(P3) Scales.** `K := 4`, `N_j := 4^j`, `B_j := [N_j, N_{j+1}) ∩ ℤ`, `W_j := 2N_{j+1}`,
`N'_j := 4W_j = 8N_{j+1} = 32N_j`, `ρ_0 := 3/(8qζ(3/2))`, `ρ_j := ρ_0(j+1)^{−3/2}`. Let `g_j` be the
comb of §1.6 with `N' = N'_j`, `ρ = ρ_j`, and `E_j` its support. Then

    Σ_{j≥0} λ(E_j) = 2πρ_0 ζ(3/2) = 3π/(4q) = λ(J')/2.                                   (2.2)

**(P4) Orders, offset height.** `S_K := 4/(1−K^{−2}) + (3/2)/(1−K^{−1}) = 94/15`,
`Z := 2064·S_K·C_Ξ` (`= 8·258·S_K·C_Ξ`), `Y_j := Z K² ρ_j^{−2}`, and

`k_j` is well defined, nondecreasing, `2^{k_j} ≤ C_k (j+1)^{θ'}`, and `A < ∞` (Lemma F). None of
`k_j, A` depends on `M`. (Numerically, for `q = 60`: `k_0 = 11`, `k_{100} = 16`, `k_{10^6} = 25`,
`A ≈ 6·10³`.)

**(P5) The threshold `j_0`; depends on `M` and `θ`.** For `j ≥ 0` put

    ε_j := 2^{k_j} U_4 [(1 + M²) + 3C_Ξ ρ_j^{−2}]/N_j³.                                  (2.4)

Let `j_0 ≥ 2` be so large that, with `X := N_{j_0}`:

#### 9.22.3 Lemmas

**Lemma A (the filter is small on `supp u`).** For every integer `s ≥ 0`,

    Λ_s := ‖((e^{iθ} − 1)^s u)^‖_{ℓ¹} ≤ τ^s λ(s),     and     ‖((e^{−iθ} − 1)^s u)^‖_{ℓ¹} = Λ_s .

*Proof.* Let `f_s := (e^{iθ}−1)^s u`. On `supp u`, `|e^{iθ} − 1| ≤ τ` and `0 ≤ u ≤ 1`, so
`‖f_s‖_∞ ≤ τ^s`. Next `f_s' = is e^{iθ}(e^{iθ}−1)^{s−1}u + (e^{iθ}−1)^s u'` (no first term if
`s = 0`), and `supp u' ⊂ supp u`, so `‖f_s'‖_∞ ≤ sτ^{s−1} + τ^s‖u'‖_∞`. By the first inequality in
(1.1), `Λ_s ≤ ‖f_s‖_∞ + (π/√3)‖f_s'‖_∞ ≤ τ^s λ(s)`. Since `u` is even,
`(e^{−iθ}−1)^s u(θ) = f_s(−θ)`, whose coefficients are `\hat f_s(−p)`. ∎

**Lemma B (shaped errors are small on the arc, coefficientwise).** Let `k ≥ 0` and
`N ≥ max(2, 4k/τ)` be integers, let `e : ℤ → [−½, ½]` be supported in a finite subset of
`[N, ∞)`, `d := h^{(k)} * e`, and

    e^Ψ(θ) := Σ_{n≥1} a(n) d_n (e^{inθ} + e^{−inθ}),      v := e^Ψ·u .

Then `|d_n| ≤ 2^{k−1}`, `‖\widehat{e^Ψ}‖_{ℓ¹} ≤ 2^{k+2}M²/N`, and

    sup_{l∈ℤ} |\hat v(l)| ≤ a(N) Σ_{r=0}^k C(k,r)(r+1)! N^{−r} Λ_{k−r} ≤ (16/9)·a(N)·τ^k·λ(k).      (3.1)

*Proof.* `|d_n| ≤ ½Σ_i|h_i| = 2^{k−1}`, and `d` is supported in `[N, ∞)`, so
`‖\widehat{e^Ψ}‖_1 = 2Σ_n a(n)|d_n| ≤ 2^k Σ_{n≥N} a(n) ≤ 2^{k+2}M²/N` (§1.2).

Write `\hat v(l) = Σ_{n≥1} a(n)d_n \hat u(l−n) + Σ_{n≥1} a(n)d_n \hat u(l+n) =: Σ_+ + Σ_−`. Substituting
`n = m + i` in `d_n = Σ_i h_i e_{n−i}`,

    Σ_+ = Σ_m e_m Σ_{i=0}^k h_i F_m(i) G_m(i),     F_m(i) := a(m+i),   G_m(i) := \hat u(l − m − i).

By (1.4) and (1.5) the inner sum is `(−1)^k Σ_{r=0}^k C(k,r)(Δ^r F_m)(0)(Δ^{k−r}G_m)(r)`.
- *Weights (the commutator).* For `m ∈ supp e`, `m ≥ N`, and by §1.4 and (1.2),
  `|(Δ^r F_m)(0)| ≤ sup_{x∈[m,m+r]} |a^{(r)}(x)| ≤ (r+1)! a(N) N^{−r}` (`a(x)x^{−r}` decreases on `x > 0`).
- *The filter.* `(Δ^t G_m)(r) = Σ_{s'=0}^t C(t,s')(−1)^{t−s'} \hat u(p − s')` with `p := l − m − r`;
  this is the `p`-th Fourier coefficient of `(e^{iθ} − 1)^t u`. As `m` runs over `ℤ`, so does `p`;
  hence `Σ_m |(Δ^t G_m)(r)| = Λ_t`.

With `|e_m| ≤ ½`: `|Σ_+| ≤ ½ Σ_r C(k,r)(r+1)! a(N)N^{−r} Λ_{k−r}`. For `Σ_−` (the
cos-symmetrisation) take `G_m(i) := \hat u(l + m + i)`: then
`(Δ^t G_m)(r) = Σ_{s'} C(t,s')(−1)^{t−s'}\hat u(p + s')`, `p := l + m + r`, the `p`-th coefficient of
`(e^{−iθ} − 1)^t u`, whose `ℓ¹` norm is also `Λ_t` (Lemma A). Adding gives the first inequality
in (3.1). For the second, `Λ_{k−r} ≤ τ^{k−r}λ(k−r) ≤ τ^{k−r}λ(k)` (Lemma A; `λ` increases) and
`C(k,r)(r+1)! = (r+1)k!/(k−r)! ≤ (r+1)k^r`; since `k/(Nτ) ≤ ¼`,
`Σ_r (r+1)(k/(Nτ))^r ≤ Σ_{r≥0}(r+1)4^{−r} = 16/9`. ∎

*Comment.* This is the rigorous form of item 5 of the design: `(e^Ψ u)^` is controlled by
`sup|\hat{e^Ψ}|·‖(Hu)^‖_{ℓ¹}`. The terms `r ≥ 1` are the commutator with the weights `a(n)`;
they are harmless once `N ≫ k/τ`.

**Lemma F (the orders `k_j`).**
- (a) `φ(k) := (2/τ)^k/λ(k)` is strictly increasing on `k ≥ 0`. Hence
  `{k ≥ 1 : Y_jτ^kλ(k) ≤ 2^k} = {k ≥ 1 : φ(k) ≥ Y_j}` is a nonempty up-set, `k_j` is well defined,
  `j ↦ k_j` is nondecreasing, and `Y_j τ^{k_j} λ(k_j) ≤ 2^{k_j}`.
- (b) `k_j ≤ 1 + log(Λ^* Y_j)/log(1/τ)`, where `Λ^* := λ(0) + π/(√3 τ)`.
- (c) `2^{k_j} ≤ 2 (Y_j λ(k_j))^{1/(1+β)}`.
- (d) There is `C_k = C_k(q) < ∞` with `2^{k_j} ≤ C_k (j+1)^{θ'}` for all `j ≥ 0`. Hence
  `A ≤ (2C_k + 1)(2/log 4)^{θ'} < ∞`.

*Proof.* (a) `λ(k+1) = λ(k) + π/(√3τ)` and `λ(k) ≥ 1`, so
`φ(k+1)/φ(k) = (2/τ)λ(k)/λ(k+1) ≥ (2/τ)/(1 + π/(√3τ)) = 2/(τ + π/√3) > 1`, because `τ < 1/10` and
`π/√3 < 1.814`. `φ(k) → ∞`, and `Y_j = ZK²ρ_0^{−2}(j+1)³` increases with `j`.
(b) `λ(k) = λ(0) + πk/(√3τ) ≤ Λ^*(1+k) ≤ Λ^* 2^k`, so `φ(k) ≥ τ^{−k}/Λ^*`, which is `≥ Y_j` once
`k ≥ log(Λ^*Y_j)/log(1/τ)` (note `Λ^*Y_j ≥ 1`).
(c) If `k_j = 1`, then `2^{k_j} = 2 ≤ 2(Y_jλ(1))^{1/(1+β)}`. If `k_j ≥ 2`, minimality gives
`Y_jτ^{k_j−1}λ(k_j−1) > 2^{k_j−1}`, i.e. `2^{(1+β)(k_j−1)} = (2/τ)^{k_j−1} < Y_jλ(k_j−1) ≤ Y_jλ(k_j)`.
(d) By (b), `λ(k_j) ≤ λ(0) + (π/(√3τ))(1 + log(Λ^*Y_0(j+1)³)/log(1/τ)) ≤ C'(j+1)` with `C' = C'(q)`.
By (c), `2^{k_j} ≤ 2(Y_0C')^{1/(1+β)}(j+1)^{4/(1+β)} =: C_k(j+1)^{θ'}`. Finally
`(2^{k_J+1}+1)/(J log 4)^{θ'} ≤ (2C_k+1)((J+1)/(J log 4))^{θ'} ≤ (2C_k+1)(2/log 4)^{θ'}`. ∎

**Lemma E (summation over scales; as in part (c) of the proof of Theorem 9.6).** Let `n ≥ 1`,
`I ⊂ ℤ_{≥0}`, and for `j ∈ I` let `k^*_j ≥ 1` be an integer with `|n − k^*_j N'_j| ≤ N'_j/2`.
Then for all weights `w_j ≥ 0`,

    Σ_{j∈I} w_j Ξ(k^*_j ρ_j) ≤ C_Ξ S_K sup_{j∈I} w_j .

*Proof.* Put `x_j := n/N'_j`, `y_j := x_jρ_j`. From `|n − k^*_jN'_j| ≤ N'_j/2` and `k^*_j ≥ 1`:
`x_j ≥ ½`, `k^*_j ≤ x_j + ½ ≤ 2x_j` and `k^*_j ≥ max(1, x_j − ½) ≥ 2x_j/3`. So
`z_j := k^*_jρ_j ∈ [2y_j/3, 2y_j]` and `Ξ(z_j) ≤ C_Ξ min(z_j², z_j^{−1}) ≤ C_Ξ min(4y_j², 3/(2y_j))`.
For `j < j'`: `y_j/y_{j'} = K^{j'−j}ρ_j/ρ_{j'} ≥ K^{j'−j}`. So distinct `y_j` differ by factors
`≥ K`: those `≤ 1` contribute `≤ 4Σ_{t≥0}K^{−2t}`, those `> 1` contribute `≤ (3/2)Σ_{t≥0}K^{−t}`. ∎

**Lemma O (the offset leak; item 4 of the design).** For every `l ∈ ℤ`,

    (M² + l²)|\widehat{Ou}(l)| ≤ Φ (M²+4) U^O X^{−3/2},      Φ := 44 M² X_χ A .            (3.2)

Consequently `|μ_O| ≤ 44X_χA(M²+4)U^O X^{−3/2}`, and for every `n ≥ 1`,
`|\widehat{Ou}(n)|/a(n) + |μ_O||\hat w_0(n)|/a(n) ≤ ε^O` (the quantity in (J3)).

*Proof.* (1) *Derivatives.* For `x ≥ X/2 ≥ 3` we have `log x ≥ 1`, and with `0 < θ' < 1` the function
`L(x) := (log x)^{θ'}` satisfies `|L'| ≤ (log x)^{θ'}/x` and `|L''| ≤ 2(log x)^{θ'}/x²`. The factor
`χ_X(x) := χ(x/X)` has `|χ_X^{(t)}(x)| = X^{−t}|χ^{(t)}(x/X)| ≤ ‖χ^{(t)}‖_∞ x^{−t}` for `t ≥ 1`, because
`χ^{(t)}(x/X) ≠ 0` forces `x < X`. By Leibniz, for `t ≤ 2`,
`|c^{(t)}(x)| ≤ 2X_χ A (log x)^{θ'} x^{−t} ≤ 2X_χ A x^{1/2−t}` (using `log x ≤ x^{1/2}`). With (1.2),
`|a^{(s)}(x)| ≤ (s+1)!·2M²x^{−2−s}`, so for `|x| ≥ X/2` and `r ≤ 2`,
`|f^{(r)}(x)| ≤ 4M²X_χA Σ_{s≤r} C(r,s)(s+1)! |x|^{−3/2−r} ≤ Φ|x|^{−3/2−r}`
(`Σ_s C(2,s)(s+1)! = 11`). And `f ∈ C^∞(ℝ)` is even and vanishes on `[−X/2, X/2]`.

(2) *Vanishing moments.* `Σ_n|f(n)| < ∞` and `\hat u ∈ ℓ¹`, so `\widehat{Ou}(l) = Σ_p f(l−p)\hat u(p)`.
By (2.1) with `r = 0, 1`, `\widehat{Ou}(l) = Σ_p R(l,p)\hat u(p)` with
`R(l,p) := f(l−p) − f(l) + p f'(l)`.
- If `|p| ≤ |l|/2`: `R = (p²/2)f''(ξ)` with `ξ` between `l−p` and `l`, so `|ξ| ≥ |l|/2`, and
  `f''(ξ) = 0` unless `|ξ| ≥ X/2`. Hence `|R| ≤ (p²/2)Φ max(|l|/2, X/2)^{−7/2}`, and with
  `M²+l² ≤ (M²+1)max(|l|,X)²` this part contributes `≤ 2^{5/2}(M²+1)Φ X^{−3/2} Σ_p p²|\hat u(p)|`.
- If `|p| > |l|/2`: `|R| ≤ 2 sup|f| + |p| sup|f'| ≤ Φ(X/2)^{−3/2}(2+|p|)`, and
  `M² + l² < M² + 4p² ≤ (M²+4)(1+p²)`; this part contributes `≤ 2^{3/2}(M²+4)Φ X^{−3/2} U^♯`.

Since `2^{5/2} + 2^{3/2} ≤ 2^{7/2}`, (3.2) follows. (3) `|\widehat{Ou}(n)|/a(n) = (M²+n²)|\widehat{Ou}(n)|/(2M²)`;
`|μ_O| = |\widehat{Ou}(0)|` is (3.2) at `l = 0` divided by `M²`; and `|\hat w_0(n)|/a(n) ≤ W_0/(2M²)`.
Adding: `22X_χA(M²+4)U^O X^{−3/2}(1 + W_0/M²) = ε^O`. ∎

*Comment.* The leak is tiny because `u` vanishes near `0`: the Taylor polynomial of `f` is
annihilated by `\hat u` (2.1). Two moments suffice here; the remark's "all moments vanish" is
used only through `r = 0, 1`.

**Lemma D (one round-up step; Lemma 9.7 with `N'_j = 8N_{j+1}`).** Let `j ≥ 0`,
`δm : B_j → [0,1)`, `e^Ψ_j := Σ_{n∈B_j} a(n)δm_n·2cos(nθ)`, `ψ_j := e^Ψ_j g_j`. Then:

- (ii) `\hat ψ_j(n) = \widehat{e^Ψ_j}(n)` for `|n| < 7N_{j+1}`: the multiplicity change is `δm_n` on

- (iii) for `n ≥ 7N_{j+1}`: `0 ≤ \hat ψ_j(n)/a(n) ≤ 82K²ρ_j^{−2}Ξ(k^*ρ_j)`, where, if
  `\hat ψ_j(n) ≠ 0`, `k^* ≥ 1` is the unique integer with `|n − k^*N'_j| < N_{j+1}`;
- (iv) `ψ_j(0) = e^Ψ_j(0)κ_b/ρ_j ∈ [0, 4M²κ_bρ_j^{−1}Σ_{n∈B_j} n^{−2}]`, and `ψ_j ∈ H¹`.

*Proof.* (i) §1.6. (ii) `\hat ψ_j(n) = Σ_k \widehat{e^Ψ_j}(n − kN'_j)\hat g_j(kN'_j)` and
`\widehat{e^Ψ_j}` lives on `±B_j ⊂ (−N_{j+1}, N_{j+1})`; for `|n| < 7N_{j+1}` and `k ≠ 0`,
`|n − kN'_j| > N_{j+1}`. (iii) The translates `kN'_j ± B_j` are pairwise disjoint (`N'_j > 2N_{j+1}`),
so at most one term is nonzero, with `|n − kN'_j| < N_{j+1}`; `k ≠ 0` since `n ≥ 7N_{j+1}`. That term
is `a(|n−kN'_j|)δm\,\hat g_j(kN'_j) ∈ [0, a(N_j)\hat g_j(kN'_j)]`. Now
`a(N_j)/a(n) ≤ 1 + (n/N_j)²` and `n ≤ kN'_j + N_{j+1} = (8k+1)KN_j ≤ 9kKN_j`, so
`a(N_j)/a(n) ≤ 1 + 81K²k² ≤ 82K²k²`; conclude with (1.8). (iv) `g_j(0) = κ_b/ρ_j` (1.7) and
`0 ≤ e^Ψ_j(0) = 2Σ_{B_j}a(n)δm_n ≤ 4M²Σ_{B_j}n^{−2}`. ∎

**Lemma C (one modified step).** Let `j ≥ j_0`, `k := k_j`, and let `e : ℤ → [−½,½]` be supported in
`B_j`. Let `d := h^{(k)} * e` (supported in `[N_j, N_{j+1}+k) ⊂ [N_j, W_j)` by (J1)),
`e^Ψ_j := Σ_{n≥1} a(n)d_n(e^{inθ} + e^{−inθ})`, `v_j := e^Ψ_j u`,

    L_j(n) := Σ_{k'≠0} \hat v_j(n − k'N'_j)\hat g_j(k'N'_j)   (|n| < W_j),      P_j := Σ_{|n|<W_j} L_j(n)e^{inθ},
    ψ_j := e^Ψ_j (1 − u) + (v_j − P_j) g_j .

Then:
- (i) `ψ_j` is `C^∞`, real, even, and `ψ_j = 0` on the open set `T ∖ E_j`.
- (ii) `\hat ψ_j(n) = \widehat{e^Ψ_j}(n)` for `|n| < W_j`. In multiplicity units the change is `d_n`
  for `1 ≤ n < W_j` (i.e. on `B_j` and on the *spill zone* `[N_{j+1}, N_{j+1}+k)`, and `0` elsewhere
  below `W_j`), and `\hat ψ_j(0) = 0`.
- (iii) For `n ≥ W_j` and `k^*` a nearest integer to `n/N'_j`:

      |\hat ψ_j(n)|/a(n) ≤ 258 K² τ^k λ(k) ρ_j^{−2} Ξ(k^*ρ_j)·1[k^* ≥ 1] + ε_j .            (3.3)

- (iv) `ψ_j(0) = e^Ψ_j(0) − P_j(0)κ_b/ρ_j`, and `|ψ_j(0)| ≤ 2^{k+2}M²/N_j + 2W_j ℓ_j κ_b/ρ_j`, where
  `ℓ_j := sup_{|n|<W_j}|L_j(n)| ≤ 440·2^k M² U_4 N_j^{−1} N'^{−4}_j`.
- (v) `‖ψ_j‖_{H¹} ≤ C_H 2^k ρ_j^{−2} N_j^{−1/2}`, with
  `C_H := M²[(5 + 3‖u'‖_∞)(1 + κ_b) + 24κ'_b + 0.02U_4(κ_b + κ'_b)]`.

*Proof.* *Step 0 (two bounds on `\hat v_j`).* By Lemma B (applicable: `N_j ≥ max(2, 4k/τ)` by (J1),
(J2)), `sup_l |\hat v_j(l)| ≤ V_j := (16/9)a(N_j)τ^kλ(k)` and `‖\widehat{e^Ψ_j}‖_1 ≤ E_j := 2^{k+2}M²/N_j`.
`\widehat{e^Ψ_j}` lives in `(−W_j, W_j)`; for `|l| ≥ 2W_j` and `|m| < W_j`, `|l − m| ≥ |l|/2`, so

    |\hat v_j(l)| ≤ 16 E_j U_4 |l|^{−4}       (|l| ≥ 2W_j).                                  (3.4)

*The leak.* For `|n| < W_j` and `k' ≠ 0`: `|n − k'N'_j| ≥ |k'|N'_j − W_j ≥ (3/4)|k'|N'_j ≥ 3W_j`. By
(3.4) and `\hat g_j ≤ 1`: `|L_j(n)| ≤ 16E_jU_4(4/3)^4·2ζ(4)N'^{−4}_j ≤ 110E_jU_4N'^{−4}_j`, which is the
bound on `ℓ_j`.

(i) `e^Ψ_j` is a real even trigonometric polynomial; `u`, `g_j` are real, even, `C^∞`; `\hat v_j`,
`\hat g_j` are real and even, so `L_j(−n) = L_j(n) ∈ ℝ` and `P_j` is a real even trigonometric
polynomial. On `T`, `u = 1`, so `ψ_j = (v_j − P_j)g_j`, which vanishes off `E_j`; `E_j` is closed.

(ii) By §1.1 and `\hat g_j(0) = 1`,

    \hat ψ_j(n) = \widehat{e^Ψ_j}(n) − \hat P_j(n) + Σ_{k'≠0}(\hat v_j − \hat P_j)(n − k'N'_j)\hat g_j(k'N'_j).   (3.5)

For `|n| < W_j`: `Σ_{k'≠0}\hat v_j(n−k'N'_j)\hat g_j(k'N'_j) = L_j(n) = \hat P_j(n)`, and
`\hat P_j(n − k'N'_j) = 0` for `k' ≠ 0` because `|n − k'N'_j| ≥ 3W_j`. So `\hat ψ_j(n) = \widehat{e^Ψ_j}(n)`.
(This is the exact leak removal; the second comb of the design is not needed, see §7.)

(iii) For `|n| ≥ W_j`, `\widehat{e^Ψ_j}(n) = \hat P_j(n) = 0` in (3.5). Let `n ≥ W_j` and
`r := n − k^*N'_j ∈ [−N'_j/2, N'_j/2]`.
- *Main term* (`k' = k^*`, if `k^* ≥ 1`): by `V_j`,
  `|\hat v_j(r)|\hat g_j(k^*N'_j)/a(n) ≤ (16/9)τ^kλ(k)·[(M²+n²)/(M²+N_j²)]·\hat g_j(k^*N'_j)`. Here
  `n ≤ (k^*+½)N'_j = 32(k^*+½)N_j`, so `(M²+n²)/(M²+N_j²) ≤ 1 + 1024(k^*+½)² ≤ 1 + 2304k^{*2} ≤ 145K²k^{*2}`.
  With (1.8) the main term is `≤ (16/9)·145K²τ^kλ(k)ρ_j^{−2}Ξ(k^*ρ_j) ≤ 258K²τ^kλ(k)ρ_j^{−2}Ξ(k^*ρ_j)`.
- *Tails* (`k' ∉ {0, k^*}`): `l_{k'} := n − k'N'_j = r − (k'−k^*)N'_j` has
  `|l_{k'}| ≥ (|k'−k^*| − ½)N'_j ≥ 2W_j`, so (3.4) applies. Using `n² ≤ 2l² + 2k'²N'^2_j`,
  `\hat g_j ≤ 1` and (1.8), `(M²+n²)\hat g_j(k'N'_j) ≤ 2(M² + l_{k'}²) + 2C_ΞN'^2_jρ_j^{−2}`. With
  `(M²+l²)l^{−4} ≤ (1+M²)l^{−2}`, `Σ_{t≠0}|r − tN'|^{−2} ≤ π²/N'²` and `Σ_{t≠0}|r−tN'|^{−4} ≤ π⁴/(3N'^4)`
  (compare with `2Σ_{t≥1}((t−½)N')^{−2}` and `^{−4}`), the tails contribute
  `≤ (16E_jU_4/(M²N'^2_j))[(1+M²)π² + (π⁴/3)C_Ξρ_j^{−2}] = (2^kU_4/(16N_j³))[(1+M²)π² + (π⁴/3)C_Ξρ_j^{−2}]`.
- *Leak copies* (the `\hat P_j` terms): `\hat P_j(n − k'N'_j) ≠ 0` needs `|n − k'N'_j| < W_j = N'_j/4`,
  so `k' = k^* ≠ 0` and `n ≤ (5/4)k^*N'_j`. They contribute
  `≤ ℓ_j(M²+n²)\hat g_j(k^*N'_j)/(2M²) ≤ ℓ_j[½ + (25/32)C_ΞN'^2_jρ_j^{−2}/M²]
  ≤ 2^kU_4N_j^{−3}[2.1·10^{−4}M² + 0.34C_Ξρ_j^{−2}]` (insert `ℓ_j` and `N'_j = 32N_j`).
- If `k^* = 0` (i.e. `n ≤ N'_j/2`), all `k' ≠ 0` are tails and there are no leak copies.

Adding, the non-main part is `≤ 2^kU_4N_j^{−3}[0.62(1+M²) + 2.4C_Ξρ_j^{−2}] ≤ ε_j`.

(iv) `u(0) = 0` gives `v_j(0) = 0`, so `ψ_j(0) = e^Ψ_j(0) − P_j(0)g_j(0)` with `g_j(0) = κ_b/ρ_j`;
`|e^Ψ_j(0)| ≤ E_j` and `|P_j(0)| ≤ 2W_jℓ_j`.

(v) `‖e^Ψ_j‖_2² = 2Σ a(n)²d_n² ≤ 2·4^{k−1}·4M⁴Σ_{n≥N_j}n^{−4} ≤ (16/3)4^kM⁴N_j^{−3}` and
`‖De^Ψ_j‖_2² ≤ 2·4^{k−1}·4M⁴Σ_{n≥N_j}n^{−2} ≤ 4·4^kM⁴N_j^{−1}`. So `‖e^Ψ_j‖_2 ≤ 2.31·2^kM²N_j^{−3/2}`,
`‖De^Ψ_j‖_2 ≤ 2·2^kM²N_j^{−1/2}`, `‖P_j‖_2 ≤ (2W_j)^{1/2}ℓ_j ≤ 0.0017·2^kM²U_4N_j^{−9/2}`,
`‖DP_j‖_2 ≤ W_j(2W_j)^{1/2}ℓ_j ≤ 0.014·2^kM²U_4N_j^{−7/2}`. With `‖FG‖_2 ≤ ‖F‖_2‖G‖_∞`, `0 ≤ u ≤ 1`
and (1.7):

    ‖ψ_j‖_2 ≤ ‖e^Ψ_j‖ + (‖e^Ψ_j‖ + ‖P_j‖)κ_b/ρ_j,
    ‖Dψ_j‖_2 ≤ ‖De^Ψ_j‖ + ‖u'‖_∞‖e^Ψ_j‖ + (‖De^Ψ_j‖ + ‖u'‖_∞‖e^Ψ_j‖ + ‖DP_j‖)κ_b/ρ_j + (‖e^Ψ_j‖ + ‖P_j‖)κ'_bN'_j/(πρ_j²).

Inserting the bounds (`N'_j = 32N_j`, `ρ_j ≤ 1`, `N_j ≥ 2`) gives (v). ∎

*Comment (cost of the copies).* (3.3) says that the copies of step `j` cost, in multiplicity
units, `≍ K²ρ_j^{−2}·τ^{k_j}λ(k_j)`: the factor `(K/ρ_j)²` of Theorem 9.6 times the size of the
shaped error **on `supp u`**, not the size of the individual errors. This is the mechanism of
Remark 9.21, now with all constants.

#### 9.22.4 The construction

**4.1 Base (comb `(1,q)` of Example 9.2 type).** Put `v_q := 2πM/q`,

    A_0 := 2π/(1 − e^{−2πM}),   A_1 := (2π/q)e^{2πM(q−1)/q}/(1 − e^{−v_q}),   b_0 := A_1/(2(A_1 − A_0)),   b_1 := ½ − b_0.

*Lemma 4.1.* `A_1/A_0 = (1/q)Σ_{s=0}^{q−1} e^{s v_q} ≥ 1 + (q−1)v_q/2 > 1`; hence
`½ < b_0 ≤ q/(2π(q−1)M) + ½`. With `m^{base}_n := b_0` (`q ∤ n`) and `m^{base}_n := b_0 + b_1 = ½`
(`q | n`), the cyclic model `F_0` vanishes on `J`.

*Proof.* `A_1/A_0 = (1/q)e^{(q−1)v_q}(1 − e^{−qv_q})/(1 − e^{−v_q}) = (1/q)Σ_{s<q}e^{sv_q}`, and
`e^{x} ≥ 1 + x`. Then `b_0 = 1/(2(1 − A_0/A_1))` and
`1 − A_0/A_1 ≥ ((q−1)v_q/2)/(1 + (q−1)v_q/2)`. By Lemma 9.0 (`N = (1,q)`, `q ≥ 2`) and the cell
computation in the proof of Theorem 9.1 (level-1 cell `J`, left ends `x_0 = 0`,
`x_1 = 2π(q−1)/q`), `F_0 = 1 − 2(b_0+b_1) + 2M(b_0A_0 + b_1A_1)e^{−Mt}` on `J`; both brackets
vanish. (Exactly as in Example 9.2, which is the case `q = 3`.) ∎

Let `Ψ_0` be even with `\hat Ψ_0(0) = 1`, `\hat Ψ_0(±n) = a(n)m^{base}_n`; `Ψ_0 ∈ H¹`, and
`(1 − D/M)Ψ_0 = F_0` by §1.3.

**4.2 Step −1: the offset, in one piece, before any rounding.**

    Ψ^{(0)} := Ψ_0 + O·(1 − u) + μ_O w_0 .

*Lemma 4.2.* `Ψ^{(0)} ∈ H¹` is real and even, `\hat Ψ^{(0)}(0) = 1`, `(1−D/M)Ψ^{(0)} = 0` a.e. on
`J'`, and the multiplicity of `Ψ^{(0)}` at `n ≥ 1` is `m^{base}_n + c(n) + ε^O_n` with
`|ε^O_n| ≤ ε^O ≤ 1/16`.

*Proof.* `|f(n)| ≤ 2M²A(log|n|)^{θ′}n^{−2}`, so `Σ n²f(n)² < ∞` and `O ∈ H¹`; `O` is real and even; so is `O(1−u) + μ_Ow_0`
(`μ_O ∈ ℝ` as `Ou` is real and even). Mean: `\hat O(0) = f(0) = 0`, so the mean of `O(1−u)` is
`−μ_O`, cancelled by `μ_O\hat w_0(0) = μ_O`. `O(1−u)` and `w_0` vanish on the open set `T`, and
`F_0 = 0` on `J`; so `(1−D/M)Ψ^{(0)} = 0` a.e. on `J ∩ T = J'`. The coefficient at `n ≥ 1` is
`a(n)m^{base}_n + a(n)c(n) − \widehat{Ou}(n) + μ_O\hat w_0(n)`; Lemma O and (J3). ∎

**4.3 Steps `j = 0, 1, …` .** For `J ≥ 0` write `Ψ^{(J)} := Ψ^{(0)} + Σ_{i<J}ψ_i` and
`m^{(J)}_n := \hat Ψ^{(J)}(n)/a(n)` (the current real *target* at `n`).
- *Low steps `j < j_0` (round-up, Theorem 9.6).* `δm_n := ⌈m^{(j)}_n⌉ − m^{(j)}_n` for `n ∈ B_j`;
  `ψ_j := e^Ψ_j g_j` as in Lemma D; `m_n := ⌈m^{(j)}_n⌉` for `n ∈ B_j`.
- *Shaped steps `j ≥ j_0`.* With `k := k_j`, `h := h^{(k)}`, for `n = N_j, N_j+1, …, N_{j+1}−1` in
  increasing order:

      y_n := m^{(j)}_n + Σ_{i=1}^{min(k, n−N_j)} h_i e_{n−i},    m_n := ⟨y_n⟩,    e_n := m_n − y_n ∈ (−½, ½];   (4.1)

  `e_n := 0` for `n ∉ B_j`; `d^{(j)} := h * e`; `ψ_j` as in Lemma C (with this `e`).

  For `n ∈ B_j`, `d^{(j)}_n = e_n + Σ_{i≥1, n−i∈B_j} h_i e_{n−i} = m_n − m^{(j)}_n`.

*The band-of-origin rule.* The feedback in (4.1) uses only errors of the same band. The error
sequence `e1_{B_j}` of band `j` is filtered **in full** by `h^{(k_j)}`; the piece `d^{(j)}_n` with
`n ∈ [N_{j+1}, N_{j+1}+k_j)` (the *spill*) is not cut off: by Lemma C(ii) step `j` adds it, as a
real change, to the targets of band `j+1`, which are rounded at step `j+1`. Summing over steps,
for `n ≥ N_{j_0}`,

    m_n − (all real contributions at n) = Σ_{j≥j_0} (h^{(k_j)} * (e 1_{B_j}))_n ,

which is the identity `δm − (offset) = Σ_j h^{(j)} * (e1_{B_j})` required in the design. Each
summand has analytic symbol `(1 − e^{iθ})^{k_j}Σ_{n∈B_j}e_ne^{inθ}`, which is what Lemma B uses. No
unshaped boundary terms occur.

#### 9.22.5 Proof of Theorem 9.22

**5.1 Bookkeeping of the coefficient at `n ∈ B_J`.**
- (a) *Later steps do not touch `n`.* For `i > J`, `n < N_{J+1} ≤ N_i`. If `i < j_0`, Lemma D(ii)
  (`n < 7N_{i+1}`) and `\widehat{e^Ψ_i} = 0` on `(−N_i, N_i)`; if `i ≥ j_0`, Lemma C(ii) (`n < W_i`)
  and `\widehat{e^Ψ_i} = 0` on `(−N_i, N_i)`. So `\hat ψ_i(n) = 0`.
- (b) *Step `J` makes it an integer.* Lemma D(ii) gives `m^{(J+1)}_n = m^{(J)}_n + δm_n = ⌈m^{(J)}_n⌉`
  (low `J`); Lemma C(ii) gives `m^{(J+1)}_n = m^{(J)}_n + d^{(J)}_n = m_n` (shaped `J`).
- (c) *The target.* By Lemma 4.2, with `μ^{(i)}_n := \hat ψ_i(n)/a(n)`,

      m^{(J)}_n = m^{base}_n + c(n) + ε^O_n + Σ_{i<min(J,j_0)} μ^{(i)}_n + Σ_{j_0≤i<J} μ^{(i)}_n .      (5.1)

  *Low copies.* For `i < J` with `i < j_0`: `n ∉ B_i` and `n ≥ N_{i+1}`, so by Lemma D(ii),(iii)
  `μ^{(i)}_n ≥ 0`, nonzero only if `n ≥ 7N_{i+1}` and `|n − k^*N'_i| < N_{i+1} ≤ N'_i/2`. Lemma E
  (weights `82K²ρ_i^{−2} ≤ 82K²ρ_{j_0−1}^{−2}`) gives

      0 ≤ Σ_{i<min(J,j_0)} μ^{(i)}_n ≤ X_low := 82 K² C_Ξ S_K ρ_{j_0−1}^{−2} .                  (5.2)

  *Shaped copies.* Let `J ≥ j_0 + 1` and `j_0 ≤ i < J`.
  - `i = J − 1`: `n ∈ [N_J, 4N_J)`, `W_{J−1} = 2N_J`, `N'_{J−1}/2 = 4N_J`. If `n < 2N_J`, Lemma C(ii):
    `μ^{(J−1)}_n = d^{(J−1)}_n` (spill), `|·| ≤ 2^{k_{J−1}−1}`. If `n ≥ 2N_J`, `k^* = 0` in (3.3):
    `|μ^{(J−1)}_n| ≤ ε_{J−1}`.
  - `i ≤ J − 2`: `n ≥ N_J ≥ 4N_{i+1} > W_i`, so (3.3) applies.

  By (2.3) and Lemma F(a), `258K²τ^{k_i}λ(k_i)ρ_i^{−2} = (258/Z)Y_iτ^{k_i}λ(k_i) ≤ (258/Z)2^{k_i} ≤
  (258/Z)2^{k_J}`. Lemma E over `I_n := {i ∈ [j_0, J−2] : k^*_i ≥ 1}`, `Z = 8·258·S_KC_Ξ` and (J4):

      |Σ_{j_0≤i<J} μ^{(i)}_n| ≤ 2^{k_{J−1}−1} + 2^{k_J}/8 + 1/16 ≤ 2^{k_J−1} + 2^{k_J}/8 + 1/16 .          (5.3)

  (For `J = j_0` the sum is empty.)

None of these bounds uses the actual values of the targets; they use only `|e_n| ≤ ½`,
`δm_n ∈ [0,1)` and the parameters. So no induction hypothesis is needed. The order of the steps
only guarantees that every target is a well-defined real number when it is rounded.

**5.2 Positivity, integrality and growth.**
- *Low bands `J < j_0`.* By (5.1), (5.2), `m^{base}_n ≥ ½`, `c ≥ 0`, `|ε^O_n| ≤ 1/16`:
  `m^{(J)}_n ≥ 7/16`, so `m_n = ⌈m^{(J)}_n⌉ ≥ 1`, and `m_n ≤ m^{(J)}_n + 1 ≤ b_0 + c(n) + X_low + 2`.
- *Shaped bands `J ≥ j_0`.* By (5.1)–(5.3) and (2.5),
  `m^{(J)}_n ≥ ½ + c(n) − 1/16 − 2^{k_J−1} − 2^{k_J}/8 − 1/16 ≥ c(n) − (5/8)2^{k_J} ≥ (11/8)2^{k_J} + 1`.
  In (4.1), `|Σ_{i≥1}h_ie_{n−i}| ≤ ½(2^{k_J} − 1)`, so `y_n ≥ m^{(J)}_n − 2^{k_J−1} + ½ ≥ ½`, and
  `m_n = ⟨y_n⟩ ≥ 1`. Also `m_n ≤ y_n + ½ ≤ m^{(J)}_n + 2^{k_J−1} ≤ b_0 + c(n) + X_low + 1/8 + (9/8)2^{k_J}
  ≤ b_0 + X_low + 1 + 2c(n)`, by (2.5).
- *Integrality.* By 5.1(a),(b), the final coefficient at `n ∈ B_J` is `a(n)m_n` with `m_n ∈ ℤ`.
- *Growth.* `c(n) ≤ A(log n)^{θ'}`, `θ' ≤ θ` and `log(n+2) ≥ log 3 > 1`. So for all `n ≥ 1`

      1 ≤ m_n ≤ b_0 + X_low + 2 + 2A(log n)^{θ'} ≤ C(M,θ)(log(n+2))^θ,    C(M,θ) := b_0 + X_low + 2 + 2A.     (5.4)

  (`b_0` depends on `M` directly (Lemma 4.1), `X_low` through `j_0`; `A` depends on `θ` only.)

**5.3 Convergence, the limit, vanishing, measure.**
- *Convergence.* By Lemma C(v) and Lemma F(d),
  `Σ_{i≥j_0}‖ψ_i‖_{H¹} ≤ C_HC_kρ_0^{−2}Σ_i(i+1)^{θ'+3}2^{−i} < ∞`; the finitely many low `ψ_i` are in
  `H¹`. So `Ψ^{(J)} → Ψ_∞` in `H¹`, hence uniformly (1.1), and
  `F^{(J)} := (1 − D/M)Ψ^{(J)} → F_∞ := (1 − D/M)Ψ_∞` in `L²`
  (`‖(1 − D/M)φ‖_2 ≤ ‖φ‖_2 + ‖Dφ‖_2/M`).
- *The limit is the cyclic model with multiplicities `m_n`.* `Ψ_∞` is real and even;
  `\hat Ψ_∞(0) = 1` because every `ψ_i` has mean `0` (Lemma D(ii), C(ii)) and
  `\hat Ψ^{(0)}(0) = 1`; and `\hat Ψ_∞(n) = \hat Ψ^{(J+1)}(n) = a(n)m_n` for `n ∈ B_J` (5.1(a),(b)).
  Apply §1.3.
- *Vanishing.* Put `S := J' ∖ ∪_{j≥0}E_j`. `F^{(0)} = 0` a.e. on `J'` (Lemma 4.2). Each
  `(1−D/M)ψ_j` vanishes a.e. on an open set containing `S`: `𝕋 ∖ E_j` for low `j` (Lemma D(i)),
  `T ∖ E_j ⊃ J' ∖ E_j` for shaped `j` (Lemma C(i)); §1.3. So every `F^{(J)}` vanishes a.e. on `S`,
  and so does the `L²` limit `F_∞`, since a subsequence converges a.e.
- *Measure.* By (2.2), `λ(S) ≥ λ(J') − Σ_jλ(E_j) = 3π/(2q) − 3π/(4q) = 3π/(4q)`.
  `S ⊂ J' = (2π − 7π/(4q), 2π − π/(4q))`. `S` has empty interior: `E_j` contains the grid
  `(2π/N'_j)ℤ`, so it meets every arc of length `> 2π/N'_j`.

This proves Theorem 9.22 with the multiplicities `m_n` and the constant `C(M,θ)` of (5.4). ∎

#### 9.22.6 Proof of Corollary 9.23

(i) `[T, T+1]` contains at most two integers `n`, each with `n + 2 ≤ T + 3`, so by (5.4) and
`θ ≤ 1`, `log(T+3) > 1`: `Σ_{n∈[T,T+1]} m_n ≤ 2C(M,θ)(log(T+3))^θ ≤ 2C(M,θ) log(T+3)`.

(ii) By (1.3) and the uniform convergence of §5.3, using `u(0) = 0`,

    σ_M² = 2(Ψ_∞(0) − 1) = 2(Ψ_0(0) − 1) + 2O(0) + 2μ_Ow_0(0) + 2Σ_{j<j_0}ψ_j(0) + 2Σ_{j≥j_0}ψ_j(0).      (6.1)

- *Low steps.* By Lemma D(iv),
  `2Σ_{j<j_0}ψ_j(0) ≤ 8M²κ_bρ_0^{−1}Σ_{j≥0}(j+1)^{3/2}Σ_{n∈B_j}n^{−2} ≤ 8M²κ_bρ_0^{−1}[1.3612 + Σ_{j≥1}(j+1)^{3/2}/(4^j−1)]
  ≤ 22.8κ_bρ_0^{−1}M² ≤ 159κ_bqM²` (`Σ_{j≥1}(j+1)^{3/2}/(4^j−1) = 1.4809…`, `ρ_0^{−1} = 8qζ(3/2)/3`).

      δ_σ(j_0) := 16M²A(X/2 − 1)^{−1/2} + 88X_χA(M²+4)U^OX^{−3/2}‖w_0‖_∞ + 2Σ_{j≥j_0}(2^{k_j+2}M²/N_j + 2W_jℓ_j^{max}κ_b/ρ_j),   (6.2)

  `2O(0) = 4Σ_{n≥1}a(n)c(n) ≤ 8M²AΣ_{n>X/2}n^{−3/2} ≤ 16M²A(X/2−1)^{−1/2}`, `|μ_O|` is from Lemma O,

For `M ≤ M_0(θ)`: `2.2M + 6.6M² ≤ 0.286` and `159κ_bqM² ≤ 0.159`; so `σ_M² ≤ 0.455 < ½`.

(iii) For `γ = Ωn`, `4M/(M + in) = 4·½/(½ + iγ)` since `MΩ = ½`; `F_Ω(s) := F(Ωs)` is `2π/Ω`-periodic,
and the normalised Lebesgue measure on `ℝ/(2π/Ω)ℤ` (the dual of `Ωℤ`) is the image of that on
`𝕋`. `σ²` is unchanged because `8M²/(M²+n²) = 8(½)²/((½)²+γ²)`. Since `Ω ≥ 1`, each `[T,T+1]` holds
at most two `γ`, with `n ≤ γ`, so the counting law persists with the same constant. All
hypotheses of Question P″ hold for `G = Ωℤ`, `Γ = Ωℕ`, and `λ{F_Ω = 0} > 0`. At weight `M` itself
(`G = ℤ`, `Γ = ℕ`) they hold as well, since P″ allows every `M > 0`. ∎

*Remark 9.24 (what this does to Remarks 9.9, 9.14, Conjecture U and the bounded case).*
- Remark 9.9's lower bound `≫ (log n)²` concerned corrections *supported* in the gaps. The
  modified correction is not: it only has to vanish on `T ∖ E_j`, and on `𝕋 ∖ T` it is
  `e^Ψ_j(1 − u)`, of size `≍` the rounding error itself. Remark 9.14's count ("the counting law is
  one logarithm short") is therefore reversed, as Remark 9.21 predicted.
- *Conjecture U* is not refuted. Truncating `F` as in the comments after Lemma 9.13 gives finite
  integer models with `λ{|F_N| < ε_N} ≥ λ(S)/2` and `ε_N → 0`. They obey the counting law with the
  constant `A_c`, and `σ² ≤ 0.1556` after shrinking `M_0` (e.g. `M ≤ (6000κ_bq)^{−1/2}`). So U fails
  for every `A_0 ≥ A_c(θ)`. But U asks for *some* admissible `A_0`, and `A_c` is astronomically
  larger than any constant that (Q3) could plausibly allow (see §8, item 9). So U stays open, as a
  question about the size of the counting constant. (Not written out in detail.)
- *Bounded multiplicities* are not touched. Summable `ρ_j` force `Y_j → ∞`, hence `k_j → ∞` and an
  offset `≥ 2^{k_j−1} → ∞`. The fixed-alphabet obstruction of Remark 9.21 (Borwein–Erdélyi–Littmann)
  explains why. By Lemma 9.18 the bounded case remains equivalent to a uniform small-ball bound.

#### 9.22.7 Deviations from the design of Remark 9.21, and why

1. **Offset in one piece, before any rounding; no partition of unity, no comb.** Before any
   band is rounded nothing needs to be exact: the leak `−\widehat{Ou}(n)/a(n)` of
   `O(1 − u)` is a real, tiny (Lemma O) change of every target, and is simply rounded later. The
   only exact requirement is the constant term `\hat F(0) = 1`; the mean defect `μ_O` is removed
   by the bump `μ_Ow_0`, which is supported in `{|θ| < η/4}`, off `T`. The design's
   "`O = Σ_jO_j`, each corrected before the bands it touches are rounded" is the special case
   where all `O_j` are corrected at step `−1`.
2. **The leak of the modified correction is removed with the same comb `g_j`.** Because
   `N'_j = 4W_j`, the copies of the trigonometric polynomial `P_j` (spectrum in `(−W_j,W_j)`) land
   at `|n| ≥ 3W_j`, so `P_jg_j` has low part exactly `P_j` (Lemma C(ii)). A second comb would work
   too, but would remove more measure.
3. **Band of origin.** Implemented as intra-band feedback (4.1), with the spill of band `j`
   carried as a real change of the targets of band `j+1` (Lemma C(ii)). This is equivalent to the
   design's feedback `v_n = Σ_{i≥1}h^{(j(n−i))}_ie_{n−i}` with the spill not counted in the target;
   both give `m_n − (real part) = Σ_j(h^{(k_j)} * (e1_{B_j}))_n`.
4. **Window.** The quantiser is unbounded (`⟨·⟩` on `ℤ`), so the scheme is trivially stable
   (`|e_n| ≤ ½`). Only the lower edge matters: `y_n ≥ ½` gives `m_n ≥ 1`. The window width is
   `2^{k_j}`, i.e. `B_j ≍ 2^{k_j}`.
5. **Low scales.** The offset can only start at a height `X = N_{j_0}` where Lemma O makes its leak
   `< 1/16`, and Lemma B needs `N_j ≥ 4k_j/τ`. Below `j_0` the round-up mechanism of Theorem 9.6 is
   used: its copies are `≥ 0`, so no offset is needed there.
6. **`m_n ≥ 1`** instead of `≥ 0` (offset `≥ 2^{k_J+1} + 1`): it costs nothing, and gives `Γ = ℕ`.
7. **Choice of `k_j` and the exponent.** `k_j` is the least `k` with
   `ZK²ρ_j^{−2}τ^kλ(k) ≤ 2^k`. So the copies of all scales together cost `≤ 2^{k_J}/8` at
   every height in band `J` (Lemma E), and `2^{k_J} ≍ (ρ_J^{−2}λ(k_J))^{1/(1+β)}`. With
   `ρ_j ≍ j^{−3/2}` and `λ(k_j) ≍ log j` this is `≍ j^{3/(1+β)}(log j)^{1/(1+β)}`. The draft uses the
   crude bound `θ' = 4/(1+β)`; with `ρ_j ≍ j^{−1−δ}` one gets any exponent `> (2+2δ)/(1+β)`. The
   design's `β = log_2(1/(2 sin(π/q)))` becomes `β = log_2(1/τ)` with `τ = 2 sin(15π/(16q))`: the
   support of `u` reaches `15π/(8q)`.
8. **`q ≥ 60`** makes `τ < 1/10`, hence `φ(k) = (2/τ)^k/λ(k)` increasing and `k_j` monotone
   (Lemma F(a)). Monotonicity is used in (5.3).

#### 9.22.8 Points where I am not fully sure (and how much it matters)

1. **Quoted results.** Lemma 9.0 and the cell formula in the proof of Theorem 9.1 are quoted from
   `DENSITY.md`. The `(1,q)` computation (Lemma 4.1) is re-derived here, and the vanishing is
   confirmed numerically (`|F_0| ≤ 7·10^{−15}` on `J` for `q = 20, 60`, `M = ½, 0.02`).
   Confidence high.
2. **Standard identities.** The discrete Leibniz rule (1.5), the mean-value property
   `Δ^rF(x) = F^{(r)}(ξ)` (B-spline/Hermite–Genocchi), `|sin(nφ)| ≤ n|sin φ|`, and the Taylor
   remainder. All standard. The Lemma A/B bounds hold numerically with margins `≥ 30×`.
3. **Arithmetic of the absolute constants** (`258`, `82`, `110`, `440`, `0.62`, `2.4`, `C_H`, `ε_j`).
   Each was derived twice by hand. A slip would change a constant by a bounded factor, which is
   absorbed by enlarging `Z` (hence `k_j`, `A`) or `j_0`. The structure of the proof is unaffected.
4. **Numerical constants.** `‖s'‖_∞ = 2`, `C_Ξ ≈ 1.4256` and `κ_b ≈ 2.7005` were computed by
   quadrature and are not certified. The theorem uses only their finiteness. The corollary states
   `M_0` in terms of `κ_b`. It uses only `2πq/(3(q−1)) ≤ 2.2` (`q ≥ 60`) and
   `Σ_{j≥1}(j+1)^{3/2}/(4^j−1) = 1.4809… ≤ 1.482` (checked numerically; elementary to certify).
5. **`D` and supports.** I use: if `ψ ∈ H¹` vanishes on an open `U`, then `Dψ = 0` a.e. on `U`;
   and an `L²` limit of functions vanishing a.e. on `S` vanishes a.e. on `S`. Both are standard.
6. **Lemma O, case `|p| > |l|/2`.** It bounds `|f'(l)|` by `Φ(X/2)^{−5/2}` even when `|l| < X/2`
   (where `f' = 0`). This is harmless.
7. **Uniformity of the a priori bounds.** §5.1 claims that no induction is needed, because every
   bound depends only on `|e_n| ≤ ½`, `δm_n ∈ [0,1)` and the parameters. I re-checked that the
   targets enter only through the rounding, which is always admissible since the quantiser is
   unbounded. Confidence high.
8. **Interpretation of Question P″.** Its statement allows `Γ = ℕ ⊂ G = ℤ`, any `M > 0`, any `A`.
   So the corollary answers it negatively *as stated*. If P″ is meant with a fixed numerical
   constant (`A_0` of (Q3)) or at the fixed weight `M = ½` with spectrum constraints mimicking the
   zeros, the corollary does not decide it (see item 9).
9. **Size of the constants.** For `q = 60`, `M = ½`: `k_0 = 11` (so `2^{k_0} = 2048`) and
   `A ≈ 6·10³`. (J1) holds from `j = 5` and (J4) from `j = 14`. (J3) needs `X ≥ 5.9·10^{16}`, because
   it involves `U^O ≈ 1.1·10^{10}` and `W_0/M² ≈ 3·10^6`; so `j_0 = 28`. Then
   `X_low ≈ 4.5·10^{13}` (`thm922_check.py`, part (7)). The counting constant `A_c` is therefore
   astronomically large. Nothing here
   was optimised: a smoother `u` with optimised `σ`, `ρ_j` decaying more slowly at small `j`, and
   Chebyshev-type filters would lower everything, but not to the size of the true `A_0`.
10. **Not used, not checked:** the remark's claim that optimal filters give `β` up to `(q−2)/2`.
11. **Possible objection:** "the offset is a huge real perturbation of the base, so the model is

#### 9.22.10 Verdict

**Proved, for every `θ > 0`** (and every `M > 0`), with `m_n ≥ 1` integers (drafting subagent's
verdict, confirmed by the seventh adversarial read: no fatal error). Explicitly,
`q = 60` gives `θ = 0.92`. Corollary 9.23: for `θ ≤ 1` and `M ≤ M_0(θ)` the example obeys the
local counting law `Σ_{γ∈[T,T+1]}m_γ ≤ A_c log(T+3)` and has `σ² < ½` (also at the classical weight
after rescaling). So **Question P″, in which the counting constant `A` is free, has a negative answer for `G = ℤ`**
(and for `G = Ωℤ` at `M = ½`). The counting constant is `≈ 10^{14}` or more, so Conjecture U at
an admissible `A_0` is not affected (Corollary 9.23(iv)).

The main new lemmas are:
- **Lemma B**: the shaped error is coefficientwise small on the arc,
  `sup|(e^Ψu)^| ≤ (16/9)a(N)τ^kλ(k)`, by `k`-th differences, discrete Leibniz, and the
  commutator with `a(n)`;
- **Lemma C**: the modified correction, with exact leak removal by the same comb and the copy
  cost `258K²τ^kλ(k)ρ^{−2}Ξ(k^*ρ) + ε_j`;
- **Lemma O**: the offset leak, via vanishing moments of `\hat u`;
- **Lemma F**: the choice of the orders `k_j`;
- **Lemma E**: geometric summation over scales.

The only steps resting on unrefereed arithmetic are the absolute constants (§8, item 3), which
do not affect the structure.
