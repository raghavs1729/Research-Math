## 0. Summary

Setting: `χ` real primitive mod `q`, `χ* = −χ`, `D_m(x) = Σ_{p≤x} χ*(p) p^m`,
`R_m(x) = Σ_{p≤x} χ*(p) p^m log p`, `M = m + 1/2 > 0`,

    E_M(x) = 2M x^{-M} (log x) D_m(x),        E^θ_M(x) = 2M x^{-M} R_m(x)

(these are `𝓔_{m,0}`, `𝓔_{m,1}` of `monotone-coupling.tex`, and `E_m'`, `E_m` of
`paper/weighted-races.tex`), `ν_M` their common limiting logarithmic distribution.
Hypotheses: GRH(χ) and NCZ(χ): `L(1/2,χ) ≠ 0` (unconditional for `q = 3, 4`; for all real
primitive `χ` of conductor `≤ 10^9`, resp. `≤ 10^10` if `χ` is odd, by Platt–Trudgian
arXiv:2610.29018, Theorem 1; see Remark 1.5 for what changes without NCZ).

## 1. Setting and the limiting distribution

### 1.1 Notation and quoted inputs

`ρ = 1/2 + iγ` runs over the nontrivial zeros of `L(s,χ)` (GRH). `Γ` denotes the set of
**distinct** positive ordinates, `m_γ ≥ 1` the multiplicity of `γ ∈ Γ`. Sums written
`Σ_{γ>0}` run over positive ordinates *with multiplicity*; sums written `Σ_{γ∈Γ}` over
distinct ordinates. Put

    r_γ(M) = 4M/(M + iγ),   |r_γ(M)| = c_γ(M) = 4M/sqrt(M^2+γ^2),
    w_M(γ) = 8M^2/(M^2+γ^2) = |r_γ(M)|^2/2,
    σ_M^2 = Σ_{γ>0} w_M(γ) = Σ_{γ∈Γ} m_γ w_M(γ),      V_M = Σ_{γ∈Γ} m_γ^2 w_M(γ),
    λ_χ(M,M') = 1/(MM') − Σ_{γ>0} 4/(|M+iγ||M'+iγ|)      (the Λ(M,M') of CORE-NOTES).

`y = log x` throughout; `f_M(y) := E_M(e^y)`, `g_M(y) := E^θ_M(e^y)`; both vanish for
`y < log 2` (no primes below 2).

Quoted inputs.

- **(Q1) Explicit formula** (`weighted-races.tex`, proofs of Thm 1(i) and Prop 1; stated as
  Lemma `lem:WRexp` in `monotone-coupling.tex`). Under GRH + NCZ, for `h ∈ {f_M, g_M}`,
  all `u ≥ 2`, `T ≥ 2`:
  `h(u) = 1 + Re Σ_{0<γ≤T} r_γ(M) e^{iγu} + 𝓔(u,T)`,
  `|𝓔(u,T)| ≪ u e^{u/2} log^2(e^u T)/T + d(u)`, where `d(u) ≪ 1/u` is a fixed bounded
  function with `d(u) → 0`.
- **(Q2) Hadamard product / variance identity** (`weighted-races.tex`, Prop. `prop:varid`).
  With `Λ(s,χ) = (q/π)^{(s+a)/2} Γ((s+a)/2) L(s,χ)`, `Ξ(z) = Λ(1/2+z)`: under GRH + NCZ,
  `Ξ(z)/Ξ(0) = Π_{γ>0} (1 + z^2/γ^2)`, hence for `Re z > 0`
  `(Ξ'/Ξ)(z) = Σ_{γ>0} 2z/(z^2+γ^2) = Σ_{γ>0} [1/(z−iγ) + 1/(z+iγ)]`,
  and `σ_M^2 = 4M (Λ'/Λ)(M + 1/2)`, `C(χ) := Σ_{γ>0} 2/γ^2 = (log Λ)''(1/2)`.
- **(Q3) Zero counting** (Davenport §16): `N(t+1) − N(t) ≤ A_0 log(t+3)` for `t ≥ 0`,
  `N(t) ~ (t/2π) log t`.
- **(Q4) Prime powers** (`weighted-races.tex`, Lemmas `lem:trunc`, `lem:squares`):
  `ψ_m(t) := Σ_{n≤t} Λ(n)χ(n)n^m = −Σ_{|γ|≤T} t^{ρ+m}/(ρ+m) + O(t^{m+1} log^2(tT)/T + (1+t^m) log t)`
  uniformly in `t, T ≥ 2`, and `R_m(t) = −ψ_m(t) + t^M/(2M) + o(t^M)`.

Subtracting the two instances of (Q1) with the same `T` and letting `T → ∞`:

    f_M(y) − g_M(y) = O(1/y)   pointwise, y ≥ 2                                   (1.1)

(this is Theorem C(i) of `monotone-coupling.tex` for `b = 0`).

### 1.2 A mean-value lemma for all even moments

**Lemma 1.1.** Let `0 < λ_1 ≤ λ_2 ≤ …` with `#{j : t ≤ λ_j < t+1} ≤ A_0 log(t+3)` for all
`t ≥ 0`, and let `|c_j| ≤ A_1/λ_j`. For `3 ≤ T < X`, put `S(y) = Σ_{T<λ_j≤X} c_j e^{iλ_j y}`.
Then for every integer `k ≥ 1` and every `Y ≥ 1`,

    (1/Y) ∫_0^Y |S(y)|^{2k} dy  ≤  C_k (A_0 A_1)^{2k} (log T)^{4k−1} / T ,

with `C_k` depending on `k` only (uniformly in `X` and `Y`).

*Proof.* `S^k = Σ c_{j_1}⋯c_{j_k} e^{iωy}`, `ω = λ_{j_1}+…+λ_{j_k}`. For integers `n ≥ 0` let
`b_k(n)` be the sum of `|c_{j_1}⋯c_{j_k}|` over the `k`-tuples with `n ≤ ω < n+1`. Since
`|(1/Y)∫_0^Y e^{i(ω−ω')y}dy| ≤ min(1, 2/(Y|ω−ω'|)) ≤ min(1, 2/|ω−ω'|)` for `Y ≥ 1`,

    (1/Y)∫_0^Y |S^k|^2 ≤ Σ_{n,n'} b_k(n) b_k(n') h(n−n'),   h(d) = 1 (|d| ≤ 3),  h(d) = 2/(|d|−1) (|d| ≥ 4).

*Claim:* `b_k(n) ≤ B_k log^{2k−1}(n+3)/(n+1)` with `B_k = C'_k (A_0A_1)^k`, and `b_k(n) = 0`
for `n < kT − 1`. For `k = 1`: `b_1(n) ≤ A_0 log(n+3)·A_1/max(n,T) ≤ 2A_0A_1 log(n+3)/(n+1)`.
Inductively, a `k`-tuple with `ω ∈ [n,n+1)` has `λ_{j_1} ∈ [n_1,n_1+1)` and the remaining sum
in `[n_2,n_2+1)` with `n_1 + n_2 ∈ {n−1, n}`, so
`b_k(n) ≤ Σ_{n_1} b_1(n_1)[b_{k−1}(n−n_1) + b_{k−1}(n−1−n_1)]
≤ 4B_1B_{k−1} log^{2k−2}(n+3) Σ_{n_1=0}^{n} 1/((n_1+1)(n−n_1+1))`, and the last sum is
`2H_{n+1}/(n+2) ≤ 2(1+log(n+1))/(n+2)`. The vanishing for `n+1 ≤ kT` is clear since every
`λ_j > T`.
Now fix `n`; splitting `n'` into `|n'−n| ≤ n/2`, `n' > 3n/2`, `n' < n/2` and using the claim,
`Σ_{n'} b_k(n')h(n−n') ≪ B_k log^{2k}(n+3)/(n+1)`. Hence the double sum is
`≪ B_k^2 Σ_{n ≥ kT−1} log^{4k−1}(n+3)/(n+1)^2 ≪ B_k^2 log^{4k−1}(T)/T`. ∎

### 1.3 The compact group and the identification of `ν_M`

Let `ι : ℝ → 𝕋^Γ`, `ι(y) = (e^{iγy})_{γ∈Γ}`, and `A := closure of ι(ℝ)`, a compact connected
metrizable abelian group with normalised Haar measure `λ_A`. Let `e_γ : A → 𝕋` be the
coordinate characters and `τ_s(θ) = θ·ι(s)` the translation flow.

**Lemma 1.2 (Kronecker–Weyl, no LI).**
(a) For continuous `h : A → ℂ`, `(1/Y)∫_0^Y h(ι(y))dy → ∫_A h dλ_A`.
(b) The characters `1, e_γ, \bar e_γ` (`γ ∈ Γ`) are pairwise distinct on `A`, hence
orthonormal in `L^2(A)`.
(c) The flow `(τ_s)` is ergodic on `(A, λ_A)`.
(d) Every nonempty open subset of `A` has positive Haar measure, and the push-forward of
`λ_A` under a nontrivial character is Haar measure on `𝕋`.

*Proof.* The characters of `A` are the restrictions `ψ_n(θ) = Π θ_γ^{n_γ}` (`n` finitely
supported), and `ψ_n(ι(y)) = e^{iω_n y}` with `ω_n = Σ n_γ γ`. If `ω_n = 0` then `ψ_n ≡ 1`
on `ι(ℝ)`, hence on `A`; if `ω_n ≠ 0`, `ψ_n` is a nontrivial character, `∫_A ψ_n = 0`, and
the time average of `e^{iω_n y}` tends to `0`. So (a) holds for characters, hence for their
span, which is uniformly dense in `C(A)` (Stone–Weierstrass). (b): `e_γ, e_{γ'}^{±1}, 1` have
frequencies `γ, ±γ', 0`, pairwise different. (c): if `h ∈ L^2(A)` is flow-invariant then
`\hat h(ψ_n)(1 − e^{iω_n s}) = 0` for all `s`, so `\hat h(ψ_n) = 0` unless `ω_n = 0`, i.e.
unless `ψ_n` is trivial; so `h` is constant. (d): translation invariance and compactness;
the image of a connected group under a nontrivial character is `𝕋`, and the image measure is
invariant. ∎

For `T ≥ 2` let

    F_{M,T}(θ) := 1 + Re Σ_{γ∈Γ, γ≤T} m_γ r_γ(M) θ_γ ,     P_{M,T}(y) := F_{M,T}(ι(y)) = 1 + Re Σ_{0<γ≤T} r_γ(M) e^{iγy}.

Since `Σ_{γ∈Γ} m_γ^2 |r_γ|^2 < ∞` (by (Q3), or by Lemma 4.2), Lemma 1.2(b) gives
`F_{M,T} → F_M` in `L^2(A)`, where

    F_M := 1 + Re Σ_{γ∈Γ} m_γ r_γ(M) e_γ  ∈ L^2(A),   ∫F_M = 1,   ‖F_M − 1‖_2^2 = ½ Σ_{γ∈Γ} m_γ^2 |r_γ(M)|^2 = V_M.   (1.2)

**Theorem 1.3 (GRH + NCZ).** Let `h ∈ {f_M, g_M}` and let `ν_{M,Y}` be the law of `h(y)` for
`y` uniform on `[0,Y]`.

(a) For every `p < ∞`: `lim_{T→∞} limsup_{Y→∞} (1/Y)∫_0^Y |h(y) − P_{M,T}(y)|^p dy = 0`
(`h` is Besicovitch `B^p`-almost periodic for every `p`).

(b) `ν_{M,Y}` converges, as `Y → ∞`, in every Wasserstein distance `W_p` (`p < ∞`) to
`ν_M := law of F_M under λ_A`. Equivalently: for every continuous `φ` with
`|φ(x)| ≤ C(1+|x|)^p` for some `p`,

    lim_{Y→∞} (1/Y) ∫_0^Y φ(E_M(e^y)) dy = ∫ φ dν_M = ∫_A φ(F_M) dλ_A      (finite),

and likewise for `E^θ_M`. In particular `F_M ∈ L^p(A)` for all `p < ∞`, `ν_M` has mean `1`
and variance `V_M`.

*Proof.* (a) It suffices to take `p = 2k`. On `2 ≤ y ≤ Y` use (Q1) with truncation `e^Y`:
`h − P_{M,T} = Re S_{T,e^Y}(y) + 𝓔(y, e^Y)` where `S_{T,X} = Σ_{T<γ≤X} r_γ e^{iγy}`. By
Lemma 1.1 (with `c_j = r_γ`, `A_1 = 4M`, and (Q3)) the first term has `2k`-th Cesàro mean
`≪ log^{4k−1}T/T` uniformly in `Y`. For the second, `u e^{u/2}log^2(e^u e^Y)e^{−Y} ≤ 4Y^3 e^{−Y/2} → 0`
uniformly in `u ≤ Y`, and `d(u) → 0` is bounded, so its `2k`-th Cesàro mean tends to `0`. On
`[0,2]` both functions are bounded, contributing `O(1/Y)`.

(b) Let `ν^T_Y`, `ν^T` be the laws of `P_{M,T}(y)` (`y ∈ [0,Y]`) and of `F_{M,T}` under
`λ_A`. By Lemma 1.2(a), `ν^T_Y → ν^T` weakly, and all these measures live on one compact
interval, so `W_p(ν^T_Y, ν^T) → 0`. Coupling through the same `y`,
`W_p(ν_{M,Y}, ν^T_Y)^p ≤ (1/Y)∫_0^Y |h − P_{M,T}|^p`. Hence
`limsup_Y W_p(ν_{M,Y}, ν^T) ≤ η_T` with `η_T → 0` by (a); so `(ν^T)_T` is `W_p`-Cauchy, with
a limit `ν^∞` (completeness of `W_p`), and `ν_{M,Y} → ν^∞` in `W_p`. As `F_{M,T} → F_M` in
`L^2`, `ν^T → law(F_M)` in `W_2`, so `ν^∞ = law(F_M)`. Convergence in `W_p` is equivalent to
weak convergence plus convergence of `p`-th absolute moments, i.e. to convergence of
`∫φ dν_{M,Y}` for all continuous `φ` of growth `≤ C(1+|x|^p)` (Villani, *Optimal transport*,
Thm 6.9). Finally `‖F_M‖_{L^p} = lim_T ‖F_{M,T}‖_{L^p} < ∞`. ∎

**Remark 1.4 (what is known here).** Existence of `ν_M` is `weighted-races.tex` Thm 1(i)
via Akbary–Ng–Shahabi; the representation as a push-forward of Haar measure on the closure
subtorus, without LI, is in Rubinstein–Sarnak §2, Devin (arXiv:1706.06394, §4) and Bailleul
(arXiv:2007.05763, §4); exponential-type tails `ν(ℝ∖[−A,A]) ≪ exp(−c√A)` under GRH alone are
RS94 Thm 1.2 / Devin Thm 2.1 for the unweighted race (RS94 Thm 1.2 bounds the mass outside
a ball; the two one-sided lower bounds are Devin Thm 2.5(1)). A clean alternative route to
`F_M ∈ L^p`: by the Hausdorff–Young inequality on `A`,
`‖F_M − 1‖_{L^p(A)} ≤ (2 Σ_{γ∈Γ} (m_γ|r_γ|/2)^{p'})^{1/p'}`, `1/p + 1/p' = 1`, `p ≥ 2`,
finite for every `p < ∞`. We claim no novelty for Theorem 1.3 beyond writing out the `B^p`
statement for the weighted races.

**Remark 1.5 (without NCZ).** If `n_0 = ord_{s=1/2}L(s,χ) > 0` the central zero contributes
the constant `2n_0` to (Q1), so everything below holds with mean `1 + 2n_0` in place of `1`
(Cantelli becomes `V_M/(V_M + (1+2n_0)^2)`), and `σ_M^2 = 4M(Λ'/Λ)(M+1/2) − 4n_0`. We have
not rewritten the proofs of (Q1), (Q4) in that generality; `monotone-coupling.tex` proves
Theorem A for general `n_0`. This remark is not used.

**Remark 1.6 (constants, and what Lemma 1.1 cannot give).** (i) The constant `C_k` of
Lemma 1.1 is of factorial size in `k` (about `(4k)!`); this is irrelevant for the theorems.
(ii) The class of functions covered by Lemma 1.1 does **not** have exponential moments in
general: for frequencies `n ∈ (T,X]` with multiplicity `⌈log n⌉`, coefficients `1/n` and
aligned phases, `P(S > a) ≍ exp(−\sqrt{2a})` (Referee 4's stress test). This matches the
`exp(−c√A)` tails of RS94/Devin and is why no statement about `∫e^{tx}dν_M` beyond
Theorem 3.1 is made (Corollary 3.5). (iii) Dependency: Theorem 3.1 for every convex `φ`
needs only `F_M ∈ L^1(A)`, the multiplier identity and the identification of `ν_M` as a
weak limit (the case `k = 1` of Lemma 1.1); Lemma 1.1 with `k ≥ 2` is used only for the
convergence of *time averages* of `φ(E_M(e^y))` when `φ` grows faster than quadratically.

## 2. The resolvent (averaging) identity

For `0 < M < M'` let `κ = M' − M` and

    μ = μ_{M,M'} := (M/M') δ_0 + (1 − M/M') · M e^{−Mu} du   on [0,∞),

a **probability** measure, with `\hat μ(ξ) := ∫ e^{−iξu}μ(du) = (M/M')·(M'+iξ)/(M+iξ)`.
Write `K_M h(y) := ∫_0^∞ M e^{−Mu} h(y−u) du` (the resolvent `M(M + d/dy)^{-1}`).

**Theorem 2.1 (GRH + NCZ only for (b), (c); (a) is unconditional).** Let `0 < M < M'`.

(a) *Exact identity for the θ-form.* For every real `y`,

    E^θ_M(e^y) = ∫_{[0,∞)} E^θ_{M'}(e^{y−u}) μ_{M,M'}(du)
               = (M/M') E^θ_{M'}(e^y) + (1 − M/M') ∫_0^∞ M e^{−Mu} E^θ_{M'}(e^{y−u}) du .

(b) *Unlogged race, pointwise.* For `y ≥ 2`,

    E_M(e^y) = ∫_{[0,∞)} E_{M'}(e^{y−u}) μ_{M,M'}(du) + O_{χ,M,M'}(1/y).

(c) *The smoothed piece is an absolutely convergent zero sum.* As `y → ∞`,

    K_M g_{M'}(y) = 2MM' e^{−My} ∫_2^{e^y} t^{−κ−1} R_{m'}(t) dt
                  = 1 + Re Σ_{γ>0} 4MM' e^{iγy} / ((M+iγ)(M'+iγ)) + o(1).

(d) *Multiplier and semigroup.* `r_γ(M) = \hat μ_{M,M'}(γ) r_γ(M')`, `|\hat μ_{M,M'}| ≤ 1`, and
`μ_{M,M'} * μ_{M',M''} = μ_{M,M''}` for `M < M' < M''`.

*Proof.* (a) Riemann–Stieltjes partial summation (`R_{m'}(t) = 0` for `t < 2`):
`R_m(x) = ∫_{2^-}^x t^{−κ} dR_{m'}(t) = x^{−κ}R_{m'}(x) + κ∫_2^x t^{−κ−1}R_{m'}(t)dt`.
Multiply by `2Mx^{−M}`; the first term is `(M/M')E^θ_{M'}(x)`. In the second substitute
`t = e^{y−u}`: `2Mκ x^{−M} ∫_2^x t^{M−1}·t^{−M'}R_{m'}(t) dt = (κ/M') ∫_0^{y−log 2} M e^{−Mu} g_{M'}(y−u) du`,
and `κ/M' = 1 − M/M'`; the integral may be extended to `u ∈ [0,∞)` because `g_{M'} = 0` below
`log 2`. For `y < log 2` both sides vanish.

(b) By (1.1), `f_{M'}(v) − g_{M'}(v) = O(1/v)` for `v ≥ 2`; both functions are bounded on
`[log 2, 2]` and vanish below `log 2`. Hence
`|∫(f_{M'} − g_{M'})(y−u) μ(du)| ≪ ∫_0^{y/2} Me^{−Mu}(2/y)du + e^{−My/2} + 1/y ≪ 1/y` for
`y ≥ 4` (so that `y − u ≥ 2` on `u ≤ y/2`); for `2 ≤ y < 4` the bound is trivial. Combine
with (a) and (1.1) at weight `M`.

(c) The first equality is the substitution of (a). By (Q4), multiply the formula for
`ψ_{m'}(t)` by `t^{−κ−1}`, integrate over `[2,x]` and let `T → ∞` with `x` fixed: the
truncation error tends to `0`, and since `ρ + m' − κ = ρ + m`,

    ∫_2^x t^{−κ−1} ψ_{m'}(t) dt = − Σ_ρ (x^{ρ+m} − 2^{ρ+m}) / ((ρ+m)(ρ+m')) + O((1 + x^m) log^2 x),

the sum over all zeros converging absolutely (`Σ|γ|^{-2} < ∞`). After multiplication by
`x^{−M}` the `2^{ρ+m}` terms and the error are `o(1)`. By (Q4) again,
`R_{m'}(t) = −ψ_{m'}(t) + t^{M'}/(2M') + o(t^{M'})`, and
`x^{−M}∫_2^x t^{−κ−1}(t^{M'}/(2M') + o(t^{M'}))dt = 1/(2MM') + o(1)` because `M > 0`.
Pairing `ρ` with `\bar ρ` gives the claim (`ρ + m = M + iγ`).

(d) `∫_0^∞ Me^{−Mu}e^{−iξu}du = M/(M+iξ)`, so
`\hat μ(ξ) = M/M' + (1−M/M')M/(M+iξ) = (M/M')(M'+iξ)/(M+iξ)`, and
`\hat μ(γ)·4M'/(M'+iγ) = 4M/(M+iγ)`. The multipliers multiply, and a finite measure on
`[0,∞)` is determined by its Fourier transform. ∎

**Remark 2.2 (reading).** `E_M` is the output of the one-pole low-pass filter
`K_M` applied to a weight-independent driving signal; passing from `M'` to `M < M'` is
averaging against a probability kernel supported on the past. All LI-free monotonicity
below is a consequence of this positivity. Theorem 2.1(d) is the precise sense in which
the family `(ν_M)` is "a semigroup of contractions".

**Remark 2.3 (exact unlogged form).** For the unlogged race the same partial summation
gives, exactly, for `y > log 2`,
`E_M(e^y) = (M/M')E_{M'}(e^y) + (1−M/M')∫_0^{y−log 2} M e^{−Mu} · (y/(y−u)) · E_{M'}(e^{y−u}) du`;
the factor `y/(y−u)` is the source of the `O(1/y)` in (b). (Checked numerically to `10^{-13}`,
`sieve_checks.py`.)

**Corollary 2.4 (Theorem A of CORE-NOTES / `monotone-coupling.tex`, θ-form and unlogged form).**
For `0 < M < M'`, as `x → ∞`,

    E^θ_M(x)/M − E^θ_{M'}(x)/M' = (κ/(MM')) K_M g_{M'}(log x) ≥ κ (λ_χ(M,M') − o(1)),

and the same holds for `E_M, E_{M'}` (by (1.1)). So if `λ_χ(M,M') > 0` there is `x_0` with:
`D_{m'}(x) ≥ 0 ⇒ D_m(x) > 0` and `R_{m'}(x) ≥ 0 ⇒ R_m(x) > 0` for all `x ≥ x_0`.

*Proof.* Theorem 2.1(a),(c) and `|Re Σ 4MM'e^{iγy}/((M+iγ)(M'+iγ))| ≤ 1 − MM'λ_χ(M,M')`. ∎

(The other note proves this directly for `D_m` with an `O(x^M/log^2 x)` error and without
NCZ; an all-`x` explicit version for `(m,m') = (0,1)` is in `monotonicity/explicit/EXPLICIT.md`.
Nothing is added to Theorem A here except the short θ-form proof.)

## 3. Convex order

**Theorem 3.1 (convex order; GRH + NCZ, no LI).** Let `0 < M < M'`. Then
`F_M(θ) = ∫ F_{M'}(τ_{−u}θ) μ_{M,M'}(du)` for `λ_A`-a.e. `θ`, and for **every** convex
`φ : ℝ → ℝ`,

    ∫ φ dν_M ≤ ∫ φ dν_{M'}        (both sides in (−∞, +∞]).

For convex `φ` of polynomial growth both sides are finite and are the limits of the time
averages `(1/Y)∫_0^Y φ(E_M(e^y))dy`, resp. with `M'` (Theorem 1.3(b)).

*Proof.* Fix a Borel representative of `F_{M'}`. `(θ,u) ↦ F_{M'}(τ_{−u}θ)` is jointly
measurable and `∫∫|F_{M'}(τ_{−u}θ)|λ_A(dθ)μ(du) = ‖F_{M'}‖_1 < ∞` by invariance of `λ_A`. So
`Φ(θ) := ∫F_{M'}(τ_{−u}θ)μ(du)` is defined a.e., lies in `L^2(A)` (Minkowski), and for
every character `ψ` of `A` with frequency `ω`,
`∫Φ\bar ψ dλ_A = ∫μ(du) e^{−iωu} ∫F_{M'}\bar ψ dλ_A = \hat μ(ω)\hat F_{M'}(ψ)`. By (1.2) and
Theorem 2.1(d) these are the Fourier coefficients of `F_M` (both vanish off
`{1, e_γ^{±1}}`). Characters are complete in `L^2(A)`, so `Φ = F_M` a.e.

Let `φ` be convex and `x ↦ ax+b` a supporting line. For a.e. `θ`, Jensen's inequality for
the probability measure `μ` gives `φ(F_M(θ)) ≤ ∫φ(F_{M'}(τ_{−u}θ))μ(du)`. Integrate in `θ`,
apply Tonelli to the nonnegative function `φ(F_{M'}(τ_{−u}θ)) − aF_{M'}(τ_{−u}θ) − b`, and
use the invariance of `λ_A`: the right side becomes `∫_A φ(F_{M'})dλ_A`. ∎

The time-domain version of the same proof (Jensen in Theorem 2.1(a), then Theorem 1.3(b))
gives the inequality for convex `φ` of polynomial growth without the group; the group
version removes the growth condition.

**Proposition 3.2 (unbounded support; GRH + NCZ).** For every `M > 0`,
`ess inf F_M = −∞` and `ess sup F_M = +∞`; i.e. `ν_M((−∞,−a)) > 0` and `ν_M((a,∞)) > 0`
for every `a`.

*Proof.* Let `k_T(u) = (T/2π)(sin(Tu/2)/(Tu/2))^2` be the Fejér kernel on `ℝ`: `k_T ≥ 0`,
`∫k_T = 1`, `∫k_T(u)e^{−iξu}du = (1−|ξ|/T)^+`. As in the previous proof,
`∫F_M(τ_{−u}θ)k_T(u)du = F^{(T)}(θ)` a.e., where

    F^{(T)}(θ) := 1 + Re Σ_{γ∈Γ, γ<T} (1 − γ/T) m_γ r_γ(M) θ_γ

is a *continuous* function on `A`. Suppose `F_M ≥ −a` a.e. Then `F^{(T)} ≥ −a` a.e., hence
everywhere on `A` (Lemma 1.2(d)), in particular at `θ = ι(−1/T)`:

    −a ≤ F^{(T)}(ι(−1/T)) = 1 + Σ_{0<γ<T} (1−γ/T) · 4M [M cos(γ/T) − γ sin(γ/T)] / (M^2+γ^2)
       ≤ 1 + σ_M^2/2 − 4M Σ_{T/4≤γ≤T/2} (1/2)·sin(1/4)·γ/(M^2+γ^2).

For `γ ≥ M` one has `γ/(M^2+γ^2) ≥ 1/(2γ) ≥ 1/T`, so the last sum is
`≥ (sin(1/4)/2T)(N(T/2) − N(T/4)) ≫ log T` by (Q3). Letting `T → ∞` gives a contradiction.
Evaluating at `ι(+1/T)` instead gives `ess sup F_M = +∞`. ∎

**Lemma 3.3.** For `λ_A`-a.e. `θ`, the function `u ↦ F_M(τ_{−u}θ)`, `u ≥ 0`, is essentially
unbounded above and below (Lebesgue measure on `[0,∞)`).

*Proof.* For `a ∈ ℕ` let `N_a = {θ : F_M(τ_{−u}θ) ≥ −a for a.e. u ≥ 0}`. Then
`τ_{−s}N_a ⊂ N_a` for `s ≥ 0`, and `τ_{−s}` preserves `λ_A`, so `N_a` is invariant mod null
sets; by ergodicity (Lemma 1.2(c)) `λ_A(N_a) ∈ {0,1}`. If it were `1`, Fubini and invariance
would give `0 = ∫∫1{F_M(τ_{−u}θ) < −a}e^{−u}du λ_A(dθ) = λ_A(F_M < −a)`, contradicting
Proposition 3.2. The upper side is the same; take the countable union. ∎

**Theorem 3.4 (strictness; GRH + NCZ).** Let `0 < M < M'` and let `φ` be convex, not affine,
with `∫φ dν_{M'} < ∞`. Then `∫φ dν_M < ∫φ dν_{M'}`.

*Proof.* Suppose equality. Then Jensen's inequality is an equality for a.e. `θ`. Fix such a
`θ`, put `g(u) = F_{M'}(τ_{−u}θ)`, `x_0 = ∫g dμ`, and let `ℓ` be a supporting line of `φ` at
`x_0`. Then `∫(φ−ℓ)(g)dμ = 0` with `φ − ℓ ≥ 0` continuous, so `φ = ℓ` on the essential range
of `g` with respect to `μ`; as `μ` has a positive density on `(0,∞)`, this is the
Lebesgue-essential range, whose convex hull is `ℝ` for a.e. `θ` (Lemma 3.3 for `M'`). The
zero set of the convex function `φ − ℓ ≥ 0` is an interval, so `φ = ℓ` on `ℝ`. ∎

**Corollary 3.5 (monotone functionals; GRH + NCZ, no LI).** Each of the following is a
strictly increasing function of `M ∈ (0,∞)`, and is the limit of the corresponding
logarithmic time average of the race `E_M` (or `E^θ_M`) (Theorem 1.3(b)):

- the variance `V_M = lim (1/Y)∫_0^Y (E_M(e^y) − 1)^2 dy`;
- every absolute central moment `∫|x−1|^p dν_M`, `p ≥ 1`, and every `∫|x−c|^p dν_M`;
- the **mean deficit** `∫x^- dν_M = lim (1/Y)∫_0^Y max(−E_M(e^y), 0) dy`, and more generally
  `∫(c − x)^+ dν_M` and `∫(x − c)^+ dν_M` for every real `c`.

For real `t ≠ 0`, `M ↦ ∫e^{tx}dν_M ∈ (0,∞]` is nondecreasing, and
`∫e^{tx}dν_M < ∫e^{tx}dν_{M'}` for `M < M'` whenever the right side is finite. This is a
statement about `ν_M` only: no time-average statement is made, and under GRH alone we do
not know that this integral is finite for any `t ≠ 0` (Remark 1.6(ii)).

Moreover, for convex `Ψ ≥ 1_{(−∞,0]}` and `M < M'`:

    ν_M((−∞,0]) ≤ ∫Ψ dν_M ≤ ∫Ψ dν_{M'} ;

so a bound for `ν_M((−∞,0])` obtained by transporting a convex test function from a heavier
weight is never better than the same test function at `M`.

*Proof.* Theorems 3.1, 3.4, 1.3(b): the functions `|x−c|^p`, `(c−x)^+`, `(x−c)^+` are
convex, non-affine and of polynomial growth; `e^{tx}` is convex and non-affine, and Theorems
3.1, 3.4 apply to it directly. The last display is `1_{(−∞,0]} ≤ Ψ` and Theorem 3.1. ∎

**Theorem 3.6 (both lead sets have positive lower density; GRH + NCZ, no LI).** For every
`m > −1/2`,

    liminf_{X→∞} (1/log X) ∫_2^X 1{D_m(t) < 0} dt/t ≥ ν_M((−∞,0)) > 0,
    liminf_{X→∞} (1/log X) ∫_2^X 1{D_m(t) > 0} dt/t ≥ ν_M((0,∞)) ≥ 1/(1+V_M) > 0,

and the same for `R_m`. Consequently both upper densities are `< 1`. Quantitatively, for
every `γ ∈ Γ` with `m_γ c_γ(M) > 2` (e.g. `M > γ_1/√3`),

    ∫x^- dν_M ≥ (m_γ c_γ(M) − 2)/4,      ν_M((−∞,0)) ≥ (m_γ c_γ(M) − 2)^2 / (16 V_M).

*Proof.* `{D_m < 0} = {E_M < 0}` for `x > 1`, `(−∞,0)` is open, so the portmanteau theorem
and Proposition 3.2 give the first line; the second is Theorem 5.1 below. For the
quantitative part: `m_γ c_γ/2 = |\hat F_M(e_γ)| ≤ ‖F_M‖_1 = 1 + 2∫F_M^-`, and
`∫F_M^- ≤ (λ_A(F_M<0))^{1/2}(∫(F_M^-)^2)^{1/2} ≤ (λ_A(F_M<0) V_M)^{1/2}` because
`|F_M| ≤ |F_M − 1|` where `F_M < 0`. ∎

At `m = 0` the positivity is Devin Thm 2.5(1) (`0 < δ_lower ≤ δ_upper < 1`, with one-sided
tails `≫ exp(−exp(ca))`; this generalises Rubinstein–Sarnak Thm 1.2, which bounds the mass
outside a ball) and Kaczorowski (natural lower density), see §8; for `m ≠ 0` we did not
find it stated. The proof above is qualitative.

**Remark 3.7.** Corollary 3.5 answers "can one improve a bound at weight `m` by passing
through another weight?" negatively for all convex test functions (Cantelli is one): the
bound computed at `M` itself is the best in the family.

## 4. The variance without simple zeros, and explicit control of multiplicities

**Theorem 4.1 (GRH + NCZ).** For every `M > 0`,

    Var(ν_M) = lim_{Y→∞} (1/Y) ∫_0^Y (E_M(e^y) − 1)^2 dy = V_M = Σ_{γ∈Γ} m_γ^2 · 8M^2/(M^2+γ^2),

    V_M = σ_M^2 + Σ_{γ∈Γ} (m_γ^2 − m_γ) w_M(γ) ≥ σ_M^2 = 4M (Λ'/Λ)(M + 1/2),

with equality if and only if every zero of `L(s,χ)` is simple. `M ↦ V_M` is strictly
increasing, `V_M ≤ C_2(χ)(2M)^2` with `C_2(χ) := Σ_{γ∈Γ} 2m_γ^2/γ^2 < ∞`.

*Proof.* (1.2) and Theorem 1.3(b); (Q2) for `σ_M^2`; Lemma 4.2 for finiteness. ∎

The closed form `σ_M^2` counts the zeros with weight `m_γ`; the variance of the race counts
them with weight `m_γ^2`. In general form this is Fiorilli (ANT 2014, Lemma 2.5) and Devin
(2020, Thm 2.1). The companion paper states the mean square with `m_γ` (see §9 for the
erratum). Under GRH alone simplicity is not known, so *numerical* statements need an
explicit bound for the excess.

**Lemma 4.2 (explicit multiplicity bound; GRH + NCZ).** Let `χ(−1) = (−1)^a`, `δ > 1/2`,
`x_0 = (1/2 + δ + a)/2`, `Z(σ) = −ζ'(σ)/ζ(σ)`. For every real `t`,

    Σ_{ρ} δ^2/(δ^2 + (t−γ_ρ)^2) ≤ μ_δ(t) := (δ/4) log(t^2 + 4x_0^2) + δ b_δ ,
    b_δ := ½ log(q/2π) + 1/(24 x_0^2) + Z(1/2 + δ),

the sum over all zeros with multiplicity. In particular `m_γ ≤ μ_δ(γ)` for every ordinate.

*Proof.* By (Q2) with `z = δ + it`: `Re(Λ'/Λ)(1/2+δ+it) = Σ_ρ δ/(δ^2+(t−γ_ρ)^2)`. On the
other hand `Λ'/Λ(s) = ½log(q/π) + ½ψ((s+a)/2) + L'/L(s,χ)`, `|L'/L(s,χ)| ≤ Z(Re s)` for
`Re s > 1`, and for `Re w = x_0 > 0` Binet's formula
`ψ(w) = log w − 1/(2w) − ∫_0^∞ (½ − 1/t + 1/(e^t−1)) e^{−tw} dt` with
`0 ≤ ½ − 1/t + 1/(e^t−1) ≤ t/12` gives `Re ψ(w) ≤ log|w| + 1/(12x_0^2)`. With
`w = (1/2+δ+a+it)/2`, `log|w| = ½log(t^2+4x_0^2) − log 2`. Multiply by `δ`. ∎

For `q = 4`, `δ = 0.9`: `μ_δ(640.17) = 4.52`, so zeros just above `T_4` have multiplicity at
most `4` (for `q = 3`: `4.43` at `T_3`) [cert-A]. (The bound of Bennett–Martin–O'Bryant–
Rechnitzer, `|N(T,χ) − main| ≤ 0.22737ℓ + 2log(1+ℓ) − 0.5`, `ℓ = log(q(T+2)/2π)`, where
`N(T,χ)` counts the zeros with `|γ| ≤ T` so that an ordinate of multiplicity `m_γ` makes it
jump by `2m_γ`, gives `2m_γ ≤ 9.53` at `T_4` and `≤ 9.32` at `T_3`, i.e. `m_γ ≤ 4`,
unconditionally, at the same heights. Lemma 4.2 gives the same integer bound, is
self-contained under GRH, and has the smooth form needed in Proposition 4.4.)

**Lemma 4.3 (a log-weighted zero sum in closed form; GRH + NCZ).** For `A > 0`,

    J_A := Σ_{γ>0} γ^{-2} log(1 + γ^2/A^2) = ∫_A^∞ (Λ'/Λ)(s + 1/2) s^{-2} ds ,

and, with `c = 1/2 + a`,

    J_A ≤ J_A^+ := log(q/2π)/(2A) + ½[log(A+c)/A + log(1+c/A)/c] − ½[1/(cA) − log(1+c/A)/c^2] + Z(A+1/2)/A ,

with `J_A^+ − J_A ≤ 1/(18A^3) + 2Z(A+1/2)/A`.

*Proof.* `∫_A^∞ 2 ds/(s(s^2+γ^2)) = γ^{-2}log(1+γ^2/A^2)`; sum over `γ` (Tonelli) and use
`Σ_{γ>0} 2s/(s^2+γ^2) = (Ξ'/Ξ)(s)`. For the bound: `ψ(x) ≤ log x − 1/(2x)` (and
`≥ log x − 1/(2x) − 1/(12x^2)`) for `x > 0`, `|L'/L(s+1/2)| ≤ Z(A+1/2)` for `s ≥ A`, and the
elementary integrals `∫_A^∞ log(s+c)s^{-2}ds = log(A+c)/A + log(1+c/A)/c`,
`∫_A^∞ ds/(s^2(s+c)) = 1/(cA) − log(1+c/A)/c^2`. ∎

**Proposition 4.4 (certified variance bounds).** Assume GRH(χ) and that every zero with
`0 < γ ≤ T` is simple. Let `A ≥ 2x_0`, `δ > 1/2`, and

    tail_2 := C(χ)/2 − Σ_{γ≤T} γ^{-2},     tail_log := J_A^+ − Σ_{γ≤T} γ^{-2} log(1+γ^2/A^2),
    τ_M := σ_M^2 − Σ_{γ≤T} w_M(γ),          c' := δ((log A)/2 + b_δ) − 1 .

(For `A = 30`, `δ = 0.9`: `c' = 2.1447` for `χ_4` and `2.0152` for `χ_3`.)
Then `V_M ≤ V_M^+ := σ_M^2 + 8M^2 (δ/4) tail_log + c' τ_M`, and
`C_2(χ) ≤ C^+(χ) := C(χ) + 2[(δ/4) tail_log + c' tail_2]`.

*Proof.* `V_M − σ_M^2 = Σ_{γ>T}(m_γ − 1)w_M(γ)` (sum with multiplicity; zeros up to `T`
are simple). By Lemma 4.2 and `log(γ^2+4x_0^2) ≤ log A^2 + log(1+γ^2/A^2)`,
`m_γ − 1 ≤ (δ/4)log(1+γ^2/A^2) + c'`. Use `w_M(γ) ≤ 8M^2/γ^2` in the first term; the sums
over `γ > T` of `γ^{-2}log(1+γ^2/A^2)`, of `γ^{-2}` and of `w_M` are `≤ tail_log`,
`= tail_2`, `= τ_M` by Lemma 4.3 and (Q2). ∎

**Numerical values** (`certified_bounds.py`, Arb, `A = 30`, `δ = 0.9`; output in

So `σ_{1/2}^2 ≤ V_{1/2} ≤ 1.0904 σ_{1/2}^2` for `χ_4` and `≤ 1.1080 σ_{1/2}^2` for `χ_3`, under

**Proposition 4.5 (tier B).** Assume GRH(χ) and that all zeros with `0 < γ ≤ X` are simple,
`X ≥ 2x_0`. Then `V_M ≤ σ_M^2 + 8M^2 E_X`,

    E_X := (δ/4)(J_X^+ + 2 log X · U_X) + (δ b_δ − 1) U_X,
    U_X := [½log(q/π) + ½log((X+c)/2) + Z(X+1/2)]/X ≥ Σ_{γ>X} γ^{-2}.

*Proof.* (We need `δ b_δ ≥ 1`, true for the parameters used.) For `γ > X`: `γ^{-2} ≤ 2/(X^2+γ^2)` and `Σ_{γ>0} 2/(X^2+γ^2) = (Λ'/Λ)(X+1/2)/X ≤ U_X`;
`log(γ^2+4x_0^2) ≤ log X^2 + log(1+γ^2/X^2)`; then Lemmas 4.2, 4.3. ∎

With `X = 10^8/q`: `E_X ≤ 2.83·10^{-6}` (`χ_4`), `2.12·10^{-6}` (`χ_3`), giving
`V_{1/2}(χ_4) ≤ 0.1555736302`, `V_{1/2}(χ_3) ≤ 0.1132342076` [cert-B].

**Remark 4.6 (status of tier B).** Platt (*Numerical computations concerning the GRH*,
Math. Comp. 85 (2016); arXiv:1305.3087) verifies GRH for all primitive characters of modulus
`q ≤ 400 000` to height `max(10^8/q, …)`; Theorem 7.1 there states only GRH. The method
(§3 there) locates sign changes of the completed `L`-function and matches their number
against Turing's count *with multiplicity*, which forces every zero in the range to be
simple. The same inference is made explicitly in Platt–Trudgian arXiv:2610.29018 §2.1
("…lie on the 1/2 line (and are simple)") for a different computation. We therefore state
tier-B constants as conditional on the hypothesis "all zeros of `L(s,χ_q)` up to `10^8/q`
are simple", which we believe is a by-product of Platt's computation but which we have not
seen stated as a theorem; tier A does not depend on it.

**Remark 4.7 (how to close the gap between tiers).** The tier-A excess `0.0141` (for
`χ_4`, `M = 1/2`) is `≈ 4.04 τ` and decays like `log^2 T/T`; certifying the zero list (with
simplicity) to `T = 10^4` would reduce it to `≈ 0.002`. An explicit pair-correlation bound
would do the same without more zeros; not attempted.

## 5. Cantelli: an LI-free lower bound for the bias

**Theorem 5.1 (GRH + NCZ, no LI).** For every `m > −1/2`,

    liminf_{X→∞} (1/log X) ∫_2^X 1{D_m(t) > 0} dt/t  ≥  1/(1 + V_M),
    limsup_{X→∞} (1/log X) ∫_2^X 1{D_m(t) ≤ 0} dt/t  ≤  ν_M((−∞,0]) ≤ V_M/(1 + V_M),

and the same for `R_m`. In particular the lower logarithmic density of `{D_m > 0}` exceeds
`1/2` whenever `V_M < 1`, and tends to `1` as `m → −1/2^+`, at least as fast as
`1 − C_2(χ)(2m+1)^2`.

*Proof.* For `x > 1`, `D_m(x) ≤ 0 ⇔ E_M(x) ≤ 0`. The set `(−∞,0]` is closed, so by Theorem
1.3(b) and the portmanteau theorem `limsup_Y ν_{M,Y}((−∞,0]) ≤ ν_M((−∞,0])`; the passage
between `(1/log X)∫_2^X … dt/t` and `(1/Y)∫_0^Y … dy`, `Y = log X`, costs `O(1/Y)`. `ν_M` has
mean `1` and variance `V_M` (Theorem 4.1), and Cantelli's one-sided inequality
`P(Z − EZ ≤ −t) ≤ Var Z/(Var Z + t^2)` at `t = 1` gives the bound. Ties `D_m = 0` are counted
on the losing side throughout. ∎

The Chebyshev form of this bound, `δ_lower ≥ 1 − V` (resp. `1 − 2V/mean^2`), with the
multiplicity-squared variance, is **Devin, Cor. 5.8** and **Fiorilli, Lemma 2.7** (see §8);
the gain from the one-sided inequality is `1 − V → 1/(1+V)`. What is added here: the
weighted family, the explicit control of `V_M` under GRH alone, and the evaluation.

**Theorem 5.2 (Chebyshev's race, explicit; GRH only).**

(a) Assume GRH(χ_4). The set `{x : π(x;4,3) > π(x;4,1)}` has lower logarithmic density

    ≥ 1/(1 + 0.1696308345) > 0.8549        [cert-A]
    ≥ 0.8653   if moreover all zeros of L(s,χ_4) up to height 2.5·10^7 are simple   [cert-B]
    = lower bound 1/(1+σ_{1/2}^2) = 0.865375…   if all zeros of L(s,χ_4) are simple.

(b) Assume GRH(χ_3). The set `{x : π(x;3,2) > π(x;3,1)}` has lower logarithmic density

**Theorem 5.3 (peeling off an absolutely convergent head; GRH + NCZ).** Let `S ⊂ Γ` be
finite and `0 ≤ s_γ ≤ m_γ c_γ(M)` for `γ ∈ S`, with `B := Σ_{γ∈S} s_γ < 1`. Then

    ν_M((−∞,0]) ≤ V'/(V' + (1−B)^2),     V' := V_M − ½ Σ_{γ∈S} [ (m_γ c_γ)^2 − (m_γ c_γ − s_γ)^2 ].

*Proof.* Write `F_M = 1 + H_S + U`, `H_S := Re Σ_{γ∈S} s_γ u_γ e_γ` with
`u_γ = r_γ/|r_γ|`. Then `|H_S| ≤ B` everywhere, `U` has mean `0` and variance `V'` by
orthogonality, and `{F_M ≤ 0} ⊂ {U ≤ −(1−B)}`. Cantelli. ∎

The optimal choice is soft thresholding `s_γ = (m_γ c_γ − τ)^+`. To first order there is
a gain if and only if the largest amplitude exceeds `2V` (the derivative of the bound in
`s` at `s = 0` is `(2V − m_γc_γ)/(1+V)^2`). At `m = 0` in tier A it gains nothing
(`c_{γ_1}(1/2) = 0.3310 < 2V^+ = 0.3393` for `χ_4`); with simple zeros
`2σ_{1/2}^2 = 0.3111 < 0.3310` and there is a slight gain. It wins near `m = −1/2`
(bracketed values above), and asymptotically it changes the order of magnitude:

**Proposition 5.4 (GRH + NCZ).** There are `c, C > 0` depending on `χ` such that for
`0 < M ≤ 1/2`

    limsup log-density {x : D_m(x) ≤ 0} ≤ ν_M((−∞,0]) ≤ C exp(−c M^{-1/2}).

*Proof.* Take `S = {γ ≤ G}` and `s_γ = m_γ c_γ` in Theorem 5.3. By (Q3),
`B ≤ 4M Σ_{γ≤G} 1/γ ≤ c_1 M log^2(G+3)`, and by Lemma 4.2 and (Q3)
`V' = Σ_{γ∈Γ, γ>G} m_γ^2 w_M(γ) ≤ 8M^2 Σ_{γ>G} μ_δ(γ)/γ^2 ≤ c_2 M^2 log^2(G+3)/G`. Choose
`G = exp((2c_1M)^{-1/2}) − 3` (for `M` small; adjust `C` otherwise): `B ≤ 1/2` and the bound
is `≤ 4V' ≤ (2c_2/c_1) M exp(−(2c_1M)^{-1/2})·(1+o(1))`. ∎

Under LI one has the upper bound `exp(−1/(8C(χ)M^2))` (`weighted-races.tex`, Thm 1(iii));
the true order under LI is expected to be much smaller still, doubly exponential in
`M^{-1/2}` (heuristic: `Σ c_γ(M) = ∞` with `c_γ ≈ 4M/γ`; not proved here). Cantelli alone
gives `O(M^2)`. At the endpoint `m = −1/2` Hayani and Sheth prove density one under GRH.

## 6. Beyond Cantelli without LI: finite non-resonance

LI is an infinite statement, but *the absence of integer relations of bounded order among
finitely many certified zeros* is a finite statement, verifiable (when true) by comparing
enclosures; an exact relation, if one existed, could not be detected this way.
The following theorem converts such a certificate into a density bound.

Setting: GRH + NCZ. Let `γ_1 < … < γ_N` be the `N` smallest ordinates and `T ≥ 3γ_N` a
height such that the list of zeros with `0 < γ ≤ T` is **complete** (this is used in
(NR2), (NR3); it is what part [D] of `certify_arb.py` provides) and all of them are simple.
Put `T' = T − 2γ_N`,
`𝒦 = Γ ∩ (γ_N, T']`, `a_n = c_{γ_n}(M)`,

    V_𝓗 = ½ Σ_{n≤N} a_n^2,     V_𝒦 = ½ Σ_{γ∈𝒦} c_γ(M)^2,     V_t = V_M − V_𝓗 − V_𝒦 .

Non-resonance conditions (`n` denotes an integer vector indexed by `{1..N}`,
`n·γ = Σ n_iγ_i`, `|n|_1 = Σ|n_i|`):

- **(NR1)** `n·γ ≠ 0` for all `n ≠ 0` with `|n|_1 ≤ 4`;
- **(NR2)** for `|n|_1 ≤ 3`: `|n·γ|` is not an ordinate outside `{γ_1..γ_N}`;
- **(NR3)** for `1 ≤ |n|_1 ≤ 2`: `n·γ ≠ ±γ' ± γ''` for all ordinates `γ', γ'' ∈ (γ_N, T]`.

**Theorem 6.1 (finite non-resonance bound; GRH + NCZ, no LI).** Assume (NR1)–(NR3). Then for
all `κ_0 > 0` and `b_1,…,b_N ≥ 0` with `Σ b_n ≤ 1`,

    ν_M((−∞,0]) ≤ J(κ_0,b) := (1−κ_0)^2 + (1−κ_0)κ_0 Σ a_nb_n
        + κ_0^2 [ ½Σ(a_n−b_n)^2 + ¼(Σa_n^2)(Σb_n^2) + ½(Σa_nb_n)^2 − ⅜Σa_n^2b_n^2
                  + (1 + ½Σb_n^2) V_𝒦 + (1 + Σb_n)^2 V_t ],

and therefore the lower logarithmic density of `{D_m > 0}` is `≥ 1 − inf J`. (For `b = 0`,
`inf_{κ_0} J = V_M/(1+V_M)`: Cantelli.)

*Proof.* Let `ϑ_n := arg(r_{γ_n} e_{γ_n}(θ))`, so that
`F_M = 1 + H + W_𝒦 + W_t`, `H = Σ_{n≤N} a_n cos ϑ_n`, `W_𝒦 = Re Σ_{γ∈𝒦} r_γ e_γ`,
`W_t = Re Σ_{γ∈Γ, γ>T'} m_γ r_γ e_γ` (the zeros in `(T',T]` are put into `W_t`),
`‖W_𝒦‖_2^2 = V_𝒦`, `‖W_t‖_2^2 = V_t`. Let `L = Σ b_n cos ϑ_n` and `κ = κ_0(1 − L)`; then
`0 ≤ κ ≤ κ_0(1+Σb_n)`. Since `κ ≥ 0`, `1 − κF_M ≥ 1` wherever `F_M ≤ 0`, so

    ν_M((−∞,0]) = λ_A(F_M ≤ 0) ≤ ∫_A (1 − κF_M)^2 dλ_A .

Write `1 − κF_M = Q − κW_𝒦 − κW_t`, `Q := 1 − κ(1+H)`, and expand:

    ∫(1−κF_M)^2 = ∫Q^2 − 2∫QκW_𝒦 − 2∫QκW_t + ∫κ^2W_𝒦^2 + 2∫κ^2W_𝒦W_t + ∫κ^2W_t^2 .

The integral over `A` of a monomial in the characters `e_γ^{±1}` is `1` if its total
frequency is `0` and `0` otherwise (Lemma 1.2). `Q`, `Qκ`, `κ^2` are trigonometric
polynomials in `e_{γ_1},…,e_{γ_N}` of degrees `2, 3, 2`.

1. `∫Q^2`: degree `4` in the head; by (NR1) only the formally constant monomials
   contribute, so the integral equals its value for independent uniform phases. With
   `G := H − L − LH`, `Q = (1−κ_0) − κ_0G`: `E G = −½Σa_nb_n`,
   `E G^2 = ½Σ(a_n−b_n)^2 + E[L^2H^2]` (the degree-3 term has mean `0`), and
   `E[L^2H^2] = ¼Σa^2Σb^2 + ½(Σab)^2 − ⅜Σa_n^2b_n^2`
   (from `E cos^2 = ½`, `E cos^4 = ⅜`). This gives the first line of `J` and the first four
   terms of the bracket.
2. `∫QκW_𝒦 = 0` and `∫QκW_t = 0`: a head frequency `n·γ`, `|n|_1 ≤ 3`, would have to equal
   `±γ'` for an ordinate `γ'` outside the head; excluded by (NR2).
3. `∫κ^2W_𝒦^2 = (∫κ^2)·V_𝒦 = κ_0^2(1 + ½Σb_n^2)V_𝒦`: a nonconstant head monomial of
   `κ^2` has frequency `n·γ ≠ 0`, `1 ≤ |n|_1 ≤ 2` (NR1), and would need
   `n·γ = ±γ' ± γ''` with `γ', γ'' ∈ 𝒦`; excluded by (NR3). The constant term of `κ^2` is
   `κ_0^2(1+½Σb_n^2)` by (NR1).
4. `∫κ^2W_𝒦W_t = 0`: one needs `n·γ ± γ' ± γ'' = 0` with `|n|_1 ≤ 2`, `γ' ∈ 𝒦`,
   `γ'' > T'` an ordinate outside the head. If `n = 0` formally then `γ' = γ''`, impossible.
   If `γ'' ≤ T` this is excluded by (NR3). If `γ'' > T`: `γ'' ≤ |n·γ| + γ' ≤ 2γ_N + T' = T`,
   a contradiction. (This is the reason for the buffer `T' = T − 2γ_N`.)
5. `∫κ^2W_t^2 ≤ ‖κ‖_∞^2 V_t ≤ κ_0^2(1+Σb_n)^2 V_t`.

Summing gives `J`. The density statement follows as in Theorem 5.1. ∎

(`J` is increasing in `V_t`, so an upper bound for `V_M` — Proposition 4.4 or 4.5 — may be
used in place of the unknown exact value.)

Only items 1–4 use arithmetic information, and each is a finite check against the certified
list: the unknown zeros beyond `T` enter only through the single number `V_t`, in a term where
no orthogonality is used (and they can resonate with nothing that enters items 1–4, by the
size argument in 4 and by `3γ_N ≤ T` in 2; they may resonate with the head at order `≥ 4`,
with `𝒦`, or among themselves, with any multiplicity).

**Computation.** `nonresonance.py` (output `nonresonance_results.txt`) searches for a good

- (NR1)–(NR3) are verified in **exact integer arithmetic** on the stored ordinates

  value over all head sizes `N ≤ 20` and both characters is `7.9412·10^{-7}` (`χ_3`, NR3).
- `J` is evaluated in **Arb** (200 bits, ordinates as balls of radius `10^{-20}`) at an

**Remark 6.3 (limits of the method, and what was tried and failed).**

1. *Degree and positivity of the multiplier.* Theorem 6.1 uses a multiplier `κ` of degree
   one in the head phases. With *all* information (every zero in the head, full LI) a
   quadratic test function `(1−κF)^2` with `κ = κ(head)` can reach
   `E[v/(v + (1+H)^2)]` (`v` the variance not in the head), which tends to the LI value
   `P(1+H_∞ ≤ 0)` as the head grows; so in principle the method converges to the LI
   density as the certified order and height grow — *provided LI is true* (otherwise some
   non-resonance check eventually fails). **Heuristic.** In practice the gain is limited
   by (i) the sup-norm penalty `‖κ‖_∞^2 V_t`, (ii) the constraint `Σ b_n ≤ 1` that keeps
   `κ ≥ 0` (it binds from `N ≈ 16`), and (iii) the variance `V_𝒦` of the zeros that are
   certified but not in the head. A numerical exploration with degree-two multipliers
   `κ = κ_0(1 − L + s(L^2 − E L^2))` (`explore_degree2.py`, [num], non-resonance of order 6
   not verified) gave **no measurable gain** over degree one for `N ≤ 5` (optimal `s = 0`
   in tier A; `< 0.001` in tier B). A positive multiplier of Riesz-product type
   `Π(1 − b_n cos ϑ_n)` would remove (ii) at the price of non-resonance checks of order
   `≈ 2N`; not attempted.
2. *The tail.* In tier A the term `(1+Σb_n)^2 V_t` with `V_t ≈ 0.0177` (of which `0.0141`
   is the multiplicity excess) is what stops the gain at `N = 8`. Extending the certified
   list is the cheapest improvement (Remark 4.7).
3. *Fourth moments / Paley–Zygmund.* Not available: `∫(F_M−1)^4` depends on all relations
   `γ_1+γ_2 = γ_3+γ_4`. The Hausdorff–Young bound (Remark 1.4) is rigorous but gives
   only `‖F_{1/2}−1‖_4 ≲ 1.9` for `χ_4` [num] against the LI value `≈ 0.51`; useless at `m = 0`.
4. *Union bound "head + tail".* `P(F ≤ 0) ≤ P(1+H ≤ t) + P(W ≤ −t)` with Cantelli on the
   tail cannot beat Cantelli at `m = 0` for `T = 640`, whatever is known about the head
   (the two terms sum to `> 0.15`). The quadratic test function of Theorem 6.1 is what
   makes head information usable.
5. *Transfer through other weights.* Using Corollary 2.4 to bound `{E_M ≤ 0}` by an event
   at `M' > M` and Cantelli there is strictly worse than Cantelli at `M` for all small `M`
   (computed: the derivative in `M'` at `M' = M` has the wrong sign whenever
   `σ_M^2/2 > M^2 Σ(M^2+γ^2)^{-2}/Σ(M^2+γ^2)^{-1}`); and no convex test function can gain
   (Remark 3.7).
6. *Smoothed versions.* Mean squares of smoothed races are smaller, but `K h ≤_cx h` goes
   the wrong way for bounding `P(h ≤ 0)`; smoothing helps only through Theorem 5.3 and
   Corollary 2.4.

## 7. LI-free monotonicity of the densities, ties, and weak substitutes for LI

For a set `S` write `\underline{d}(S)`, `\bar d(S)` for its lower and upper logarithmic
densities, and

    δ^>_-(m) = \underline d{D_m > 0},  δ^>_+(m) = \bar d{D_m > 0},  δ^≥_-(m) = \underline d{D_m ≥ 0},  δ^≥_+(m) = \bar d{D_m ≥ 0}.

**Theorem 7.1 (GRH for (a), (b); GRH + NCZ for (c); no LI).** Let `−1/2 < m < m'` with
`λ_χ(M,M') > 0`, where in general
`λ_χ(M,M') = (1+2n_0)/(MM') − Σ_{γ>0} 4/(|M+iγ||M'+iγ|)` as in `monotone-coupling.tex`
(under NCZ, `n_0 = 0`, this is the `λ_χ` of §1.1). Sufficient: `σ_Mσ_{M'} < 2`; in
particular `M' ≤ M_σ(χ)`, the root of `σ_M^2 = 2` (`M_σ(χ_4) = 1.8239`,
`M_σ(χ_3) = 2.1278` [num]).

(a) (Corollary `cor:densities` of `monotone-coupling.tex`.) For `∗ ∈ {−, +}`:
`δ^>_∗(m') ≤ δ^≥_∗(m') ≤ δ^>_∗(m) ≤ δ^≥_∗(m)`. The same holds for natural densities.

(b) *Ties.* Let `I ⊂ (−1/2,∞)` be an interval of weights `m` such that `λ_χ(M,M') > 0` for
all `m < m'` in `I` (`M = m+1/2`, `M' = m'+1/2`; e.g. `I = (−1/2, M_σ(χ) − 1/2]`). Then each of the four functions `δ^>_±, δ^≥_±` is
nonincreasing on `I`, and `δ^>_∗(m) = δ^≥_∗(m)` for all but countably many `m ∈ I`
(`∗ = ±`): outside a countable subset of `I`, counting ties for or against the leader does
not change the upper or the lower density. Moreover the tie sets `{x ≥ x_0 : D_m(x) = 0}`,
`m ∈ I`, are pairwise disjoint beyond a point depending on the pair, so for any finitely
many `m_1,…,m_k ∈ I`, `Σ_j \underline d{D_{m_j} = 0} ≤ 1`; in particular at most countably
many `m ∈ I` have a tie set of positive lower density.

(c) (GRH + NCZ.) Combined with Theorem 5.1: for `m < m'` as above,
`δ^>_-(m) ≥ δ^≥_-(m') ≥ 1/(1+V_{M'})`.

*Proof.* (a) By Theorem A(ii) of `monotone-coupling.tex` (GRH only, general `n_0`; under
NCZ also Corollary 2.4 above), `{x ≥ x_0 : D_{m'}(x) ≥ 0} ⊂ {D_m > 0}`; densities
are monotone under inclusion and ignore bounded sets. (b) Monotonicity is (a). By (a),
for `m < m'` in `I`: `δ^≥_∗(m') ≤ δ^>_∗(m) ≤ δ^≥_∗(m)`, so the monotone functions `δ^>_∗` and
`δ^≥_∗` interlace; hence they have the same one-sided limits at every interior point and
coincide wherever one of them is continuous, i.e. off a countable set. For `m < m'` in `I`
and `x ≥ x_0(m,m')`, `D_{m'}(x) = 0` forces `D_m(x) > 0`, so the tie sets are eventually
disjoint; lower densities are superadditive on disjoint sets. (c) is (a) and Theorem 5.1. ∎

We cannot exclude under GRH alone that `ν_M` has an atom at `0` for some `M`; the theorem
shows that this can affect at most countably many weights in `I`.

**Definition 7.2.** An ordinate `γ_0 ∈ Γ` is *self-sufficient* if `γ_0` is not a rational
linear combination of `Γ ∖ {γ_0}` — a far weaker hypothesis than LI, though equally
unproved. (This is Devin's Definition 5.3: it is formulated on the *set* of distinct
ordinates, so `γ_0` may be a multiple zero. Martin–Ng's Definition 1.3, from which the name
comes, is on the *multiset* of ordinates and forces the zero to be simple.)

**Proposition 7.3 (one self-sufficient ordinate; GRH + NCZ).** Suppose `Γ` contains a
self-sufficient ordinate `γ_0`. Then for every `M > 0`:

(a) `ν_M` is absolutely continuous; hence `δ(m) := ν_M((0,∞))` is the logarithmic density
of `{D_m > 0}`, of `{D_m ≥ 0}`, of `{R_m > 0}`; ties have logarithmic density zero.

(b) `m ↦ δ(m)` is continuous on `(−1/2,∞)`, `0 < δ(m) < 1`, and `δ` is nonincreasing on
every interval `I` as in Theorem 7.1(b).

(c) `δ(m) ≥ 1/(1+V_M)`, and all bounds of §§5–6 hold for `δ(m)` itself (Theorem 6.2:
`δ_4(0) ≥ 0.8842`, `δ_3(0) ≥ 0.9112`).

*Proof.* (a) Self-sufficiency means that every integer relation `Σ_{γ∈Γ} n_γγ = 0` has
`n_{γ_0} = 0`. Hence the annihilator of `A` in the dual of
`𝕋 × A'` (`A'` the closure of the one-parameter group in `𝕋^{Γ∖{γ_0}}`) is trivial, so
`A = 𝕋 × A'` and `λ_A` is the product measure. Thus `F_M = Re(m_{γ_0}r_{γ_0}θ_{γ_0}) + F'(θ')`
with independent summands, the first having an (absolutely continuous) arcsine law; a convolution with an absolutely continuous law is absolutely
continuous. Then `ν_M(∂(0,∞)) = 0` and the portmanteau theorem give the densities.
(b) `M ↦ F_M ∈ L^2(A)` is continuous (dominated convergence in (1.2)), so `M ↦ ν_M` is
weakly continuous, and `ν_M({0}) = 0`; positivity is Theorem 3.6; monotonicity is Theorem
7.1. (c) Theorems 5.1, 6.1. ∎

Devin (Thm 2.2: no atoms, and the density exists) and Bailleul (Thm 1.7) treat the
unweighted race under this type of hypothesis; Devin proves continuity of the limiting
measure, the absolute continuity in (a) is our (easy) addition. Martin–Ng, Theorem 1.5(a),
is a *two-way* statement: three self-sufficient ordinates (in their multiset sense) imply
that the race is weakly inclusive, i.e. that the logarithmic densities exist. Symmetry of `ν_M` about `1` (hence `δ ≥ 1/2`) would follow from
Devin's parity condition (Thm 2.3); the *strict* decrease of `δ` (Theorem B of
`monotone-coupling.tex`) uses the freedom to rotate each phase separately and genuinely
needs LI — under GRH alone only the weak monotonicity above survives.

   Devin 2020, Cor. 5.8: `δ_lower ≥ 1 − Var/m_S^2` when the mean is positive (read, proof
   included; "inspired from [Fio14a, Lem. 2.7]"); Bailleul, arXiv:2007.05763, §3.3 mentions
   the same strategy. For `S = {χ_4}` Devin's corollary reads `δ_lower ≥ 1 − V_{1/2}`, which
   would be `0.844` *if* the zeros are simple. **We found no paper that evaluates such a
   bound for `q = 4` or `q = 3`, and none that controls the multiplicities so as to make
   the number unconditional under GRH.** New here: Cantelli's one-sided form, the certified
   constants of Theorem 5.2, the weight family, the soft-threshold and non-resonance
   improvements (Theorems 5.3, 6.1, 6.2). The inequality itself must be credited to
   Fiorilli and Devin.
3. **Positivity of both densities under GRH alone (m = 0).** Rubinstein–Sarnak 1994,
   Thm 1.2 (lower bound `≫ exp(−exp(cλ))` for the mass outside a ball; not read in the
   original, quoted via Devin Remark 4 and Fiorilli); Devin Thm 2.5(1) (read): the two
   one-sided statements, hence `0 < δ_lower ≤ δ_upper < 1`; Kaczorowski, *A
   contribution to the Shanks–Rényi race problem*, Quart. J. Math. 44 (1993), Thm 1: under
   GRH the sets `{π(x;k,1) ≷ max/min_{a≠1}π(x;k,a)}` have positive lower density (quoted
   from the Martin–Scarfy survey arXiv:1202.3408, §7). Theorem 3.6 extends this to all
   `m > −1/2` with a different, shorter (qualitative) proof.
4. **Limiting distribution as Haar push-forward without LI.** RS94 §2; Devin §4; Bailleul
   (explicit Kronecker–Weyl). Theorem 1.3 claims no novelty.
5. **Weight `p^{-1/2}`, GRH only.** Hayani, arXiv:2512.23302 (abstract and introduction
   read): natural density `1`; Sheth independently. This is the endpoint `m = −1/2`, outside
   our range; Proposition 5.4 is the quantitative approach to it from inside.
6. **Convex order / monotonicity in a weight.** Not found (searches: "convex order",
   "stochastic order", "majorization" with prime races / Rubinstein–Sarnak; and the
   searches recorded in `PRIOR-ART.md`). Theorems 2.1(d), 3.1, 3.4 and Corollary 3.5 appear
   to be new; the underlying Abel identity is elementary and its use for Theorem A is
   credited in `monotone-coupling.tex`.
7. **Finite-order linear independence checks.** Best–Trudgian (arXiv:1209.3843, not read;
   quoted from `advances/ANT-TOOLS.md`) verify "`m`-independence" numerically for zeta
   zeros. We did not find such checks used to *prove* a density bound; Theorem 6.1 seems
   new.
8. **Explicit inputs.** Bennett–Martin–O'Bryant–Rechnitzer, arXiv:2005.02989, Thm 1.1
   (`N(T,χ)`; statement quoted via `ANT-TOOLS.md`); not needed (Lemma 4.2 is
   self-contained under GRH). Platt 2016 (read §3, §7): see Remark 4.6. Platt–Trudgian,
   arXiv:2610.29018, Thm 1 (read): no real zeros `s > 0` for real primitive characters of
   modulus `≤ 10^9` (`≤ 10^10` if `χ` is odd), hence NCZ in that range.

Not checked: Rubinstein–Sarnak's and Kaczorowski's original texts (by the author of this note); Martin–Ng beyond the
statements summarised in `ANT-TOOLS.md`; Kaczorowski–Ramaré.

## 9. Erratum paragraph for the companion paper (`paper/weighted-races.tex`)

The remark after Proposition `prop:unlogged` (item (1)) and §2 of `RESEARCH-CONTEXT.md`
state that under GRH alone `lim (1/Y)∫_0^Y E_m(e^u)^2 du = 1 + ½Σ_γ|r_γ|^2 = 1 + Σ_γ 2a_γ(m)^2
≤ 1 + C(χ)(2m+1)^2`, the sum being over zeros with multiplicity. This is correct only if all
zeros of `L(s,χ)` are simple. Proposed replacement text:

> *Erratum.* In Remark (1) the frequencies in \cite[Thm 1.14]{ANS14} must be the *distinct*
> positive ordinates, and the coefficient attached to an ordinate $\gamma$ of multiplicity
> $m_\gamma$ is $m_\gamma r_\gamma(m)$. Hence, under GRH$(\chi)$ and NCZ$(\chi)$ alone,
> \[
> \lim_{Y\to\infty}\frac1Y\int_0^Y E_m(e^u)^2\,du
> =1+\tfrac12\sum_{\gamma\ \mathrm{distinct}}m_\gamma^2\,|r_\gamma(m)|^2
> =1+V_m,\qquad
> V_m:=\sum_{\gamma\ \mathrm{distinct}}m_\gamma^2\,\frac{8M^2}{M^2+\gamma^2}
> \;\ge\;\sigma_m^2=4M\,\frac{\Lambda'}{\Lambda}(m+1),
> \]
> with equality if and only if all zeros are simple (this is the form of the variance in
> Fiorilli \cite[Lemma 2.5]{Fio14} and Devin \cite[Thm 2.1]{Dev20}). The closed form
> $\sigma_m^2$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:varid} is the variance of the model variable $X_m$
> and of $\nu_m$ under LI (which forces simplicity); without LI it is only a lower bound for
> the variance of $\nu_m$. The bound $1+C(\chi)(2m+1)^2$ must be replaced by
> $1+C_2(\chi)(2m+1)^2$, $C_2(\chi)=\sum_{\gamma\ \mathrm{distinct}}2m_\gamma^2\gamma^{-2}$;
> for $q=4,3$ one has $C_2(\chi_4)\le0.170093$, $C_2(\chi_3)\le0.125624$ under GRH alone
> (certified), so the qualitative conclusion (quadratic collapse of the spread as
> $m\to-\tfrac12$) is unaffected. The existence of $\nu_m$
> (\cite[Cor.~1.3(a)]{ANS14}, Theorem~\ref{thm:interp}(i)) needs no change of convention.
> No statement made under LI changes.

*Where the slip comes from* (Referee 4, who read arXiv:1306.1657v1; not re-read by the
author of this note): Akbary–Ng–Shahabi state their theorems for a **non-decreasing**
sequence `(λ_n)`, and their Theorem 1.14 gives the second moment as `c^2 + ½Σ|r_n|^2`
(eq. (1.22) in the arXiv version; the companion paper cites "(1.24)", so the journal
numbering should be checked). The companion's "literal" application with the ordinates
listed with multiplicity was therefore faithful to the source; but the formula is valid
only after equal frequencies have been merged, which turns `Σ|r_γ|^2` into
`Σ_{distinct} m_γ^2|r_γ|^2`.

*Also to be corrected, in `RESEARCH-CONTEXT.md` (not edited by us):*

1. §2, "LI-free residue": the displayed mean square `1 + Σ_γ 2a_γ(m)^2 ≤ 1 + C(χ)(2m+1)^2`
   needs the same correction (`1 + V_m`, `C_2(χ)`), as does any future GRH-only use of
   `σ_m^2` as "the variance".
2. §2, "The dissolution law (Thm 2) and monotonicity (Thm 4) need only GRH + NCZ for the
   *limiting distribution*": this is wrong as worded. Those theorems concern the model
   variable `X_m`; `X_m` has the law `ν_m` of the limiting distribution only under LI.
   Correct wording: "need only GRH + NCZ for the model variable `X_m` (GRH + NCZ make its
   variance the closed form); LI is what identifies the law of `X_m` with the limiting
   distribution of the race, and hence with a logarithmic density."

Both points were raised by Referee 4 (item 9).

## 11. What failed, what is open

- **Atoms.** Under GRH alone we cannot exclude `ν_M({0}) > 0`, so existence of the density
  is not proved (it is open even at `m = 0`: Fiorilli, Remark 1.3). Theorem 7.1(b) is the
  best we have; Proposition 7.3 needs one self-sufficient zero.
- **Strict monotonicity of the densities** without LI: not obtained (only weak, and only on
  the Theorem-A range `λ_χ > 0`). Convex order does not imply monotonicity of
  `ν_M((0,∞))`.
- **The gap to the truth.** GRH-only: `0.8842` (tier A) / `0.9227` (tier B) at `q = 4`,
  `m = 0`; GRH + LI: `0.9959`. Higher-degree multipliers (Remark 6.3.1) and longer certified
  zero lists would narrow it; neither was done.
- **Tier B** rests on reading Platt's computation as a proof of simplicity (Remark 4.6).
- **NCZ-free versions** are only sketched (Remark 1.5).
- **Natural densities.** Cantelli-type bounds are for logarithmic density only. Whether
  the natural density of `{D_m > 0}` fails to exist for every `m > −1/2` under GRH
  (Kaczorowski at `m = 0`) was not examined.
- **Quantitative Theorem 3.6.** The lower bound for `ν_M((−∞,0))` is explicit only for
  `M > γ_1/√3` (`m > 2.98` for `q = 4`); for smaller `M` the proof of Proposition 3.2 is not
  quantitative (RS94's method would give a doubly exponentially small bound).
