# The Nahm sum of C(E7)⁻¹: an explicit formula and proof of modularity (the pair (T1,E7)); an infinite family (§6)

Status: **complete proof** (written 2026-11-26). Ingredients: the Durfee rectangle identity (Lemma 1 of
`E8-E6.md`), the fusion lemma (Lemma 2 there; known, see the prior-art paragraph there), Bailey's lemma with the
unit Bailey pair, Cauchy's identity and the q-Chu–Vandermonde sum. Exact certificate and numerical checks:
`code/certify_E7.py`, log `certificates/certify_E7.txt`.

Source of the problem. K. Sun and H. Wang, arXiv:2604.00847, Conjecture 1.1 (Dynkin-pair modularity conjecture;
for ADET pairs the "folklore" conjecture, = Conjecture 1.1 of Wang–Wang arXiv:2607.08606) predicts that for the
pair (T1,E7) the Nahm sum of C(E7)⁻¹ with B = 0, C = −c/24, c = 7·3/(3+18) = 1, is modular. No formula is given:
Table 1 of Sun–Wang lists (T1,E7) with CFT "?" (the only (T1,E_n) without an identification; (T1,E6), (T1,E8)
are their (3.19), (3.20)). The same series is the E7 member of the "♭-case" of I. Cherednik and B. Feigin, *Rogers–Ramanujan type
identities and Nil-DAHA*, Adv. Math. 248 (2013), §3.6 (matrix ((ω_i,ω_j)) = C(R)⁻¹; their Table 1 gives L♭ = 6 for E7, where L♭ = (6/π²)Σ_i L(Q_i) is the dilogarithm sum of
the Q-system; the effective central charge of the q-series is c = rank − L♭ = 7 − 6 = 1, in agreement with the
value above — their own "c♭_eff" = L♭/h = 6/18 = 1/3 is a differently normalised quantity; likewise E6: 6 − 24/5 = 6/5,
E8: 8 − 80/11 = 8/11), who state that their method "cannot be directly extended to the
♭-case" and give no formula. The formula below was found numerically in this project.

Labelling: E7 = chain 1 — 2 — 3 — 4 — 5 — 6 with node 7 attached to node 4. Notation as in `E8-E6.md`.

**Theorem 4.**

    Σ_{n∈N⁷} q^{½ nᵀ C(E7)⁻¹ n} / ∏_{i=1}^{7} (q)_{n_i}
        = (1/(q;q)_∞) ( Σ_{k≥0} q^{3k²/4} + Σ_{k≥1} q^{7k²/4} )
        = (1/(2(q;q)_∞)) Σ_{k∈Z} ( q^{3k²/4} + q^{7k²/4} ).

Consequently q^{−1/24} times the left side equals (ϑ(3τ/2) + ϑ(7τ/2))/(2η(τ)) with ϑ(τ) = Σ_k e^{πik²τ}, a modular
function of weight 0 on a congruence subgroup (ϑ(τ) is a modular form of weight 1/2 on the theta group, so
ϑ(3τ/2), ϑ(7τ/2) are modular forms of weight 1/2 on congruence subgroups, with multiplier systems; dividing by
η, of weight 1/2, gives weight 0; some power of the quotient is invariant under a principal congruence
subgroup — the standard sense of "modular" for Nahm sums): Conjecture 1.1 of Sun–Wang holds for (T1,E7). Separating the
exponents in Z and in 3/4 + Z gives the two refinements (n_1+n_3+n_7 even, resp. odd):

    even part = (1/(2(q)_∞)) Σ_{k∈Z} (q^{3k²} + q^{7k²}),      odd part = (1/(2(q)_∞)) Σ_{k∈Z} (q^{3(k+1/2)²} + q^{7(k+1/2)²}).

(The exponent ½nᵀC⁻¹n is ≡ ¾(n_1+n_3+n_7)² mod 1: the diagonal entries of C⁻¹ are 3/2, 4, 15/2, 12, 6, 2, 7/2 and the
off-diagonal ones are half-integers exactly between two of the nodes 1, 3, 7.) Remarkably the right side is
½[(A1,D_3) + (A1,D_7)] in terms of the Kedem–Klassen–McCoy–Melzer sums Σ_{n∈N^r} q^{nᵀC(D_r)⁻¹n}/∏(q)_{n_i} = (q)_∞⁻¹Σ_k q^{rk²/4}.

## 2. Evaluation of T(a)

**Lemma 3 (Bailey).** Let x be a parameter with x ≠ q^{−m} (m ≥ 1) and put, for r ≥ 0,
α_r = (−1)^r q^{r(r−1)/2} (1−xq^{2r})(x;q)_r / ((1−x)(q)_r) (α_0 = 1; for x = 1 read (x)_r/(1−x) as (q)_{r−1}, i.e.
α_r = (−1)^r q^{r(r−1)/2}(1+q^r) for r ≥ 1). Then for every integer k ≥ 1 and all N ≥ 0

    Σ_{N≥N_2≥…≥N_k≥0} x^{N_2+…+N_k} q^{N_2²+…+N_k²} / ((q)_{N−N_2}(q)_{N_2−N_3}⋯(q)_{N_{k−1}−N_k}(q)_{N_k})
        = Σ_{r=0}^{N} x^{kr} q^{kr²} α_r / ((q)_{N−r} (xq;q)_{N+r})                                                  (2.1_k)

(for k = 1 the left side is 1/(q)_N). The case k = 3, with (N_2,N_3) = (r,s), is

    Σ_{N≥r≥s≥0} x^{r+s} q^{r²+s²} / ((q)_{N−r}(q)_{r−s}(q)_s) = Σ_{r=0}^{N} x^{3r} q^{3r²} α_r / ((q)_{N−r} (xq;q)_{N+r}).          (2.1)

*Proof.* A pair of sequences (α_n, β_n) is a Bailey pair relative to x if β_n = Σ_{r=0}^n α_r/((q)_{n−r}(xq)_{n+r}).
(i) The unit Bailey pair is α_r as above, β_n = δ_{n,0}. (ii) Bailey's lemma in the limit ρ_1,ρ_2 → ∞: if (α,β)
is a Bailey pair relative to x then so is α'_r = x^r q^{r²}α_r, β'_n = Σ_{r=0}^n x^r q^{r²}β_r/(q)_{n−r}. Both are classical:
W. N. Bailey's lemma in the form of G. E. Andrews, *Multiple series Rogers–Ramanujan type identities*, Pacific
J. Math. 114 (1984) 267–283, Lemma (2.3)–(2.6)/Theorem 1 (2.7) (the finite statement; (3.1) there is its N → ∞
limit), unit pair (2.12)–(2.13); see also S. O. Warnaar, *50 years of Bailey's lemma*, arXiv:0910.2062, §§1–2
(the referee compared the statements used here with both sources and verified (2.1) exactly for N ≤ 6).
Define (α⁽ᵏ⁾,β⁽ᵏ⁾) by applying (ii) k times to (i). By induction on k: α⁽ᵏ⁾_r = x^{kr}q^{kr²}α_r; β⁽¹⁾_n = Σ_r x^rq^{r²}δ_{r,0}/(q)_{n−r} =
1/(q)_n; and if β⁽ᵏ⁾_n equals the left side of (2.1_k) with N = n, then β⁽ᵏ⁺¹⁾_N = Σ_{N_2≤N} x^{N_2}q^{N_2²}β⁽ᵏ⁾_{N_2}/(q)_{N−N_2} is the left side of
(2.1_{k+1}) (after renaming the summation variables of β⁽ᵏ⁾ as N_3,…,N_{k+1}). (2.1_k) is the statement that
(α⁽ᵏ⁾,β⁽ᵏ⁾) is a Bailey pair relative to x. ∎ [Both sides of (2.1_k) are rational functions of x and q; (2.1) is
checked numerically for x = q^a through check (c) of the certificate.]

**Lemma 4.** For integers a, R ≥ 0:

    Σ_{m≥0} q^{m²+Rm} / ((q)_m (q)_{m+R+a}) = (1/(q)_∞) Σ_{j=0}^{a} [a;j]_q q^{j²+Rj},     [a;j]_q = (q)_a/((q)_j(q)_{a−j}).        (2.2)

*Proof.* Cauchy's identity Σ_{m≥0} q^{m(m+c)}/((q)_m(q)_{m+c}) = 1/(q)_∞ holds for every integer c ≥ 0 (cf. Andrews, *The
Theory of Partitions*, (2.2.8)). Self-contained derivation: Lemma 1 with (a,b) → (A, A+c) and summation index
m = A − (its c) reads 1/((q)_A(q)_{A+c}) = Σ_{m=0}^{A} q^{m(m+c)}/((q)_m(q)_{m+c}(q)_{A−m}); letting A → ∞ coefficientwise in Z[[q]]
gives 1/(q)_∞² = (q)_∞⁻¹ Σ_m q^{m(m+c)}/((q)_m(q)_{m+c}). Use it with c = R+a+j for each j and substitute
m → m − j:

    (1/(q)_∞) Σ_j [a;j] q^{j²+Rj} = Σ_j [a;j] q^{j²+Rj} Σ_{m≥j} q^{(m−j)(m+R+a)}/((q)_{m−j}(q)_{m+R+a})
        = Σ_{m≥0} (q^{m²+Rm}/(q)_{m+R+a}) Σ_{j=0}^{min(a,m)} [a;j] q^{(m−j)(a−j)}/(q)_{m−j},

because j²+Rj+(m−j)(m+R+a) = m²+Rm+(m−j)(a−j). By Lemma 1, Σ_j q^{(m−j)(a−j)}/((q)_j(q)_{a−j}(q)_{m−j}) = 1/((q)_a(q)_m), so the
inner sum is 1/(q)_m. ∎

**Proposition 5.** For every integer a ≥ 0,

    T(a) = (1/(q)_∞) Σ_{r≥0} (−1)^r q^{3ar+(9r²−r)/2} · ((1−q^{a+2r})(q)_{a+r−1}/(q)_r) · Σ_{j=0}^{a} [a;j]_q q^{j²+2rj},              (2.3)

where for a = r = 0 the factor (1−q^{a+2r})(q)_{a+r−1}/(q)_r is read as 1.

*Proof.* With x = q^a, N = N_1, r = N_2, s = N_3, the left side of (2.1) multiplied by q^{N²} and summed over N is
T(a). Hence, interchanging the (formal, summable) sums and writing N = m + r,

    T(a) = Σ_{r≥0} q^{3ar+3r²} α_r Σ_{m≥0} q^{(m+r)²}/((q)_m (q^{a+1};q)_{m+2r}).

Since (q^{a+1})_{m+2r} = (q)_{m+2r+a}/(q)_a, Lemma 4 with R = 2r gives for the inner sum
q^{r²}(q)_a(q)_∞⁻¹ Σ_j [a;j]q^{j²+2rj}. Finally α_r(q)_a = (−1)^r q^{r(r−1)/2}(1−q^{a+2r})(q)_{a+r−1}/(q)_r (as (q^a)_r/(1−q^a) =
(q)_{a+r−1}/(q)_a for a ≥ 1, and the convention above for a = 0), and 3r²+r²+r(r−1)/2 = (9r²−r)/2. ∎
[Check (c): (2.3) for a ≤ 5 as power series mod q^{120}.]

## 3. The finite identity

Insert (2.3) in (1.1). The triple series over (a,r,j) is summable (exponent ≥ ¾(a+2r)²), so we may collect the
terms with n := a + 2r fixed. Since 3a²/4 + 3ar + (9r²−r)/2 = ¾n² + (3r²−r)/2 and [a;j]/(q)_a = 1/((q)_j(q)_{a−j}),

    (q)_∞ F(q) = Σ_{n≥0} q^{3n²/4} c_n,   c_0 = 1,
    c_n = (1−q^n) Σ_{r,j≥0, 2r+j≤n} (−1)^r q^{(3r²−r)/2 + j² + 2rj} (q)_{n−r−1} / ((q)_r (q)_j (q)_{n−2r−j})    (n ≥ 1).          (3.1)

**Proposition 6.** c_n = 1 + q^{n²} for all n ≥ 1.

*Proof.* Put u = r + j; then (3r²−r)/2 + j² + 2rj = u² + r(r−1)/2 and

    c_n/(1−q^n) = Σ_{u=0}^{n} q^{u²} S_u,    S_u := Σ_{r=0}^{min(u,n−u)} (−1)^r q^{r(r−1)/2} (q)_{n−r−1} / ((q)_r (q)_{u−r} (q)_{n−u−r}).

For u = 0: S_0 = (q)_{n−1}/(q)_n = 1/(1−q^n). For u = n: only r = 0 occurs and S_n = (q)_{n−1}/(q)_n = 1/(1−q^n). For
1 ≤ u ≤ n−1 use (q)_{n−r−1} = (q)_{n−1}/(q^{n−r};q)_r, (q^{n−r};q)_r = (−1)^r q^{nr−r(r+1)/2}(q^{1−n};q)_r, 1/(q)_{u−r} =
(−1)^r q^{ur−r(r−1)/2}(q^{−u};q)_r/(q)_u and 1/(q)_{n−u−r} = (−1)^r q^{(n−u)r−r(r−1)/2}(q^{u−n};q)_r/(q)_{n−u}; the powers of q
combine to q^r and the signs to +1:

    S_u = ((q)_{n−1}/((q)_u(q)_{n−u})) Σ_{r≥0} (q^{−u};q)_r (q^{u−n};q)_r q^r / ((q)_r (q^{1−n};q)_r)
        = ((q)_{n−1}/((q)_u(q)_{n−u})) · ₂φ₁(q^{−u}, q^{u−n}; q^{1−n}; q, q).

(The denominators (q^{1−n};q)_r, r ≤ u ≤ n−1, do not vanish.) By the q-Chu–Vandermonde sum
₂φ₁(q^{−u}, b; c; q, q) = (c/b;q)_u b^u/(c;q)_u (Gasper–Rahman, *Basic Hypergeometric Series*, (1.5.3); an identity of
rational functions of b, c, valid whenever (c;q)_u ≠ 0) with b = q^{u−n}, c = q^{1−n}: c/b = q^{1−u} and
(q^{1−u};q)_u = 0 for u ≥ 1. Hence S_u = 0 for 1 ≤ u ≤ n−1 and c_n = (1−q^n)(1 + q^{n²})/(1−q^n). ∎
[Check (b): (3.1) = 1 + q^{n²} exactly, as polynomials, for 1 ≤ n ≤ 30.]

**Proof of Theorem 4.** (q)_∞F(q) = Σ_{n≥0} q^{3n²/4}c_n = 1 + Σ_{n≥1}(q^{3n²/4} + q^{3n²/4+n²}) = Σ_{k≥0}q^{3k²/4} + Σ_{k≥1}q^{7k²/4}. ∎

## 6. The same proof gives an infinite family

**Theorem 5.** For every integer k ≥ 1, with N_j = n_j + … + n_k,

    Σ_{a,n_1,…,n_k≥0} q^{(k/4)a² + a(N_2+…+N_k) + N_1²+…+N_k²} / ((q)_a (q)_{n_1}⋯(q)_{n_k})
        = (1/(q)_∞) ( Σ_{m≥0} q^{km²/4} + Σ_{m≥1} q^{(k+4)m²/4} ) = (1/(2(q)_∞)) Σ_{m∈Z} ( q^{km²/4} + q^{(k+4)m²/4} ).

The left side is the Nahm sum of the rank k+1 matrix with A_aa = k/2, A_{a,n_i} = i−1, A_{n_i,n_j} = 2min(i,j).
k = 3 is (1.1), i.e. Theorem 4 after fusion. k = 1 reads
(Σ_a q^{a²/4}/(q)_a)·(Σ_n q^{n²}/(q)_n) = (q)_∞⁻¹(Σ_{m≥0}q^{m²/4} + Σ_{m≥1}q^{5m²/4}) (T_1(a) does not depend on a).

**The case k = 2 is a known identity (new proof only).** The k = 2 matrix (1 0 1; 0 2 2; 1 2 4) is the terminal
form of C(T_4)⁻¹ under defusion, so the left side is the (T1,T_4) sum Σ q^{½(M_1²+M_2²+M_3²+M_4²)}/∏(q)_{m_i}. This is the
case of four summation variables and no linear term of Melzer's conjecture (E. Melzer, *Supersymmetric analogs of the Gordon–Andrews identities,
and related TBA systems*, hep-th/9412154, eq. (2.6)), proved by Bressoud–Ismail–Stanton, *Change of base in
Bailey pairs*, Ramanujan J. 4 (2000), Theorem 5.1 (i = k, a = 1), and by Warnaar, *The generalized Borwein
conjecture II*, Discrete Math. 272 (2003), Theorem 4.1 of arXiv:math/0110307 (= Theorem 4.4 in the numbering
quoted by Sun–Wang): Σ_{m_1,…,m_{2k−2}} q^{½(M_1²+…+M_{2k−2}²)}/∏(q)_{m_i} = ∏_{j≥1, j≢2 (4), j≢0,±(2k−1) (4k)} (1−q^{j/2})⁻¹. For 2k−2 = 4 the
product equals our theta form (checked mod q^{200}). For k ≥ 3 the Melzer sums are different from Theorem 5: the
terminal form of C(T_{2k})⁻¹ has A_aa = 1 and a coupled to N_k only (`code/fusion.py`), whereas here A_aa = k/2 and a
is coupled to N_2+…+N_k; the two families meet only at k = 2. What I read in these three sources (full text) and in
Berkovich–McCoy–Schilling (hep-th/9412030, q-alg/9607020; grep only): half-weight chains Σ q^{½ΣM_j²+linear}
and their Göllnitz–Gordon/Andrews–Gordon forms; none contains the family of Theorem 5 for k ≠ 2 or the rank-4
sum (1.1). Agarwal–Andrews–Bressoud (Bailey lattice, 1987) and Warnaar's A_2 Bailey papers were not accessible
/ not checked; an anticipation of Theorem 5 in the Bailey-lattice literature cannot be excluded.

*Proof of Theorem 5.* By Lemma 3 with x = q^a, multiplying (2.1_k) by q^{N²} and summing over N = N_1 (for k = 1
the left side is Σ_N q^{N²}/(q)_N), T_k(a) := Σ q^{N_1²+…+N_k²+a(N_2+…+N_k)}/∏(q)_{n_i} = Σ_r q^{kar+kr²}α_r Σ_m q^{(m+r)²}/((q)_m(q^{a+1})_{m+2r}), and by
Lemma 4 (exactly as in the proof of Proposition 5),
T_k(a) = (q)_∞⁻¹ Σ_r (−1)^r q^{kar+(k+1)r²+r(r−1)/2}((1−q^{a+2r})(q)_{a+r−1}/(q)_r) Σ_j [a;j]q^{j²+2rj}. With n = a+2r, u = r+j:
(k/4)a² + kar + (k+1)r² + r(r−1)/2 + j² + 2rj = (k/4)n² + r(r−1)/2 + u², so (q)_∞·(left side) = Σ_n q^{kn²/4}c_n with the
same c_n as in (3.1) (the rearrangement is legitimate as in §3: exponent ≥ (k/4)n²), and Proposition 6
applies. ∎ (The choice of the coefficient k/4 of a² is exactly what
makes the cross term in n·r cancel.) Numerical check for k = 1,…,6 mod q^{80}: `certificates/test_family_k.txt`.
